## I. Psychosis: what it is and where it comes from

> [!quote] Anchor
> Psychosis is a clinical manifestation, not a diagnosis. Always assess for the underlying cause.

#### What is psychosis?
- Some degree of ==loss of contact with reality==: disturbances in thought, perception, or interpretation of experiences.
- Causes: **schizophrenia spectrum**, **mood disorders**, **substances/withdrawal**, **medications**, **neurological or metabolic** conditions, **infections**, **trauma**.

#### Early warning signs?
- ==Social withdrawal== (isolation is often the first sign `[Engage]`), sleep changes, declining function, poor concentration, mood changes, declining self-care.
- ==Early recognition and treatment → better outcomes.==

#### Hallucination vs. delusion?
| | Hallucination | Delusion |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | ==Sensory perception without an external stimulus== | ==Fixed false belief== that persists despite evidence |
| Domain | Senses (hear, see, smell, feel, taste) | Beliefs |
| In schizophrenia | **Auditory** most common | Persecutory most common type `[ADDED]` |

> [!danger] Report immediately
> ==New **visual** hallucinations, especially in an older adult==, need careful assessment for a **medical cause**. Psychosis from medical conditions is more common in older adults `[Engage]`.

## II. Schizophrenia spectrum and other psychotic disorders

#### Which disorders are on the spectrum?
- **Spectrum:** schizophrenia · schizoaffective disorder · schizophreniform disorder · schizotypal personality disorder.
- **Other psychotic disorders:** brief psychotic disorder · delusional disorder · substance/medication-induced psychosis · psychosis due to another medical condition (also other specified / unspecified `[Engage]`).

#### Schizophrenia core criteria?
- ==**≥ 2** core symptoms== for a significant portion of a **1-month** period; ==at least one must be **delusions, hallucinations, or disorganized speech**==.
- Others: grossly disorganized or catatonic behavior; negative symptoms.
- ==Significant **functional impairment**== (work, relationships, self-care).
- **Continuous disturbance ≥ 6 months** (can include prodromal or residual symptoms).

> [!example] Mnemonic — 2–1–6
> **2** or more core symptoms · **1** month of active symptoms · **6** months of continuous disturbance overall · functional decline required.

#### Functional impact?
- Work/school decline; relationships deteriorate; **self-care impaired**; housing, finances, employment, health, quality of life suffer.
- Negative symptoms have the ==highest impact on functioning== `[Engage]`.
- ==The diagnosis alone does not predict violence.==

## III. Etiology and risk factors

#### What causes schizophrenia?
- ==No single cause==: interaction of **genetic, physiological, biochemical, environmental** factors.
- **Diathesis** = inherited vulnerability. Genes raise vulnerability but aren't destiny. **Diathesis-stress model:** vulnerability + stress → illness `[Engage]`.
- Close relative with schizophrenia → **6×** risk; twin heritability **60–80%** `[Engage]`.

#### Brain, dopamine, stress?
| Pathway | Linked to |
|---|---|
| ==**Mesolimbic** dopamine== activity | ==**Positive** symptoms== |
| ==**Mesocortical** dopamine== activity | ==**Negative and cognitive** symptoms== |

- Brain development, pregnancy/birth complications (hypoxia, maternal infection, starvation, iron deficiency; older paternal age `[Engage]`).
- **Chronic stress** alters the stress-response system (HPA axis → cortisol → ↑ D2 activity; hippocampal volume loss `[Engage]`).
- Autoimmune links `[Engage]`: celiac, MS, lupus, **Graves'**, psoriasis, Guillain-Barré, hepatitis.

#### Cannabis and psychosis?
- Cannabis ==does not cause schizophrenia in everyone==. **THC may bring earlier onset** in people already vulnerable.
- Risk ↑ with ==heavier/frequent use, younger age of use, high-potency products==.

#### Social and environmental risks? `[Engage]`
- Poverty, dense urban living, trauma (child abuse, bullying, loss), migrant status, social disadvantage of underrepresented groups.

#### Comorbidities and health risks?
- **Metabolic syndrome, diabetes, HTN, CVD, liver disease**; ~**28.5 years** of potential life lost `[Engage]` (often from under-treated medical illness).
- **Substance use** (nicotine `[Engage]`), depression, OCD, anxiety.
- ==Avoid **diagnostic overshadowing**== (blaming physical symptoms on the mental illness → missed medical illness `[ADDED]`): take physical complaints seriously.
- ==**Suicide risk requires ongoing assessment**==; highest early in the illness; **4.9%** die by suicide `[Engage]`.

## IV. Clinical presentation

#### Three symptom domains?
| Domain | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive** | ==Added== experiences/behaviors | Hallucinations, delusions, disorganized speech/behavior |
| **Negative** | ==Diminished or lost== functions | ↓ motivation, speech, pleasure, emotional expression, social engagement |
| **Cognitive** | Thinking problems | Attention, memory, planning, organization, executive function |

#### Negative symptom terms?
| Term | Meaning | Example `[Engage]` |
|---|---|---|
| **Apathy** | Reduced interest | Stops going to work or school |
| **Anhedonia** | ==Can't experience pleasure== | No pleasure when a loved one visits |
| **Alogia** | ==Poverty of speech== | Short, minimal answers |
| **Flat/blunted affect** | Reduced emotional expression | Doesn't smile at a funny show |
| **Avolition** | ==Lack of motivation/goal-directed activity== | Can't start making the bed |
| **Asociality/self-neglect** | ↓ social engagement and personal care | Unbathed, dirty clothes |

#### Types of delusions?
| Type | Belief |
|---|---|
| ==**Persecutory**== | Someone is trying to harm or monitor them |
| **Grandiose** | Exaggerated power, identity, fame, ability (royalty, deity `[Engage]`) |
| **Referential** | Events or messages are specifically about them |
| **Control** | Outside forces control thoughts, actions, body |
| **Thought insertion / withdrawal / broadcasting** | Thoughts put in, taken out, or heard by others `[ADDED for broadcasting]` |

#### Disorganized speech patterns?
| Pattern | Definition | Example `[Engage]` |
|---|---|---|
| ==**Tangentiality**== | ==Never returns== to the original point | "My mother loves to cook. The road is outside my house. Have you been to Mars?" |
| ==**Circumstantiality**== | Excessive detail, ==eventually returns== | Wanders through hearts and Valentine's Day, then "Mom, I'm home!" |
| **Loose associations** | Weak connections between ideas | Dakota (dog) → North Dakota → travel insurance |
| **Word salad** | Severely incoherent | "Trip it down flight canoes doggy…" |
| **Clang associations** | Words chosen for sound/rhyme | "Biggy, diggy, piggy…" |
| **Neologisms** | Invented words | "The banterwaggle is clagified." |
| **Echolalia** | Repeats words/sounds heard | Echoes the TV |
| **Pressured speech** `[Engage]` | Fast, increasing, hard to interrupt | "LET ME GO RIGHT NOW!" |

#### Other clinical features?
- **Thought blocking:** sudden stop in the train of thought. **Poverty of speech:** ↓ spontaneous speech.
- **Catatonia:** extreme stillness, rigidity, ↓ responsiveness, ==or== excessive purposeless movement. Immobile clients: watch intake, skin, VTE `[ADDED]`.
- ==**Anosognosia**== = lack of awareness of having an illness (frontal lobe `[Engage]`); the inaccurate self-view **feels accurate** → treatment refusal.
- Also `[Engage]`: inappropriate affect (laughing without humor), disinhibited behavior, depersonalization/derealization, anxiety, sleep disturbance.

#### Phases of schizophrenia?
| Phase | Features | Nursing focus |
|---|---|---|
| **Prodromal** | Withdrawal, sleep disruption, anxiety, ↓ functioning, ↑ suspicion, subtle changes; negative symptoms emerge | ==Early detection==, referral |
| **Active** | ==Prominent hallucinations, delusions, disorganization==; mood symptoms may occur | Safety, symptom stabilization; may need hospitalization (sometimes involuntary `[Engage]`) |
| **Residual** | Psychosis less intense; ==negative/cognitive symptoms may remain== | Support, coping, relapse prevention |

- ==Interventions should match the client's current phase.== Prodrome may start years before diagnosis; "at-risk mental state" `[Engage]`.

## V. Differentiating psychotic disorders

#### Three questions to ask?

> [!tip] NCLEX tip
> 1. ==How long== have symptoms lasted?
> 2. Are ==major mood episodes== involved?
> 3. Is there an identifiable ==medical, medication, or substance== cause?

#### Duration-based timeline?
| Disorder | Duration |
|---|---|
| **Brief psychotic disorder** | ==**≥ 1 day** but **< 1 month**==; sudden onset, often after extreme stress; returns to baseline `[Engage]` |
| **Schizophreniform** | ==**1 month** to **< 6 months**== |
| **Schizophrenia** | ==**≥ 6 months**== |

#### Schizoaffective vs. mood disorder with psychotic features?
| | Schizoaffective | Mood disorder with psychotic features |
|---|---|---|
| Psychosis timing | With a major mood episode ==AND for **≥ 2 weeks without** one== | ==**Only during** the mood episode== (mania or severe depression) |
| Key question | ==Does psychosis occur when mood symptoms are absent?== | |
| Other | Negative symptoms and anosognosia less severe than in schizophrenia `[Engage]` | MDD or bipolar |

#### Other causes of psychosis?
| Cause | Distinguishing feature |
|---|---|
| **Schizotypal personality disorder** | Long-standing odd beliefs/behavior; ==reality testing generally preserved== |
| **Delusional disorder** | Delusions **≥ 1 mo**; ==function preserved outside the delusion==. Types: erotomanic, grandiose, jealous, persecutory, somatic, mixed `[Engage]` |
| **Substance/medication-induced** | With use, shortly after use, or **withdrawal**: alcohol, stimulants, sedatives/hypnotics, hallucinogens, inhalants; ==OTC decongestants (phenylephrine, pseudoephedrine)== `[Engage]` |
| **Medical** | Neurological, endocrine, metabolic, autoimmune, hepatic, renal; electrolyte imbalance; **temporal lobe epilepsy → olfactory hallucinations** `[Engage]` |
| **Late-onset schizophrenia** `[Engage]` | Onset **≥ 40**; personality and affect usually preserved |
| **Shared psychotic disorder** `[Engage]` | Psychosis develops in someone close to a person with psychosis. [NOTE: current practice: DSM-5 no longer lists it as a separate diagnosis.] |

- Other conditions with psychotic-like features `[Engage]`: PTSD (flashbacks), autism (negative-like symptoms without prominent hallucinations/delusions), OCD/body dysmorphic disorder (preoccupations).

#### Psychosis vs. delirium vs. dementia?
| | Delirium | Dementia | Primary psychosis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Onset | ==**Acute**== | ==**Gradual, progressive**== | Often gradual; late teens–20s |
| Core problem | ==Attention/awareness, **fluctuating**== | Cognitive decline | Perception and thought content |
| Consciousness | Altered | Clear until late `[ADDED]` | Usually clear `[ADDED]` |
| Urgency | ==**Medical emergency**== | Chronic | Psychiatric |

- Both delirium and dementia can include hallucinations or paranoia. ==Don't assume new psychosis is a primary psychiatric disorder.==

## VI. Diagnosis and assessment

#### How is schizophrenia diagnosed?
- ==Clinically. **No** blood test, scan, or biomarker== confirms it.
- History, observation, client/family information (with consent `[Engage]`), **mental status exam**.
- Testing mainly ==rules out other causes==: blood/urine for **substances**, infection, electrolytes, **thyroid**; CT/MRI/PET/EEG when indicated.

#### What's in the mental status exam?
- Appearance and behavior · speech, mood, affect · thought **process** and thought **content** · perceptions · cognition · ==insight, judgment, safety (suicidal/homicidal thoughts)==.

#### Abnormal movements and AIMS?
- Key findings: **catatonia, parkinsonism, tardive dyskinesia**. These can occur from the disorder itself, not only from meds `[Engage]`.
- ==**AIMS** (Abnormal Involuntary Movement Scale)== assesses abnormal movements, especially **TD**. ==Do it at **baseline** and at **regular intervals**== during antipsychotic treatment (often q**6 mo** `[ADDED — verify vs course materials]`).

#### Holistic assessment? `[Engage]`
- Cultural, spiritual, social: "What cultural or religious beliefs should the care team know?"
- **Housing/safety:** "Where do you live? Do you feel safe?" **Food:** "Do you have access to healthy foods?"
- Education/employment: shapes how you teach (plain language).
- Interdisciplinary team: nursing, psychiatric/medical providers, social work, case management, dietitian, community services.

## VII. Nursing care

#### Core nursing priorities?
1. ==Build a **safe, therapeutic relationship**.==
2. Maintain a **calm, therapeutic environment**.
3. Complete a thorough **assessment and MSE**.
4. Manage **symptoms and medication effects**.
5. Address **housing, food, employment, support** barriers.
6. Support **recovery, self-management, engagement**.

#### Peplau phases with psychosis? `[Engage]`
| Phase | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Orientation** | Build alliance; nurse examines own bias. Barriers: client doesn't see the need, symptoms, cognitive deficits |
| **Identification** | Client becomes aware of issues; keep safe; collect data |
| **Exploitation** | Client uses interventions, learns triggers; clear limits. Involuntary admission can strain trust → rebuild it |
| **Resolution** | Lives with the disorder using ongoing recovery supports |

#### Therapeutic communication rules?
- ==Stay calm and patient==; **simple, clear, direct** language; call the client by name `[Engage]`.
- ==Respect personal space; **avoid unexpected touch**== (can feel threatening `[Engage]`).
- ==**Do not argue with, agree with, or reinforce** delusions/hallucinations.==
- ==**Acknowledge feelings while presenting reality.**==

#### Responding to delusions and hallucinations?
- Acknowledge the experience and feelings ==without validating the belief as reality==. Use reality-based statements.
- Ask about **frequency, triggers, intensity, duration, impact**; document them `[Engage]`.
- **Distraction** (walk, simple task) and **reality testing** (check with trusted others `[Engage]`); mindfulness grounding (location, weather, breathing) `[Engage]`.
- ==**Reduce environmental stimulation**==; well-lit room `[Engage]`.

> [!info] Key contrast
> Client: "Listen, they're coming for me! Did you hear that?"
> **Therapeutic:** "What did you hear?" (direct, explores) `[Engage]` · "I don't see a wolf in the room. I understand this must be scary." `[Engage]` · "Yes, there's a new client on the unit. They aren't asking about you." `[Engage]`
> **Non-therapeutic:** "There is no one coming for you." (argues) · "It's okay, you're hallucinating." (dismisses/labels) · "Yes, I hear them too." (reinforces) · "Did I hear what?" (vague) `[Engage]`

#### Command hallucinations?

> [!danger] Report immediately
> ==**Always ask what the voices are saying.**== Are they telling the client to ==harm self or others==? If yes → **immediate safety assessment and intervention**: appropriate observation (one-to-one if needed `[Engage]`), ==remove potential means of harm== (sharps, cords; secure windows/exits `[Engage]`), **notify the team**.

#### Therapeutic milieu?
- ==Calm, low-stimulation environment==: less noise, fewer competing stimuli (TV).
- Clear, concise communication; structured simple daily schedule; reorient with physical cues (whiteboard with date) `[Engage]`.
- **Monitor** mental status, **vital signs, neuro status**, safety, suicidality.
- Support **nutrition, hydration, hygiene, toileting, rest**: offer food, drink, bathroom `[Engage]`.

> [!example] Worked example — disturbed thought processes `[Engage care plan]`
> Cues: repeat admissions, word salad/neologisms, wandering in the street, can't follow directions.
> **Priority goal:** ==remains free from injury== during hospitalization. **Long-term:** states **2** activities to participate in before step-down.
> Actions: reduce stimuli, calm approach, reorient, simple schedule, consistent expectations, meds for positive symptoms.

## VIII. Treatment and medications

#### Treatment goals and continuum of care?
- ==Reduce symptoms and prevent relapse==; improve function and quality of life.
- Combine **medication, therapy, education, community support**.
- **Coordinated specialty care** (early psychosis; recovery-oriented), **CBT** (cognitive restructuring of delusions/negative thoughts `[Engage]`), family/group therapy, case management.
- **ACT = assertive community treatment**: community team for clients with repeated hospitalizations or houselessness `[Engage]` (not acceptance and commitment therapy).

#### Typical vs. atypical antipsychotics?
| | First-generation (typical) | Second-generation (atypical) |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | **Haloperidol, chlorpromazine, fluphenazine** | **Risperidone, olanzapine, quetiapine, ziprasidone, aripiprazole, clozapine** |
| Action | ==D2 dopamine blockade== | D2 + ==serotonin== effects |
| Targets | ==Positive== symptoms | Positive **and negative** `[Engage]` |
| Main risk | ==EPS== (more side effects overall `[Engage]`) | ==**Metabolic**== (several agents) |

#### Medication teaching?
- ==Antipsychotics treat symptoms; they **don't cure**.==
- Some improvement in **days**; full effect takes **several weeks** (up to **6 wk** `[Engage]`).
- ==Side effects are a major reason clients stop.== Ask about them; report rather than stop.
- **Long-acting injectables** help some clients with adherence (e.g., haloperidol decanoate, paliperidone palmitate `[ADDED]`).
- Antidepressants/mood stabilizers may be added for mood symptoms `[Engage]`.

#### EPS timeline?
| EPS | Onset | Findings | Nursing action `[ADDED]` |
|---|---|---|---|
| ==**Acute dystonia**== | ==Hours to days== | Painful muscle spasms (neck, eyes, tongue); ==**airway risk if laryngeal**== | Emergency: IM/IV anticholinergic (benztropine) or diphenhydramine; protect airway |
| **Akathisia** | Early | ==Intense inner restlessness, pacing, can't sit still== | Report; may need dose change or beta blocker; don't mistake for agitation |
| **Pseudoparkinsonism** | Weeks | Tremor, rigidity, ==shuffling gait==, slowed movement | Anticholinergic (benztropine); fall precautions |
| ==**Tardive dyskinesia**== | ==Months to years== | Involuntary **facial/oral** movements (lip-smacking, tongue protrusion `[Engage]`) | ==May be permanent==; AIMS; report early |

#### Neuroleptic malignant syndrome?

> [!danger] Report immediately
> ==**NMS**: rare but potentially fatal.== ==**High fever + severe muscle rigidity + altered mental status**==, plus autonomic instability (BP changes, tachycardia, sweating); **CK may be elevated**.
> **Priority:** ==**stop/hold the antipsychotic**, notify the provider==, supportive care (cooling, IV fluids, monitor kidney function; dantrolene or bromocriptine may be ordered `[ADDED]`).

#### Metabolic effects and clozapine?
- **Metabolic:** weight gain, hyperglycemia/diabetes, ↑ cholesterol and triglycerides. ==Monitor **weight/BMI, waist circumference, BP, glucose/A1C, lipids**.==
- ==**Clozapine**: highly effective for **treatment-resistant** schizophrenia== but requires ==**ANC monitoring**== (agranulocytosis). Hold and notify for low ANC per protocol (ANC < **1,000/µL** `[ADDED — verify vs course materials]`).
- ==Report infection symptoms promptly== (fever, sore throat). Also monitor for **seizures, myocarditis** (chest pain, tachycardia `[ADDED]`), **constipation** (can become severe `[ADDED]`), **sialorrhea** (drooling).

#### Other antipsychotic adverse effects?
| Effect | Nursing action |
|---|---|
| **Sedation, fall risk** | Fall precautions; no driving until effects known `[ADDED]` |
| ==**Orthostatic hypotension**== | Rise slowly; check orthostatic VS `[ADDED]` |
| **Anticholinergic** | Dry mouth, constipation, ==urinary retention==, blurred vision → fluids, fiber, report retention |
| **Sexual dysfunction / ↑ prolactin** | Ask directly; galactorrhea, amenorrhea, gynecomastia (risperidone) `[ADDED]` |
| **QT prolongation** (some agents) | ECG, K+/Mg per order (e.g., ziprasidone `[ADDED]`) |
| **Impulse-control problems** (some agents) | Ask about gambling, shopping, urges (e.g., aripiprazole `[ADDED]`) |

## IX. Clinical judgment, relapse prevention, discharge

#### Six steps for this population?
| Step | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Assessment** | Psychological, social, physical, spiritual, symptom, **medication**, ==safety== cues |
| **Analysis** | Connect symptoms to functioning; ==prioritize the most urgent (safety first)== |
| **Planning** | **SMART** goals (specific, measurable, attainable, time-bound); fit to phase, ability, living situation |
| **Implementation** | Care, meds, teaching, coordination |
| **Evaluation** | ==Improved, stabilized, or deteriorated?== Not met → reassess and revise |

#### Early warning signs of relapse?
- ==Sleep changes== · increasing negative thoughts · trouble thinking/remembering · poor concentration · ==uncertainty about what is real== · ==hearing voices or increasing paranoia==.

#### Relapse prevention plan?
- Identify **personal warning signs**.
- List ==family, friends, providers, **crisis lines**, emergency contacts==.
- ==Keep the plan accessible== at all times.
- Plan ahead: **children, pets, finances**, preferred providers/hospitals, who to inform.

#### Discharge and health promotion?
- ==**Begin discharge planning early**== (relapse risk is high; unmet social needs → readmission `[Engage]`).
- **Confirm** med understanding (side effects, adherence) and follow-up.
- **Connect** to outpatient mental health and community services; visiting nurse until outpatient care is set up `[Engage]`.
- **Address** housing, food, transportation.
- **Encourage** preventive care, activity, hydration, balanced nutrition (low sodium/fat/sugar `[Engage]`), no alcohol `[Engage]`, ==smoking cessation support==.

#### Three R's of recovery?

> [!example] Mnemonic — 3 R's of recovery
> **Remediation**: reduce the impact of illness; prevent relapse
> **Restoration**: rebuild confidence and daily functioning
> **Reconnection**: reconnect with community, independence, relationships, hope

## X. Living with a serious mental illness (SMI)

#### What makes an illness "serious"?
- Mental illness that ==substantially interferes with or limits major life activities==: schizophrenia spectrum, severe bipolar, severe major depression.
- ==**Functional impact, not diagnosis alone**, is central.==

#### Social challenges?
- **Housing instability/houselessness** ↔ unemployment (vicious cycle `[Engage]`) · financial stress · food insecurity · limited treatment access · isolation, loneliness · ==caregiver burden== (support caregivers to prevent burnout `[Engage]`) · **incarceration** and ==**victimization**==.
- Employment supports recovery (income, housing, social contact, self-esteem) `[Engage]`.

#### Stigma, violence, advocacy?
- ==Diagnosis alone does not determine violence.== Risk factors: **substance use, untreated symptoms**, comorbidities, poverty, environment.
- ==People with SMI are at significant risk of **being victims** of violence== (14× more likely to be crime victims `[Engage]`).
- Nurses ==challenge stigma== (including their own bias) and advocate for equitable care.

#### Treatment challenges and language?
- Barriers: ==**anosognosia**==, med adverse effects, stigma, cost, limited support, transportation, rural access.
- ==Don't automatically label a client "**noncompliant**."== Explore **why**, document objectively; the goal is to ==reduce barriers, not assign blame== (compliance bias `[Engage]`).

#### Recovery across the lifespan?
- Generally long-term; severity and function change over time. ==Relapses can happen, but stability and remission are possible.==
- Recovery = a ==meaningful, hopeful, self-directed life==, not just absence of symptoms.

## Self-quiz

> [!question]- 1. Psychosis: diagnosis or manifestation? What's the first nursing question?
> A clinical manifestation. Assess for the underlying cause (medical, medication, substance, psychiatric).

> [!question]- 2. Hallucination vs. delusion?
> Hallucination = sensory perception without a stimulus. Delusion = fixed false belief despite evidence.

> [!question]- 3. Explain the 2–1–6 hook.
> ≥ 2 core symptoms (one must be delusions, hallucinations, or disorganized speech), 1 month active, 6 months continuous, plus functional decline.

> [!question]- 4. Psychotic symptoms for 3 weeks after a major stressor, then full return to baseline. Diagnosis?
> Brief psychotic disorder (≥ 1 day, < 1 month).

> [!question]- 5. Schizophrenia-like symptoms for 4 months. Diagnosis?
> Schizophreniform disorder (1 to < 6 months).

> [!question]- 6. Hallucinations only during manic episodes: schizoaffective or bipolar with psychotic features?
> Bipolar with psychotic features. Schizoaffective needs psychosis ≥ 2 weeks without a mood episode.

> [!question]- 7. Which dopamine pathway is linked to hallucinations? To avolition?
> Mesolimbic → positive. Mesocortical → negative/cognitive.

> [!question]- 8. A client won't start tasks, speaks in short phrases, and doesn't enjoy visits. Name the symptoms.
> Avolition, alogia, anhedonia (negative symptoms).

> [!question]- 9. Speech wanders through details but gets back to the point. Term?
> Circumstantiality. Tangentiality never returns.

> [!question]- 10. A 78-year-old has new visual hallucinations and fluctuating attention. Priority?
> Suspect delirium (medical emergency); assess for a medical cause before assuming a psychiatric disorder.

> [!question]- 11. Which test confirms schizophrenia?
> None. Diagnosis is clinical; testing rules out other causes.

> [!question]- 12. Client says, "They're coming for me! Did you hear that?" Best response?
> "What did you hear?" Explore without arguing or agreeing.

> [!question]- 13. A client says the voices are telling him to hurt his roommate. First action?
> Ask what the voices say (done), then immediate safety assessment and intervention: observation, remove means of harm, notify the team.

> [!question]- 14. Which milieu changes help a hallucinating client?
> Low stimulation (less noise/TV), well-lit area, calm clear communication, structured schedule.

> [!question]- 15. Typical vs. atypical: which carries more EPS, and which more metabolic risk?
> Typical → more EPS. Atypical → metabolic risk.

> [!question]- 16. 6 hours after IM haloperidol, the client's neck twists and he struggles to swallow. Problem and priority?
> Acute dystonia with possible laryngeal involvement. Airway priority; notify; anticholinergic/antihistamine per order.

> [!question]- 17. A client on fluphenazine can't sit still and paces. Term?
> Akathisia.

> [!question]- 18. Which EPS may be permanent, and what tool screens for it?
> Tardive dyskinesia; AIMS at baseline and regular intervals.

> [!question]- 19. High fever, severe muscle rigidity, confusion, labile BP, ↑ CK on haloperidol. Priority action?
> NMS: stop/hold the antipsychotic, notify the provider, supportive care.

> [!question]- 20. A client on clozapine reports sore throat and fever. Action and lab?
> Report promptly: possible agranulocytosis; check ANC.

> [!question]- 21. Metabolic monitoring for olanzapine?
> Weight/BMI, waist circumference, BP, glucose/A1C, lipids.

> [!question]- 22. Name four early warning signs of relapse.
> Sleep changes, ↑ negative thoughts, poor concentration/memory, uncertainty about what's real, voices/paranoia.

> [!question]- 23. A client stopped his meds. How should the nurse frame and document it?
> Explore the reason (side effects, anosognosia, cost, access) and document objectively. Don't label "noncompliant"; reduce barriers.

> [!question]- 24. Does a schizophrenia diagnosis predict violence?
> No. Substance use, untreated symptoms, and environment matter more; people with SMI are more often victims.
