## I. Intestinal disorders

> [!quote] Anchor
> Symptoms overlap, so assessment and testing tell these apart. Keep four priorities in view: ==fluid/electrolytes, pain, nutrition, complications==.

### Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)

#### What is IBS?
- **Functional** disorder: abdominal pain + altered bowel habits ==without visible intestinal damage==.
- Cramping, bloating, gas, diarrhea, constipation, or alternating. Mechanism: brain–gut miscommunication, hypersensitive gut nerves, altered motility. **Stress intensifies symptoms.**
- Engage also lists mucus or bloody stools `[Engage]` [NOTE: current practice: rectal bleeding is an alarm feature that needs workup for IBD or cancer, not a typical IBS finding.]

#### Types and risk factors?
- **IBS-C** (constipation) · **IBS-D** (diarrhea) · **IBS-M** (mixed). ==Treatment follows the predominant pattern.==
- Risks: family history, younger age, **female** `[Engage]`, food sensitivities, emotional stress, meds (antidepressants, antibiotics, **sorbitol** products `[Engage]`).

#### How is IBS diagnosed?
- ==No single test; rule out other conditions first.==
- **Rome criteria** `[Engage]`: abdominal pain **≥ 1 day/wk for 3 mo** + **≥ 2** of: pain related to BMs, change in frequency, change in consistency. Rome IV also requires onset **≥ 6 mo** before diagnosis `[ADDED — verify]`.
- Rule-outs: stool studies (infection, parasites, bile acid), colonoscopy, EGD, CT, **lactose tolerance** test, **breath test** (bacterial overgrowth).

#### Management?
- Identify triggers (food/symptom diary `[Engage]`), stress reduction, hydration, activity, sleep → then symptom-directed meds (spasm, constipation, diarrhea).

### Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)

#### IBD vs. IBS?
- IBD = chronic inflammation with **flares and remission**, causing ==actual bowel injury==, nutritional problems, complications. Two main types: Crohn's and UC.

#### Crohn's vs. ulcerative colitis?
| | Crohn's disease | Ulcerative colitis |
|---|---|---|
| Location | ==Anywhere mouth → anus== | **Colon and rectum** only |
| Pattern | **Skip lesions** | ==Continuous==, starts at **rectum**, extends upward |
| Depth | **Full thickness** (transmural) | **Mucosa + submucosa** |
| Hallmark findings | Diarrhea, rectal bleeding, pain, ↓ appetite, fatigue; skin lesions, joint pain | ==Urgency, bloody diarrhea==, frequent + **nocturnal** stools, mucus, pain |
| Complications | ↑ **fistulas, abscesses, strictures** (perianal) | — |
| Smoking `[Engage]` | ↑ risk (also passive smoke) | Active smoking may ↓ risk |

- Other Crohn's risks `[Engage]`: early-life (even prenatal) antibiotic exposure, early appendectomy/tonsillectomy. UC: prior infection.

> [!warning] Priority
> **Crohn's:** assess the **perianal area** for breakdown, bleeding, abscesses, fistulas; ==meticulous perineal skin care==. **UC:** ask about urgency and nocturnal BMs. Examine **all stools** for blood `[Engage]`.

#### How is IBD diagnosed?
- **CBC:** anemia (blood loss) or infection.
- ==**Colonoscopy with biopsy**== is central. EGD if upper-GI Crohn's suspected. Flexible sigmoidoscopy when colon inflammation is severe `[Engage]`.
- **CT/MRI:** abscess, fistula, obstruction, **perforation**.
- **Capsule endoscopy** (swallowed camera) and **balloon enteroscopy** for hard-to-reach small bowel.

#### IBD medications?
| Class (examples) | Action | Adverse effects / monitoring | Teaching |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Corticosteroids** (prednisone, budesonide) · **aminosalicylates** (mesalamine, olsalazine) | ↓ inflammation of intestinal lining; prevent flares | Headache, bruising, ↑ facial hair, leg edema, appetite change, menstrual change `[Engage]`; electrolyte disturbance `[Engage]` | **Morning**, full glass of water; report infection signs; **no grapefruit** `[Engage]`; don't stop a steroid abruptly (taper) `[ADDED]` |
| **Immunosuppressants** (azathioprine, methotrexate, tofacitinib) | Suppress the immune response that drives inflammation | Headache, fatigue, hair loss, red eyes `[Engage]`; ==infection== | **CBC + LFTs**; no double doses; no breastfeeding `[Engage]`; methotrexate is contraindicated in pregnancy `[ADDED]` |
| **Biologics** (infliximab, adalimumab, vedolizumab, risankizumab) | Block inflammation-producing proteins | Headache, nausea, runny nose, yeast infection, flushing `[Engage]`; ==infection== | Given q**2–8 wk**; CBC + LFTs; report fever, chills; screen for TB and hep B before starting `[ADDED]` |
| **Antibiotics** (ciprofloxacin, metronidazole) `[Engage]` | Treat complications (abscess, fistula) | — | Metronidazole: no alcohol `[ADDED]` |

#### Nutrition, lifestyle, surgery?
- Inflammation + malabsorption → ==vitamin and mineral deficiencies==; supplements `[Engage]`.
- **Smoking and stress trigger flares** → quit; meditation, breathing, exercise `[Engage]`.
- Surgery for complications or resistant disease → possibly a **temporary or permanent ostomy**.

### Bowel obstruction

#### What causes it, and why is it urgent?
- Blockage stops food, fluid, and gas. **SBO** is most often from ==**post-surgical adhesions**==; also tumors, hernias, impacted feces, inflammatory strictures (Crohn's, UC, diverticulitis).
- ==Urgent==: ischemia, perforation, sepsis.

#### What does it look like?
- **Cramping or sharp pain, waxing and waning**; bloating/**distention**; N/V; may be **unable to pass stool or gas**.
- ==Partial obstruction can still cause watery diarrhea== (gut keeps secreting mucus `[Engage]`).
- Abdomen distended, firm; **absent bowel sounds below** the obstruction `[Engage]`.

#### Nursing priorities?
- Assess pain, **last BM**, gas passage, appetite, vomiting, abdominal findings.
- Diagnostics: abdominal x-ray (can miss some), **CT** (better), contrast studies (air/barium enema `[Engage]`).
- **Partial:** laxatives + diet changes `[Engage]`. **Complete:** ==IV fluids, NG decompression==, symptom control, possible **surgery** (adhesions).
- **NG suction care:** **I&O**, tube **patency**, **mouth care**, **electrolytes**. Gastric suction → K+ loss and metabolic alkalosis `[ADDED]`.

### Diverticular disease

#### Diverticulosis vs. diverticulitis?
- **Diverticulosis:** pouches at weak spots in the wall, usually the ==**sigmoid colon**==.
- **Diverticulitis:** a pouch becomes **inflamed or infected** → ==persistent **LLQ** tenderness==, fever, N/V, constipation (more common) or diarrhea.
- Risks: ↑ age, obesity, smoking, low activity, **low-fiber diet**, meds (**steroids, opioids, NSAIDs** `[Engage]`).

#### Complications and care?
- **Abscess, obstruction, fistula, peritonitis** from perforation.
- Workup: CBC, urine, stool (infection); liver panel + **pregnancy test** (rule-outs) `[Engage]`. ==**CT** confirms diagnosis and severity.==
- Engage: treated with more fiber, antibiotics if infected, and surgery (colon resection/colectomy) for obstruction or perforation `[Engage]`. [NOTE: current practice: acute flare = clear liquids/low fiber, then advance; high fiber is for **prevention** once it resolves.]
- **Prevention:** hydration, activity, smoking cessation, appropriate fiber.

> [!info] Key contrast
> **Obstruction pain** = crampy, comes and goes, with no stool/gas. **Diverticulitis pain** = ==constant LLQ tenderness== with fever `[Engage]`.

### Celiac disease

#### What is celiac disease?
- **Autoimmune** reaction to **gluten** (==wheat, barley, rye==) → damages the small-intestinal lining → **villous atrophy** → ↓ absorbing surface → malabsorption.
- Genes **HLA-DQ2 / HLA-DQ8**. Risks: family history (celiac or dermatitis herpetiformis), **T1DM**, Down/Turner syndrome, autoimmune thyroid disease, Addison's `[Engage]`. Not classified as IBD; often mistaken for IBS `[Engage]`.

#### Findings?
- Diarrhea, bloating, flatulence, fatigue, **weight loss**, **anemia**; lactose intolerance common `[Engage]`.
- Extraintestinal: **bone loss**, mouth ulcers, **neuropathy**, joint pain, ↓ spleen function (frequent infections) `[Engage]`, **dermatitis herpetiformis** (intensely itchy blistering rash `[ADDED]`); CNS: headache, seizures `[Engage]`.
- Assess: anemia/osteoporosis history, blistery rash, oral lesions, numbness/tingling, gait and balance `[Engage]`.

#### Diagnosis?
- **tTG-IgA** antibody; labs (anemia, malabsorption); genetic testing; ==**small-bowel biopsy** usually confirms==; skin biopsy for rash `[Engage]`.
- Client must still be eating gluten for tTG-IgA and biopsy to be accurate `[ADDED]`.

#### Treatment?
> [!tip] NCLEX tip
> ==Strict, lifelong gluten-free diet== is the only treatment. Avoid wheat, barley, rye (breads, pastas, cereals, processed foods `[Engage]`); **read labels** for hidden gluten; **dietitian** referral.

## II. Bowel function: overall health, diet, meds, ostomies

#### Effects on overall health?
- Weight loss, **malnutrition, dehydration, anemia, osteoporosis**, vitamin/mineral deficiencies; anal fissures/fistulas, fertility problems `[Engage]`.
- Work, relationships, social life disrupted → assess **anxiety, depression**, impact of unpredictable symptoms.
- ==**Older adults:** ↑ risk of dehydration, malnutrition, constipation, obstruction== (plus ADL limits on shopping and cooking `[Engage]`).

#### Nutrition, fluids, electrolytes?
- Diarrhea, inflammation, malabsorption, blood loss → deficits; corticosteroids can disturb electrolytes `[Engage]`.
- Dehydration signs to teach `[Engage]`: thirst, **dark yellow urine**, dizziness, dry mouth/cracked lips, ↓ tears, headache, **tachycardia**, confusion/delirium.
- **Low-residue diet:** for selected **flares** or **before procedures** (e.g., colonoscopy). OK: eggs, white rice, lean poultry, refined grains `[Engage]`.
- ==**Gas-producing foods**== (beans, broccoli, cabbage, carbonated drinks `[Engage]`) worsen bloating and ostomy pouch gas.
- General teaching `[Engage]`: avoid tobacco, caffeine, alcohol; ↑ water; report blood in stool, vomiting blood, persistent diarrhea or constipation, unexplained weight loss.

#### Bristol Stool Chart?
| Type | Look `[Engage]` | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **1–2** | Separate hard lumps / lumpy sausage | ==Constipation== |
| **3–4** | Sausage with cracks / smooth soft snake | **Well-formed** |
| **5** | Soft blobs, clear edges | Not classified in the deck |
| **6–7** | Mushy, fluffy pieces / watery, no solids | ==Loose to liquid== |

- Use it to **document patterns** and **evaluate treatment**.

#### Bowel-function meds?
| Drug | Action | Caution | Teaching |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Psyllium** (Metamucil) | **Bulk-forming**: absorbs fluid, ↑ stool bulk; helps both diarrhea and constipation `[Engage]` | ==Needs adequate fluid== | **8 oz** fluid; results in a couple of days; ↑ fiber and fluids `[Engage]` |
| **Magnesium hydroxide** (MOM), **polyethylene glycol** (MiraLAX) | ==Retain water in stool== | ==Mg-containing: caution in kidney impairment== | Dissolve PEG in **8 oz** fluid `[Engage]` |

### Ostomies

#### Ileostomy vs. colostomy output?
- **Ostomy** = surgical opening from bowel to skin; the opening is the **stoma**.
- **Ileostomy/jejunostomy** = small bowel · **colostomy** = colon (transverse, right, left `[Engage]`).
- ==The farther along the bowel, the more formed the output.== High small-bowel output → ==dehydration and electrolyte loss==.

#### Stoma assessment and pouch care?
- **Healthy:** ==pink to beefy red, moist==.

> [!danger] Report immediately
> Stoma **dusky, purple, or black** = impaired blood supply (possible gangrene `[Engage]`) → immediate attention.

- Empty pouch at **⅓ full** (prevents leaks); change appliance q**5–7 days** or sooner if leaking or irritated; watch peristomal skin, use barriers `[Engage]`. Stool returns around POD **2–4** `[Engage]`.
- Teaching starts **pre-op** when possible; hand hygiene, safe disposal `[Engage]`.
- **Body image:** normalize, support; refer to **WOC nurse** and support groups; teach odor and gas control.

### Clinical judgment: intestinal disorders

#### Six steps for intestinal disorders?
| Step | Focus |
|---|---|
| Recognize cues | History, meds (**ASA/NSAIDs** `[Engage]`), bowel pattern, stool/emesis (blood?), pain, bleeding, weight; ==inspect → auscultate → percuss → palpate== |
| Analyze | Pain? Bloody stool/emesis? Risks, labs, imaging |
| Prioritize | ==**Fluid/electrolyte balance** + preventing complications (perforation)== |
| Generate solutions | F&E replacement, pain control; dietitian, GI |
| Take actions | Symptom control, fluids/electrolytes, antidiarrheals as ordered, **perianal skin care**, **I&O, daily weights**, education |
| Evaluate | Pain, diarrhea, constipation, bleeding, vomiting controlled? Modify plan |

## III. Liver disorders

> [!quote] Anchor
> Think manifestation: every liver function that fails produces a finding you can see.

#### Liver function → what you'll see when it fails?
| Function | Failure → finding |
|---|---|
| **Albumin** (oncotic pressure) | ↓ albumin → ==edema, ascites== |
| **Clotting factors, thrombopoietin** | ==Bleeding risk==; thrombocytopenia (up to **70%** of cirrhosis `[Engage]`) |
| **Bilirubin processing** | **Jaundice**, dark urine, clay stool |
| **Ammonia → urea** | ↑ ammonia → ==hepatic encephalopathy== |
| **Drug/alcohol metabolism** | ↑ **medication toxicity** |

### Hepatitis

#### Causes and findings?
- Hepatitis = liver **inflammation**: viral, alcohol, toxins/meds/supplements ("toxic hepatitis"), fat accumulation, autoimmune. Chronic → **fibrosis → cirrhosis**.
- Fatigue, anorexia, N/V, abdominal pain, fever, joint pain `[Engage]`, ==dark urine, clay-colored stool, jaundice==.

#### Viral hepatitis transmission?
| Virus | Spread | Vaccine | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | ==Fecal–oral== | **Yes** | Self-limited; supportive (rest, fluids) |
| **E** | Fecal–oral | — | — |
| **B** | Blood and body fluids | **Yes** | May need **long-term antivirals** |
| **D** | Blood and body fluids; ==requires hep B== | — | — |
| **C** | **Primarily blood** | ==**No vaccine**== | **Direct-acting antivirals (DAAs)** |

- Engage: HBV treated with weekly **interferon** for **6 mo**; DAAs for **24–48 wk** by genotype `[Engage]` [NOTE: current practice: hep B is mostly treated with long-term oral antivirals (entecavir, tenofovir); most DAA courses are **8–12 wk**. Deck wording used above.]

#### DAA nursing points?
- Ledipasvir-sofosbuvir, sofosbuvir (and simeprevir `[Engage]`) block viral protein production.
- ==Test for current or prior hep B **before** starting== (boxed warning: **HBV reactivation**).
- AEs: diarrhea, fatigue, headache, N/V, **bradycardia** `[Engage]` (especially with amiodarone `[ADDED]`). Drink fluids; monitor **LFTs** `[Engage]`.

#### Prevention teaching? `[Engage]`
- **Hep A:** frequent **handwashing**.
- **Hep B/C:** cover cuts; dispose of tissues and tampons properly; ==don't share razors, toothbrushes, needles==; clean blood with **bleach and water**; condoms.
- Standard precautions; infection spreads even when the person feels well. Vaccinate for **A and B**.

### Alcohol-associated liver disease and MASLD

#### ALD vs. MASLD?
| | ALD | MASLD → MASH |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Chronic heavy alcohol | Fat buildup linked to **metabolic disorders** |
| Risks | Obesity, hepatitis, smoking, genetics | ==Central obesity, dyslipidemia, HTN, T2DM, ↑ glucose== |
| Course | Progressive injury; early disease may improve | Fat → inflammation and cell injury (**MASH**) → fibrosis → cirrhosis |
| Key intervention | ==**Alcohol abstinence**==; offer treatment/rehab `[Engage]` | Metabolic risk control `[ADDED]` |

### Cirrhosis

#### Compensated vs. decompensated?
- Progressive **scarring** from chronic liver disease. No cure; can progress to failure `[Engage]`.
- **Compensated:** scarring, no complications. **Decompensated:** ==ascites, hepatic encephalopathy, GI bleeding, jaundice==.

#### Key manifestations?
- **Edema, ascites** · ==**easy bruising and bleeding**== · **spider nevi** (face, neck, chest, arms `[Engage]`), **palmar erythema** · **pruritus** (bile salts; generalized, not relieved by scratching `[Engage]`) · weight loss · **jaundice, dark urine, clay stool** · **confusion / mental-status change**.

#### Why jaundice, dark urine, clay stool?
- Bilirubin comes from heme breakdown and is processed by the liver. Dysfunction → **bilirubin builds up → jaundice**.
- ==Excess conjugated bilirubin in urine → dark (amber/cola) urine==. **Less bilirubin reaching the gut → pale/clay stool.**
- ==Check **sclera and mucous membranes**==, especially with darker skin tones.

#### Portal hypertension, varices, ascites?
- Scarring ↑ resistance to portal flow → **portal hypertension** → collateral vessels enlarge into fragile **esophageal, gastric, rectal varices**.
- **Ascites** = portal HTN + **low albumin** + renal Na/water retention. Grades **1–2**: diuretics + diet. **Grade 3** (refractory): ==**paracentesis**== `[Engage]`. Risks: breathing problems, hernias, **spontaneous bacterial peritonitis**/sepsis `[Engage]`.
- Paracentesis care `[ADDED — verify]`: void first; weigh and measure girth before and after; watch BP (fluid shift).

> [!danger] Report immediately
> ==Variceal rupture = life-threatening hemorrhage==: hematemesis, melena, tachycardia, hypotension. Protect the airway, establish large-bore IV access, and anticipate transfusion and endoscopic banding `[ADDED]`. Older adults: "hemorrhoid" bleeding may really be **rectal varices** `[Engage]`.

#### Hepatic encephalopathy?
- ↓ conversion of **ammonia → urea** → altered brain function: subtle confusion → lethargy → disorientation → **coma**.
- ==**Asterixis**== is the classic finding: flapping tremor of the hands when wrists are extended `[ADDED]`.
- **Lactulose** promotes ammonia excretion in stool (titrate to **2–3 soft stools/day**; watch K+ and dehydration `[ADDED — verify]`). **Rifaximin** ↓ ammonia-producing gut bacteria.
- Older adults: may be mistaken for **dementia or delirium** `[Engage]`.

#### Liver cancer and failure?
- **HCC** linked to chronic inflammation and cirrhosis: chronic **hep B/C, ALD, MASLD**. More common in males, ages **45–65** `[Engage]`; often found late.
- Progressive disease → **liver failure** → ==transplant== is the only definitive option for end-stage disease.
- Acute liver failure `[Engage]`: IV fluids to maintain BP; transfusion for blood loss.

#### Liver diagnostics?
| Test | Normal `[ADDED — verify vs course materials]` | Abnormal means |
|---|---|---|
| **Albumin** | **3.5–5.0 g/dL** | ↓ → edema, ascites |
| **AST / ALT** | ~**10–40** / **7–56 U/L** | ↑ = liver cell injury |
| **Total bilirubin** | **0.1–1.2 mg/dL** | ↑ → jaundice |
| **PT / INR** | **11–13.5 sec** / **0.8–1.1** | ↑ = ↓ clotting factors → bleeding |
| **Ammonia** | ~**15–45 mcg/dL** (lab-specific) | ↑ → encephalopathy |
| **Platelets (CBC)** | **150,000–400,000/mm³** | ↓ → bleeding precautions |

- Also ALP, total protein, LDH `[Engage]`; **hepatitis panel** (antigens = virus, antibodies = immune response/vaccine); autoimmune markers ANA, AMA, ASMA `[Engage]`.
- Imaging: ultrasound, CT, MRI; ==biopsy is the gold standard== for staging fibrosis `[Engage]`; **elastography** (FibroScan/VCTE, MR elastography) measures liver stiffness noninvasively.

### Liver nursing care `[Engage]`

#### Priority concerns?
> [!warning] Priority
> ==**Bleeding** (thrombocytopenia), **fluid overload** (ascites), **hepatic encephalopathy** (↑ ammonia).== Plan: scheduled **neuro checks**, **fall and seizure precautions** (as ordered), **bed alarm**.

#### Bleeding precautions?
- **Electric razor**, soft toothbrush, **no flossing**, ==no ASA or NSAIDs==, hold pressure on venipuncture sites. Older adults: falls + low platelets → **head trauma** risk.

#### Sodium, fluids, nutrition?
- **Hyponatremia** (water retention) is common: defined as **< 130 mEq/L** in cirrhosis (vs. **< 135**); ==**< 125** → cerebral edema==, ↓ LOC, brain damage. Treat with **fluid restriction 1–1.5 L/day** and ==**hold diuretics**==; monitor K+; ==avoid rapid overcorrection==. Teach visitors to report new lethargy, confusion, aphasia, N/V.
- **Ascites:** moderate salt restriction **5–6.5 g/day** + diuretics; **daily weights**, track sodium and fluids.
- No added table salt; avoid hidden sodium: canned soups and vegetables, bread, chips, condiments, cereal, deli meats, cheese, frozen dinners. Balanced, **low-fat** diet: produce, lean protein (chicken, fish, legumes).
- Malnutrition and **sarcopenia** risk: **small frequent meals**, snacks between meals and **at bedtime**; dietitian. Calories are calculated on **ideal body weight**, not ascites weight.

#### Comfort, skin, safety?
- Ascites/edema: HOB up or legs raised for breathing; rest periods; keep skin folds (groin, perineum) clean and dry.
- **Pruritus:** trim nails, moisturize.
- Clean home; safe food handling (↓ immunity). ==Avoid alcohol and hepatotoxins (acetaminophen, NSAIDs)==. [NOTE: current practice: low-dose acetaminophen (≤ **2 g/day**) is generally the preferred analgesic in cirrhosis; NSAIDs are avoided.]
- Reflect on bias and stigma toward alcohol- or virus-related disease.

## IV. Gallbladder: gallstones and cholecystitis

#### Who gets gallstones, and when do they cause cholecystitis?
- Often **asymptomatic** until they block bile flow. ==A stone blocking the **cystic duct**== → pain, inflammation, infection, ischemia.
- Most (~**75%** `[Engage]`) are **cholesterol** stones.
- Risks: **obesity**, diabetes, ==rapid weight change, prolonged fasting==, low activity, family history, **age ≥ 65**; **female < 50** (**3×**), **estrogen/oral contraceptives**, **pregnancy** (**1–3%**), low-fiber/high-fat diet, cirrhosis, hep C, metabolic syndrome `[Engage]`.
- **Calculous** (stone; most cases) vs. **acalculous** (no stone): critically ill, trauma/burns, sepsis, prolonged **TPN**/no oral intake. ==Acalculous is life-threatening== (perforation, necrosis) `[Engage]`.

#### Clinical presentation?
- ==**RUQ pain**, often after a **high-fat meal**==, radiating to the **right shoulder**, back, or neck.
- N/V, anorexia, sweating, **fever**. **Jaundice is less common** → suggests **common bile duct** obstruction.
- **Older adults: vague symptoms.** Can mimic a **cardiac** problem `[Engage]`.

#### Diagnosis?
- **Murphy's sign:** ==inspiratory arrest with RUQ palpation==.
- **CBC** (leukocytosis), **CRP** ↑; CMP; amylase/lipase may rise in severe cases `[Engage]`.
- ==**Abdominal ultrasound** is the preferred first imaging test.==
- **HIDA scan:** IV radioactive tracer → gallbladder filling and function. **MRCP:** detailed MRI of the biliary system `[Engage]`.
- **ERCP:** endoscope through the mouth to the duodenum, **contrast dye injected into the ducts**; can **diagnose and remove duct stones** `[Engage]`. ERCP itself is a pancreatitis risk (Section V).

#### Treatment and nursing care?
- Dehydration from poor intake, N/V → ==**NPO**, IV fluids==, electrolyte correction, **I&O**, pain control, antiemetics, **IV antibiotics**.
- **Priority:** ==control pain + watch for infection== `[Engage]`.
- Quiet environment; mouth care and lip balm while NPO; position of comfort; **fall precautions** (dizziness) `[Engage]`.
- **Laparoscopic cholecystectomy** is the definitive treatment for surgical candidates. **ERCP** for common-bile-duct stones. Older adults: often medical management first `[Engage]`.
- Complications if untreated: ==**gangrene, perforation**==, abscess, sepsis `[Engage]`.

#### Post-cholecystectomy care?
- Monitor pain, N/V, **fever**, I&O; observation **12–24 hr** `[Engage]`.
- ==**Referred shoulder pain** from laparoscopy CO₂ gas== (~**3 days** `[Engage]`) → **walking** helps absorb it.
- **Lower-fat diet** easier early (no formal restrictions after lap chole `[Engage]`).
- Report ==worsening pain, fever==, jaundice, vomiting. Follow-up ~**2 wk** `[Engage]`.
- Prevention teaching `[Engage]`: less fried/fatty food, more fiber, exercise, ==no crash diets or prolonged fasting==.

## V. Pancreas: pancreatitis

#### Endocrine vs. exocrine roles?
- **Endocrine:** **insulin and glucagon** regulate blood glucose.
- **Exocrine:** digestive enzymes: ==**amylase** → carbs · **lipase** → fats · **trypsin** → proteins==.

#### Acute vs. chronic pancreatitis?
| | Acute | Chronic |
|---|---|---|
| Onset | Sudden; may resolve with treatment | Progressive |
| Mechanism | ==Premature enzyme activation → **autodigestion**== + inflammation | **Irreversible fibrosis/scarring** |
| Consequence | Fluid shifts, systemic complications; severe → **necrosis, organ failure** | ↓ digestive enzymes **and ↓ insulin** → malabsorption, diabetes risk |

#### Causes?
- ==Two major causes: **gallstones** (block the pancreatic duct) and **alcohol** (direct cell injury).==
- Also smoking, family history, **ERCP**, abdominal surgery/trauma, infection.

#### Findings?
- ==**Severe epigastric/upper abdominal pain radiating to the back**==; N/V.
- Watch for **fluid shifts, electrolyte abnormalities, hyperglycemia**, systemic complications. Assess for **jaundice** `[Engage summary]`.

#### Treatment?
- Supportive: ==**fluid/electrolytes + pain control**==. **Isotonic crystalloid (lactated Ringer's)** for resuscitation; aggressive fluids with close monitoring.
- Antiemetics, analgesics. **NPO first → gradual advance to a low-fat diet.**
- Treat the cause (biliary obstruction/gallstones).
- Key interventions `[Engage summary]`: IV hydration, pain management, **hemodynamic monitoring**, ==aggressive respiratory care==, biliary drainage management.

#### Chronic pancreatitis and enzyme replacement?
- **Malabsorption, diarrhea, steatorrhea** (fatty, foul, floating stools `[ADDED]`), weight loss.
- **Pancrelipase:** ==give with **every meal and snack**==; ==**don't crush or chew**== delayed-release products.
- **Effective** = improved stool quality + **weight gain**.

## VI. Fecal impaction and digital removal

> [!quote] Anchor
> Liquid stool after days without a formed BM may be stool leaking around an impaction, not diarrhea.

#### Recognize impaction?
- A large, hard stool mass lodged in the **rectum or lower colon**.
- Risks: **immobility**, low fluids, **low fiber**, ==**opioids**, anticholinergics==, neurologic disorders, **older age**.
- Distention, rectal fullness, pain, poor appetite, dehydration, vomiting. ==Liquid stool leaks around it and gets mistaken for diarrhea.==
- Untreated → ==**perforation and sepsis**==.

#### Nursing priorities?
- **Suspect it** with liquid stool after a long absence of formed stool. **Digital rectal exam** confirms stool in the rectum.
- ==Laxatives and enemas first==, then digital removal.

> [!warning] Priority
> Digital removal requires nursing assessment: ==**do not delegate** to assistive personnel==.

#### Before you begin?
- Review chart, **policy**, allergies, contraindications. Gather PPE, gloves, pads, **lubricant**, bedpan/commode, cleansing supplies.
- Verify ID, explain; **baseline VS** and comfort; privacy and reassurance.

#### Contraindications and risks?
- Check for: **bleeding disorders**, hemorrhoids, rectal polyps, **recent rectal/abdominal/perineal/GYN surgery**, **pregnancy**, selected cardiac/pulmonary conditions.
- **Vagal stimulation** → ==**bradycardia**==.
- **SCI at T6 or above** → ==**autonomic dysreflexia**==: sudden severe HTN, pounding headache, flushing and sweating above the injury, bradycardia. Stop, sit the client up, remove the trigger, notify `[ADDED]`.

#### Procedure steps?
1. Position: ==**left lateral, knees flexed**==; absorbent pad; privacy.
2. Inspect perianal area: breakdown, hemorrhoids, swelling, prolapse.
3. Gloved, **lubricated** finger; go **slowly**. **Assess** the amount of stool.
4. **Rotate** around the mass, keeping contact with the mucosa → **work** stool downward → **break** into smaller pieces (==don't pull forcefully==) → **remove** gradually.
5. ==Monitor HR and BP throughout==.

> [!danger] Report immediately
> **Stop** for lightheadedness, **diaphoresis**, pain, **bleeding**, ==bradycardia==, autonomic dysreflexia, or significant VS change. Mass too large and firm to break → **stop and notify the provider**.

#### After: care and documentation?
- Perineal care, reposition, restore safety, hand hygiene.
- **Document:** date/time, procedure, **amount and character** of stool, client response, education, unexpected outcomes, provider notification.
- **Prevent recurrence:** fluids, fiber, mobility, med review, bowel regimen.

## VII. Unit priorities and emergencies

#### Where do fluid/electrolyte losses come from?
| Condition | F&E problem |
|---|---|
| Diarrhea, malabsorption | Fluid and electrolyte losses |
| Vomiting, **NG suction** | Additional losses |
| Cirrhosis | Ascites; sodium/water management (hyponatremia) |
| Cholecystitis | Dehydration; NPO |
| Pancreatitis | ==Aggressive IV resuscitation, close monitoring== |

#### Pain patterns?
- **Cramping/colicky** (obstruction, IBS) · **RUQ** (cholecystitis) · ==**severe epigastric** → back== (pancreatitis) · **constant LLQ** (diverticulitis).
- Diets are condition-specific: **low-residue**, **gluten-free**, **low-fat**, individualized. ==**Daily weights and I&O**== are high-value nursing measures.

#### What can become an emergency?
| Emergency | Watch for |
|---|---|
| **Perforation / peritonitis** | Sudden severe pain, **rigid abdomen**, fever, tachycardia `[ADDED for signs]` |
| **GI bleed / variceal hemorrhage** | Hematemesis, melena, tachycardia, hypotension |
| **Sepsis** | Fever, ↑ HR, ↓ BP, confusion `[ADDED]` |
| **Fistula, stricture, obstruction** | No stool/gas, distention, vomiting |
| **Hepatic encephalopathy** | Confusion, asterixis |
| **Pancreatic necrosis / organ failure** | Worsening pain, hemodynamic instability |
| **Gallbladder gangrene / perforation** | Worsening RUQ pain, fever |

- Nursing focus: ==recognize changes early==; prioritize F&E, pain, nutrition, safety; teach what to report; reassess and modify.

## Self-quiz

> [!question]- 1. IBS vs. IBD: the core difference?
> IBS is functional with no visible intestinal damage; IBD causes real inflammatory bowel injury with flares and remission.

> [!question]- 2. Continuous inflammation starting at the rectum with bloody diarrhea and urgency: Crohn's or UC?
> Ulcerative colitis.

> [!question]- 3. Which IBD carries the higher risk of fistulas, and what does the nurse assess?
> Crohn's (full thickness). Assess the perianal area for breakdown, bleeding, abscesses, fistulas; meticulous perineal care.

> [!question]- 4. Two monitoring priorities for clients on IBD immunosuppressants or biologics?
> CBC and liver function tests; signs of infection (fever, chills, fatigue).

> [!question]- 5. Post-op client: crampy, waxing-waning pain, distention, vomiting, no flatus. Likely cause and interventions?
> Small-bowel obstruction from adhesions. IV fluids, NG decompression, symptom control, possible surgery; NG care: I&O, patency, mouth care, electrolytes.

> [!question]- 6. A client with suspected partial obstruction has watery diarrhea. Does that rule out obstruction?
> No. Partial obstruction can still produce watery diarrhea.

> [!question]- 7. Persistent LLQ tenderness with fever: likely diagnosis and confirming test?
> Diverticulitis; CT confirms diagnosis and severity.

> [!question]- 8. Which grains must a client with celiac disease avoid, and what is the only treatment?
> Wheat, barley, rye; strict lifelong gluten-free diet with label reading.

> [!question]- 9. Bristol types 1–2 vs. 6–7?
> 1–2 = constipation; 6–7 = loose to liquid.

> [!question]- 10. Which laxative needs caution in kidney impairment, and what does psyllium require?
> Magnesium-containing (milk of magnesia); psyllium needs adequate fluid (8 oz).

> [!question]- 11. New ileostomy client: biggest F&E risk?
> Dehydration and electrolyte loss from liquid, high-volume output.

> [!question]- 12. Stoma is dark purple on POD 1. Action?
> Impaired blood supply: report immediately.

> [!question]- 13. When should the ostomy pouch be emptied?
> About one-third full.

> [!question]- 14. Which hepatitis viruses spread fecal–orally, and which has no vaccine?
> A and E; hepatitis C has no vaccine.

> [!question]- 15. What must be done before starting ledipasvir-sofosbuvir?
> Test for current or prior hepatitis B (reactivation risk).

> [!question]- 16. Explain jaundice, dark urine, and clay stools in liver failure.
> Bilirubin accumulates (jaundice); excess conjugated bilirubin is excreted in urine (dark); less bilirubin reaches the gut (clay stool).

> [!question]- 17. Cirrhosis client: new confusion and a flapping hand tremor. Problem and drugs?
> Hepatic encephalopathy (asterixis) from ↑ ammonia; lactulose (ammonia excretion), rifaximin (↓ ammonia-producing bacteria).

> [!question]- 18. Cirrhosis client with Na 127 mEq/L. Interventions?
> Fluid restriction 1–1.5 L/day, hold diuretics, monitor K+, avoid rapid correction; neuro checks.

> [!question]- 19. Four bleeding precautions for a client with cirrhosis?
> Electric razor, soft toothbrush/no flossing, no aspirin or NSAIDs, hold pressure on puncture sites.

> [!question]- 20. RUQ pain after a fatty meal radiating to the right shoulder; inspiration stops on RUQ palpation. Sign and first imaging?
> Murphy's sign (cholecystitis); abdominal ultrasound.

> [!question]- 21. After lap chole, the client has right shoulder pain. Cause and advice?
> Referred pain from CO₂ insufflation gas; walking helps absorb it (resolves in ~3 days).

> [!question]- 22. Two main causes of acute pancreatitis, and the classic pain?
> Gallstones and alcohol; severe epigastric pain radiating to the back.

> [!question]- 23. Pancrelipase teaching and sign it's working?
> Take with every meal and snack, don't crush or chew; better stool quality and weight gain.

> [!question]- 24. Bedbound client on opioids has liquid stool after 5 days with no formed BM. Suspect what?
> Fecal impaction with liquid stool leaking around it.

> [!question]- 25. During digital removal the HR drops to 48 and the client is diaphoretic. Action? Who can do this procedure?
> Stop: vagal bradycardia. Licensed nurse only; don't delegate to assistive personnel.
