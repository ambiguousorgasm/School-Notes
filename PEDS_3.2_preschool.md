## I. Biological development

> [!quote] Anchor
> Growth slows and steadies. The gains now are refinement: smoother gross motor, precise fine motor, and an explosion of language, initiative, and social play.

### Physical and motor development

#### Expected preschool growth?
- Steady growth, mostly in **long bones**: about **4.5 lb** and **3 in** per year (module range: **4–5 lb / 1.8–2.25 kg** and **2–3 in / 5–7.5 cm**).
- Follows **proximodistal** trends: limb (gross) skills come before digit (fine) skills.
- Play drives physical development: fine and gross motor skills, coordination, and **spatial-visual intelligence** (hand-eye coordination).
- Example: **40 in** at the 4-year visit → expect ~**43 in** at 5. No change in a year is concerning. `[Engage]`

#### Preschool gross motor skills?
- Running, jumping, and throwing become smooth; climbing.
- Two-foot jump → **balance on one foot** → hop on one foot → skip.
- Stairs with **alternating feet**; walks forward and backward fluidly; catches and throws **overhand**. `[Engage]`
- Needing support to balance, or difficulty hopping, = earlier skill level `[Engage]`.

#### Preschool fine motor skills?
- Safety scissors → cut straight lines → cut shapes and glue `[Engage]`.
- Fist grip on crayons → **pincer grasp**. Shapes in order: circle → square → triangle → diamond. Person with **3** body parts at **4 yr**, **6** at **5 yr** `[Engage]`.
- Masters spoon and fork; spreads with a table knife by the end of the stage; dresses with minimal help (may need help with buttons and zippers early). `[Engage]`
- Supports: play dough or clay, stringing beads, varied writing tools → pencils. Age-appropriate: cutting shapes, drawing people, catch. Push-pull toys = toddler; building models = school-age. `[Engage]`

#### Why do developmental screening?
- ==Early identification makes early intervention possible==, and intervention only helps if the concern is caught in time.
- Annual well visits assess language, motor, social-emotional, and cognitive domains, with the **parent** as the best historian.
- Concerns → formal tool: **Ages and Stages Questionnaire**, **Parents' Evaluation of Developmental Status**, **Child Development Inventories** `[Engage]`. Then refer.

### Cognitive and psychosocial development

#### Piaget's preoperational stage: key traits?
- **Preoperational, 2–7 yr:** thinks symbolically (words and symbols stand for objects and people). The child asks "Why?" constantly.
- **Transductive reasoning:** links two specific events as cause and effect (dog barks, balloon pops → the dog popped it).
- **Centration:** focuses on one feature (number of cake pieces, not their size).
- **Egocentrism:** can't take another's view. Comforts a sad person with *their own* stuffed animal; may feel responsible when bad things happen. Decreases across the stage. `[Engage]`
- **Magical thinking and animism:** the world is alive with purpose; comfort objects are real. An ill child may believe their own behavior caused the illness.

#### Assimilation vs. accommodation?
| | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Assimilation** | Fits new info into an existing schema | Sees a cow, calls it a dog (four legs, like the pet dog) |
| **Accommodation** | Changes or creates a schema when info doesn't fit | Parent says "That's a cow" → new schema: not all four-legged animals are dogs |
| **Equilibration** | Balances the two | |

> [!tip] NCLEX tip
> Use magical thinking and animism, don't fight them. **Assess the doll or stuffed animal first** so the child sees what will happen, let the child "help" take the doll's BP, and **ask the child what made them sick**, correcting self-blame. `[Engage]`

#### Erikson's preschool stage and nursing support?
- **Initiative vs. guilt.** Goal: **purpose**. The child plans, invents games, starts activities, tests limits. Builds on trust and autonomy.
- Criticism or excess control → **guilt**: criticizing a self-chosen outfit, dismissing an imaginary friend, treating questions as a nuisance. Too much guilt → slow to engage. `[Engage]`
- Nurse: set boundaries that leave some control. Let the child help bathe, or **hand them the med cup or oral syringe**. Use play. ==Take questions seriously and answer at their level.==
- **Freud's phallic stage** is largely questioned and considered less relevant today.

### Language and play

#### Expected preschool language?
- **> 2,100** words and sentences of **> 4** words by **age 4**; nonstop talking and questions.
- Understanding is **literal**. At **4**, "I hungry" (missing verb) suggests a delay; "My tummy hurts" and "What are you doing?" are expected. `[Engage]`

#### How to word things for literal thinkers?
| Avoid | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "Put to sleep" (pet euthanasia) | "A doctor will help you sleep, a different kind of sleep than at night, and wake you up after." |
| "Dye" (sounds like "die") | "We'll give you a medicine." |
| "A little stick" (pictures a tree stick), "bee sting" | "Poke." Be honest: "Yes, it will hurt, but just for a few seconds." Never "it won't hurt." Say it's not punishment, it's to help. |

`[Engage: Examples of Wording]`

#### Parallel vs. associative play?
- Moves from **parallel** (beside peers) to **associative**: interest shifts from the toy to the other children.
- Builds cooperation, turn-taking, social competence, and emotional regulation. **Dramatic or pretend play** (kings and knights building a block castle) teaches social roles.
- **Play therapy:** therapist-guided play to express and process emotions at the child's pace.

#### How to use play in care?
- Let the child touch equipment (stethoscope, BP cuff); tell a story ("checking how strong your muscles are"); demonstrate on a doll; cotton balls for art; surgical hat, gown, or mask for dress-up; **blow bubbles** for deep breathing; sing or tell a story. `[Engage]`
- Procedure prep (e.g., PIV): explain what they'll **see, hear, and feel**; let them handle safe items (**open the antiseptic swab**, hold the catheter **without** the needle); **don't show the needle**; **keep the parent present**. Preschoolers fear **bodily injury and pain**. `[Engage]`

## II. Behaviors in the preschool-age client

#### Speech issues: what to know?
- Speech = phonics, grammar, intonation, tempo, rhythm. Two skill sets: **expressive** (producing) and **receptive** language (the deck calls this "impressive").
- About **1 in 12** U.S. children aged **3–17** has a speech or language disorder `[Engage]`.
- Contributors: developmental or learning disability, environment, **hearing**, multilingual homes (may be missed on screening), excess screen time `[Engage]`.
- **Stuttering** is linked to emotional processing and self-regulation, anxiety in social settings, and situational change (starting school, hospitalization, home changes) `[Engage]`.
- ==Report speech delay promptly → speech pathologist==. Earlier treatment, better outcome. Parents can read aloud daily (in any language), point out letters, sing the alphabet, use rhymes and riddles. `[Engage]`

#### Aggression: expected or concerning?
| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Physical** (overt) | Hitting, pushing, shoving, biting |
| **Verbal** (overt) | Shouting, name-calling, insulting, belittling |
| **Relational** (indirect) | Exclusion, "silent treatment" threats, rumors |

- Expected reaction when anger can't be expressed acceptably. **↑ through 3**, should **↓ significantly by 5** as perspective-taking grows.
- Concerning: **repetitive patterns**, consistent roles (aggressor, victim), **intent to harm** (grabbing a toy to get the toy ≠ intent to hurt) `[Engage]`.
- Unaddressed aggression → peer and teacher rejection and parent tension. Emotional regulation, shaped mainly by parent interactions, is what reduces it.

#### How does discipline shape behavior?
- Children model what they see. **Harsh discipline** triggers fear, disrupts emotional regulation, and is linked to ↑ aggression. **Warm, supportive** parenting is linked to ↓ aggression `[Engage]`.
- Hospital stress can bring out atypical behavior. Nurses model a calm, assertive response.
- Teach emotional literacy with **emotion cards or picture books**: name the feeling, then the alternative ("I can ask for help or more playtime"). The adult also has to control their own reaction. `[Engage]`

#### Body image and sexuality: nurse responses?
- Body image follows parents' talk about weight (theirs and the child's) and society's standards. Frame things positively (healthy eating, activity) to help prevent disordered eating. A parent worried their child is "too big" → ask what the child does well and what activities they enjoy `[Engage]`. Ask the child which words to avoid.
- Curiosity about bodies, birth, and sex is normal. **Childhood masturbation** is common at **2–5 yr**: self-exploration, not sexual intent. **Don't react negatively; redirect.** If public, explain privacy at their level. It can signal a **UTI** or stress (new sibling) `[Engage]`.

#### Preschool and kindergarten: what's tested?
- **Development** = natural unfolding; **learning** = skills gained by interacting with the environment. They're related but assessed separately.
- **Self-regulation** (executive functions: inhibitory control, working memory, mental flexibility) becomes more visible in kindergarten. Consider it when behavior problems appear.
- Behavior problems: **external** (aggression) vs. **internal** (isolation, anxiety, withdrawal). School entry brings **separation anxiety** and social anxiety. Unsupported, these can derail emotional regulation, so early detection matters. `[Engage]`

## III. Health promotion and prevention of disease and injury

> [!quote] Anchor
> Parents drive preschool health. The yearly well visit is where the nurse finds gaps and links families to resources.

### Safety and injury prevention

#### Leading causes of death and injury?
- **Unintentional injury** is the leading cause of death in this age group; **75.8%** of injuries in children **≤ 4 yr** happen **at home**, often with parents present `[Engage]`.
- **Motor vehicle crashes** are the leading cause of death **> 4 yr**. **Drowning** is the most common unintentional-injury cause and cause of death **< 4 yr** `[Engage]`.

#### Car seat progression?
Rear-facing to the seat's limit (most convertible seats allow **≥ 2 yr**) → forward-facing **five-point harness** as long as the manufacturer allows → **booster** with belt positioning until the lap/shoulder belt fits (~**4 ft 9 in**, age **9–12**) → seat belt. **Back seat until 13.** `[Engage]`

- Forward-facing fit: harness at or **above** the shoulders (rear-facing: at or **below**), chest clip at **armpit** level, no twists, no pinchable slack `[Engage]`.
- Boosters ↓ nonfatal injury **45%** vs. belts at **4–8 yr** `[Engage]`. Review state laws **annually**. Buy **new** seats (no expired or crashed seats).

#### Poisoning prevention?
- Leading cause: household chemicals and meds that aren't secured. Use child-resistant caps (Poison Prevention Packaging Act, **1970**). If an adult can't use them, store meds out of reach. `[Engage]`
- ==Medications locked and out of reach; Poison Control number easy to find.==

#### Water safety essentials?
- **Close, constant** supervision by someone who can recognize distress and respond. **Never leave a child in another child's care.** Drowning is **silent** and can take ~**1 minute** `[Engage]`.
- Pool fence separating it from house and yard, with a **self-closing, self-latching** gate; phone and rescue gear (buoy, life jackets, shepherd's hook) nearby; **parents learn CPR**; swim and water-safety skills (lessons don't replace supervision). Life jackets when boating; lifeguarded areas. `[Engage]`

#### Trampoline safety?
- **Discouraged < 6 yr** (small size, developing bones → fractures of arms, legs, head, neck). AAP discourages home trampolines `[Engage]`.
- If used: ground level, soft surface, away from structures; pads and net; inspect before **every** use; supervise; **no flips**; jump in the center; sit and slide off; remove the ladder after. `[Engage]`

#### Bicycle safety teaching?
- Tricycle at **3**, then training wheels. Traumatic brain injury is a leading cause of death. Risks: dawn, dusk, or night riding; frequent riding; roads `[Engage]`.
- **Helmet every ride** (CPSC-approved): sits above the eyebrows, chin strap snug, doesn't slide. ↓ head injury **48%**, face **23%**, death/serious injury **34%** `[Engage]`.
- Fit: tricycle wheels **12–13 in**; **foot brakes only**; seated with both feet on the ground, **1 in** clearance over the bar `[Engage]`.
- Ride away from cars, **driveways**, and pools; no street until skilled **and** with an adult. Point families to helmet giveaways. `[Engage]`

### Disease prevention

#### Preschool vaccines and injection site?
- At **4–6 yr**: **DTaP, IPV, MMRV**; **influenza yearly** (IIV injection or LAIV intranasal); **COVID-19** for **≥ 6 mo** (may need > 1 dose if **< 6 yr**) `[Engage]`.
- Site: **deltoid preferred** at **3–10 yr** (usable from **≥ 3 yr**); anterolateral thigh also acceptable. Deltoid landmark: inverted triangle, **1–2 in (2.5–5 cm)** below the shoulder edge of the scapula. **Avoid dorsogluteal** (sciatic nerve, vessels). Ventrogluteal: palm on greater trochanter, index finger on anterior superior iliac spine, inject in the center of the "V." `[Engage]`
- Comfort: distraction, cooling the site, topical analgesia. Address hesitancy with **evidence-based, empathetic** risk/benefit talk.

#### Dental care?
- **Caries** = the most common chronic disease of U.S. childhood. Untreated → pain, infection, speech, eating, and learning problems `[Engage]`.
- Dentist every **6 months** from first tooth; **sealants**; school sealant programs fill access gaps; low SES ↑ risk, so take a social history. Brushing is a **shared** parent–child task until the child is proficient `[Engage]`.

#### Nutrition guidance?
- **1,000–1,800 kcal/day** of nutrient-dense foods, matched to activity. By age and sex: **2–4 yr** F **1,000–1,400**, M **1,000–1,600** · **5–8 yr** F **1,200–1,800**, M **1,200–2,000** `[Engage]`.
- Daily dairy in the healthy U.S. pattern: **2 cups** at 1,000 kcal, **2.5 cups** at 1,200–1,400 `[Engage]`.
- Still picky, but more willing to try. Offer variety repeatedly at the child's pace; culturally appropriate foods. Healthy eating on a budget: fresh, frozen, canned, seasonal, and the **USDA Thrifty Food Plan** `[Engage]`.

#### Sleep needs?
- About **12 hr/day** (module: **11–13 hr**); naps become inconsistent. Poor sleep shows up as aggression, inattention, emotional problems, weaker executive function `[Engage]`.
- Consistent routine; **no screens before bed, electronics out of the bedroom**; cool, quiet room; comfort object; reassure if frightened `[Engage]`.

#### Physical activity and media?
- **≥ 180 min/day**, including **60 min** moderate–vigorous: tag, playground, tricycle or bike, swimming, dancing. **Bone-strengthening**: hop, skip, jump rope, tumble. Muscle-strengthening: tug-of-war, climbing `[Engage]`.
- Barriers: unsafe neighborhoods → ↑ screen time. Suggest indoor obstacle courses, scavenger hunts, family fitness nights, safe parks or pools nearby `[Engage]`.
- Media: **high-quality** content, **co-viewed** with a parent. Ask families how they choose content and set rules `[Engage]`.

## IV. Unexpected findings: neglect and abuse

> [!quote] Anchor
> Nurses are **mandated reporters**. The job is to recognize, document, and report. **Reporting starts an investigation; it doesn't automatically remove the child.**

#### Child neglect: signs and approach?
- Failure to meet basic needs. Signs: disturbed sleep, **malnutrition**, poor hygiene, irritability, attention problems, intrusive thoughts, hopelessness, **fear of parents**, intense distress, withdrawal, poor school performance `[Engage]`.
- Rates are **5×** higher in low-SES families (financial, housing, food, and access stress) `[Engage]`.
- Approach: show concern for the child and offer help. A working parent of a malnourished inpatient → "It must be worrisome having your child away. Is there anything we can do to make things easier at home?" `[Engage]`

#### Levels of abuse prevention?
| Level | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Primary** | Give children skills and info to protect themselves (body rights) |
| **Secondary** | Train professionals to recognize abuse |
| **Tertiary** | Intervene once abuse is recognized |

#### Physical abuse red flags?
| Visible | Radiologic |
|---|---|
| **Bruising** (most common) on chest, abdomen, back, buttocks, GU area, hips, **ears**, neck; accidental bruises sit on **bony prominences** | Unexpected **joint** fractures, **spiral** fracture, **multiple rib** fractures (may turn up on a CXR done for something else) |
| **All bite marks**: compare size to the story | **Scapular** fractures; **complex skull** fractures |
| **Burns** (**5–20%** of abuse): **immersion** = symmetrical, sharp demarcation; **contact** = shape of the object; small circular burns | Fractures in different stages of healing; **bilateral subdural** hemorrhage, **retinal** hemorrhage (abusive head trauma) |

- Skin findings appear in **90%** of physical abuse. Abusive head trauma is the **3rd** leading cause of head trauma **< 5 yr**. Do a **full-body** exam. `[Engage]`

> [!danger] Report immediately
> Red flags → report to **state social services/CPS**, document findings and **direct quotes**, and order or obtain x-rays when bruising isn't over bony prominences. Talk to the family **directly** and without bias: abuse happens in "normal" families too, and it is never the child's fault. Script: *"We are concerned for the safety of your child. Based on what was found today, we are required to report to state services for further investigation."* `[Engage]`

#### FDIA: what is it, and what does the nurse do?
- **Factitious disorder imposed on another** (formerly Munchausen by proxy): a caregiver fabricates or **induces** illness to deceive, even without external reward. The child suffers from the caregiver's actions and from the testing they prompt. **Doctor-shopping** across clinics and EDs delays detection `[Engage]`.
- Falsification types: false history, withheld records, exaggeration, simulated findings (e.g., blood or stool added to urine), neglect, **induction** (emetics, diuretics), **coaching** the child `[Engage]`.
- Nurse: ==**thoroughly document** parent reports and every inconsistency==; interprofessional team; stay unbiased; safety first. `[Engage]`

#### Sexual abuse: prevention and body safety?
- **90%** of reported child sexual abuse is by someone **known and trusted**, so children may not recognize or report it `[Engage]`. Prevalence: **1 in 4** girls, **1 in 20** boys.
- Teach: **boundaries** (may refuse hugs or kisses; offer high-five alternatives), a **support system** of trusted adults, **healthy vs. unhealthy secrets** (surprise party OK; secret touching never), and **private parts** = under the bathing suit, with **correct anatomical names**. `[Engage]`
- A child says a relative told them a secret they'll "get in trouble" for telling → explain that some secrets are OK (surprises) and some aren't, because they can hurt people. **Never promise to keep it secret.** `[Engage]`
- Effects: STIs (treatable ones early; others may appear later, so retest if symptoms), injuries; later depression, anxiety, PTSD, substance use, risky behavior, HPV-related cancers. Early recognition and reporting are the nurse's primary concern `[Engage]`.

#### Verbal and emotional abuse: signs?
- The **most common** form of family violence, and hard to spot because yelling, name-calling, rejection, and threats may be culturally normalized. Includes sibling violence `[Engage]`.
- Signs: behavior change, **speech disorders**, **developmental delay**, withdrawal or fear of doing wrong, extremely passive or aggressive behavior.
- Approach: be direct and factual, and point to the **child's** response so parents can see the harm `[Engage]`.

## V. Nursing process with preschoolers

#### Clinical judgment steps: preschool specifics?
| Step | Preschool emphasis |
|---|---|
| **Recognize cues** | Data comes mostly from **parents**; use questionnaires (EHR); watch nonverbal cues; **validate** subjective with objective data (feels hot → measure temp); separate **cues** from **inferences**; document |
| **Analyze** | Compare to expected development; catch delays parents don't see; consider SES |
| **Prioritize** | **Maslow**: physiologic → safety first |
| **Generate solutions** | SMART goals; interprofessional; unbiased, culturally aware; realistic, small changes |
| **Take action** | Fit to developmental level; positive reinforcement; resources; document |
| **Evaluate** | Within a set time frame; yearly if no concerns, sooner if concerns; revise the plan; report if child safety is at risk |

> [!example] Worked example — cases from the module
> - **Marcus, 4:** **88.9 → 100 cm** and **16.3 → 19 kg** in a year; always **toe-walks**; two-foot jump only just emerging; diet of nuggets, mac and cheese, waffles, milk; family can't afford produce. Priority = **physiologic (food access)** → Thrifty Food Plan and resources, activity ideas, **PT/OT referral if concerns persist**, timed re-evaluation.
> - **Sam, 5:** new **stutter** + aggression toward siblings after starting **full-day kindergarten** and grandparent moving to a nursing home (safety need: order and predictability). Plan: adjustment-period teaching, emotional regulation, discipline guidance, speech eval, follow-up in **3 months**. At 3 months aggression is down but the stutter persists → **speech therapy**, review the prior eval, keep following up.
> - **Monica, 3:** **3 cups** of milk + yogurt daily, **1 hard stool/week** with straining, wakes and goes to parents' bed, noise machine on. Plan: swap **1** milk for water, monitor stools, try removing the noise machine, **schedule follow-up**.
> - **Juan, 4:** pool gate latch failing, rides in the **driveway without a helmet**, never seen a dentist. Needs = **safety** (Maslow). Fix the latch, helmet every ride, car-seat check, dentist list; evaluate all of it at follow-up.
> - **Raya, 3:** unkempt, small clothes, withdrawn, bruises on face, arms, legs, **abdomen, buttocks**; won't walk on her leg; no consistent story → **report to CPS**, x-ray (**spiral femur fracture**). Priority = **bruising/abuse evidence** over appearance. `[Engage: Applying the Nursing Process]`

## Self-quiz

> [!question]- 1. A child was 40 in at the 4-year visit. What height do you expect at 5?
> About **43 in** (~3 in per year). No change would need follow-up.

> [!question]- 2. Which gross motor skills are age-appropriate for a preschooler?
> Overhand throw, smooth running and jumping, alternating feet on stairs, hopping and skipping on one foot. Needing support to balance is an earlier-stage finding.

> [!question]- 3. How many body parts should a 5-year-old's drawing of a person have?
> **Six** (three at age 4).

> [!question]- 4. A child says the dog's barking made the balloon pop. Which concept is this?
> **Transductive reasoning.**

> [!question]- 5. A child calls a cow a dog, then learns it's a cow. Which two processes happened, in order?
> **Assimilation**, then **accommodation**.

> [!question]- 6. What is Erikson's preschool stage, and how does a nurse support it during med administration?
> **Initiative vs. guilt.** Let the child take the med by holding the cup or oral syringe, and answer their questions seriously.

> [!question]- 7. A 4-year-old believes he got sick because he was mean to his sister. What concept is this, and what do you do?
> **Magical thinking.** Ask what they think caused the illness and correct the self-blame.

> [!question]- 8. You're preparing a 4-year-old for a PIV. Which actions are correct?
> Let the child **open the antiseptic swab**, keep the parent present, don't show the needle, and say "poke," not "bee sting."

> [!question]- 9. A 4-year-old says, "I hungry." Is this expected?
> No, it suggests a **language delay**. By 4, sentences should have more than 4 words.

> [!question]- 10. Which aggression type is threatening the "silent treatment"?
> **Relational** (indirect) aggression.

> [!question]- 11. By what age should aggression decline significantly?
> By **5** (it rises through age 3).

> [!question]- 12. A parent is worried that their 4-year-old touches their genitals. What is the best response?
> "Childhood masturbation is a normal occurrence at this age." Then teach redirection and privacy; no punishment.

> [!question]- 13. A child has outgrown a forward-facing harness seat. What's next, and until when?
> A **booster seat** until the belt fits (~**4 ft 9 in**, age **9–12**). Back seat until **13**.

> [!question]- 14. A family asks about a backyard trampoline for their 5-year-old. What do you advise?
> Use is discouraged under **6**, and the AAP discourages home trampolines. If they use one anyway: net, pads, supervision, no flips, one jumper in the center.

> [!question]- 15. Which vaccines are due at the 4–6-year visit?
> **DTaP, IPV, MMRV**, plus yearly **influenza**. Hepatitis B isn't one of them.

> [!question]- 16. What is the preferred IM site for a 4-year-old, and which site should be avoided?
> **Deltoid** (preferred **3–10 yr**). Avoid the **dorsogluteal** site.

> [!question]- 17. What sleep and activity totals does a preschooler need?
> Sleep ~**12 hr/day** (11–13). Activity **≥ 180 min**, with **60 min** moderate–vigorous.

> [!question]- 18. A 4-year-old has bruises on the ears and abdomen and a vague "he falls a lot" story. Accidental or red flag, and what's next?
> **Red flag**: accidental bruises sit on bony prominences. Report to state services and document findings and direct quotes.

> [!question]- 19. A child has six office visits in two months plus visits to other hospitals, and the parent says no one will help. What is the nurse's action?
> **Thoroughly document** parent reports, concerns, and assessments (possible FDIA).

> [!question]- 20. A mother asks why CPS was called. What is the best response?
> "We are concerned for the safety of your child. Based on what was found, we are required to report to state services for **further investigation**."

> [!question]- 21. Who commits most child sexual abuse?
> Someone **known and trusted** by the child or parents (**90%**).
