## I. Biological development

> [!quote] Anchor
> Adolescence bridges dependence and independence: a growth spurt and sexual maturation outside, abstract thought and identity formation inside.

### Physical development and puberty

#### When does puberty start, and what drives it?
- Onset varies with **genetics, nutrition, activity, environment**, and culture. Females **8–13**, males **9–14** `[Engage]`.
- **GnRH** starts the hormonal cascade for sexual maturity and fertility `[Engage]`.
- **Adrenarche** (**6–9 yr**): HPA axis ↑ adrenal androgens (DHEA) → pubic and axillary hair. **Gonadarche** (**9–11 yr**): HPG axis reactivates → sex steroids → breast and genital development `[Engage]`.

#### Pubertal changes by sex?
| Females | Males |
|---|---|
| Breast development (first sign) | Testicular enlargement, then penile |
| Pubic and axillary hair | Pubic, axillary, facial hair |
| ==Menarche **12–14**==; growth stops **2–2.5 yr** later | Voice changes; wider shoulders, ↑ muscle |
| Hands/feet grow faster; wider hips; ↑ body fat | Erections and ejaculation (voluntary and involuntary); grow until **18–20** |

- Both: growth spurt **4–6 in/yr**, ↑ muscle and bone density, body odor, **acne**. Reassure: ==everyone goes through these changes==.
- **Pubertal stages** are rated with **Tanner (sexual maturity rating) stages 1–5** for breasts/genitals and pubic hair; 1 = prepubertal, 5 = adult `[ADDED]`.

#### Delayed puberty: findings and action?
- Often from **poor nutrition** or chronic illness: stunted growth, short stature, **low BMI**, late menarche or secondary sex characteristics `[Engage]`.
- Delayed = no breast development by **13** (F) or no testicular enlargement by **14** (M) `[ADDED — verify]`.
- → plan of care, ==refer to specialist / pediatric endocrinologist==; support groups.

### Motor skills

#### Gross vs. fine motor in adolescence?
| | Gross motor | Fine motor |
|---|---|---|
| Uses | Large muscles: running, jumping, throwing, kicking | Hands/fingers: writing, drawing, **musical instruments**, hand-eye coordination |
| Influences | Growth spurt → strength and coordination but ==temporary clumsiness==; hormones, confidence | Genetics, SES, education, technology; practice |
| Promote | Regular activity: team/individual sports, dance, martial arts | Precision activities |
| Problem → refer | **Neurologist** `[Engage]` | **Neurologist + occupational therapist** `[Engage]` |

### Psychosocial and cognitive development

#### What makes adolescent psychosocial development complex?
- Hormonal, neurological, and emotional change; ==emotional self-regulation is not yet mature== → heightened reactions to real or perceived stress `[Engage]`.
- **Peer acceptance or rejection** shapes self-concept and self-worth; drive for **independence** from parents; **identity exploration**.
- **Identity formation** = Erikson's **identity vs. role confusion**: the adolescent builds a coherent sense of self (values, roles, sexuality, vocation); failure → confusion about who they are `[ADDED]`. Positive identity ↔ good mental health; identity crises ↔ distress `[Engage]`.
- Some **risk-taking is expected** and necessary for growth; excessive or reckless risk-taking is a concern `[Engage]`.
- Nurse supports positive identity, healthy relationships, coping strategies; strong support system is protective.

#### Cognitive changes?
- **Piaget** `[Engage]`: abstract thinking, logical reasoning, morals. This is the **formal operational** stage (~11/12+): hypothetical, deductive reasoning beyond concrete experience `[ADDED]`.
- **Metacognition**: thinking about one's own thinking; monitors and adjusts strategies.
- Better problem-solving, weighing pros/cons, considering long-term consequences; ↑ attention and multitasking; cognitive flexibility. Linked to **prefrontal cortex** and reward-system maturation `[Engage]`.

#### Language and play?
- Language becomes **abstract**: metaphor, analogy, storytelling, larger vocabulary, complex syntax; frontal-lobe maturation **11–18 yr** `[Engage]`. Encourage reading and writing.
- Play still matters: **sports** (→ active adult lifestyle), clubs, community groups.
- ==Hospitalized or isolated adolescent: keep peer contact== via social media/video visits; bring schoolwork `[Engage case]`.

## II. Adolescent behaviors

### Self-esteem

#### What drives adolescent self-esteem?
- Very responsive to others' opinions → self-esteem **fluctuates** with praise or rejection. Influences: parents, peers, significant adults, **appearance**, academics, fitting social norms.
- Low self-esteem → ↑ **depression, anxiety**, ↓ motivation, poorer grades, trouble with relationships `[Engage]`.

#### Nursing approach?
- Positive feedback, **constructive** criticism, chances to develop talents. Teach coping: reframe negative thoughts, seek trusted adults.
- **Therapeutic communication:** ==open-ended questions== + **reflective responses** (paraphrase, restate, reflect emotion) to build self-awareness.

> [!tip] NCLEX tip
> Adolescent with negative self-talk, withdrawn: ==first assess== self-esteem with open-ended questions and observation. Wrong: "it's common and will resolve," encouraging time alone `[Engage practice item]`.

### Sexuality

#### What does the nurse need to know?
- Puberty brings sexual attraction and desire; many are **uncertain** about attractions; self-labels (gay, straight, bisexual) may not match behavior. Influences: biology, psychology, social and cultural factors, **social media**, peers, SES.
- Assess orientation and behavior ==without preconceived notions==; let adolescents **choose their own labels** via open-ended questions `[Engage]`.
- Give accurate, comprehensive info: sexual health, **contraception, consent**. Safe, nonjudgmental space; nurses must examine their own **biases and discomfort** `[Engage]`.

### Social interactions and perspective on health

#### Why do social interactions matter?
- Intense need for peer acceptance; quality peer interaction → ↓ stress and depression, ↑ resilience; ↓ loneliness.
- Challenges: social anxiety, **peer pressure**, conflict. Active listening applies online and offline.
- Nurse/parents: provide social opportunities, model behavior, guide on **setting boundaries** and **seeking help**.

#### How do adolescents see health?
- Shaped by physical, emotional, social factors, culture; **media** shapes beauty standards, diets, and ==vaccine views (HPV, COVID-19)== → educate on immunization `[Engage]`.
- Promote a **holistic** view: activity, balanced diet, emotional support, avoiding risky behaviors. ==Involve them in their care and invite questions==; recognize strengths; constructive feedback.
- Resources `[Engage]`: Planned Parenthood, KidsHealth.org, HealthyChildren.org.

### High-risk behaviors

#### Which behaviors and risk factors?
- Substance use (alcohol, tobacco, drugs), **unprotected sex**, **reckless driving**.
- Risk factors `[Engage]`: **peer pressure**, low parental involvement/supervision, **underlying mental health issues**, **impulsivity**, lack of knowledge.

#### Nursing role?
- Education on consequences; promote positive alternatives (sports, hobbies, creative outlets).
- ==Routine screening==: substance use, mental health, risky sex, self-harm → early intervention and referral.

> [!example] Worked example `[Engage case]`
> 16-yr-old drank under peer pressure and crashed driving friends home. Assess **guardian involvement**, knowledge of alcohol risks, **mental health**, **impulsivity**. Best intervention: ==open communication + active listening== to understand his view, not strict rules or avoiding all social situations.

## III. Health promotion and disease prevention

#### Safety and injury prevention teaching?
- **MVCs are a leading cause of injury and death** → ==always wear a seat belt; no cell phone while driving== `[Engage]`.
- **Helmets and protective gear** in sports/recreation. Safe sexual practices (STIs, pregnancy), mental health support, substance-use education.
- Social health: volunteering, positive relationships with peers and adults.

#### Health promotion vs. disease prevention?
| Health promotion | Disease prevention |
|---|---|
| Regular activity, balanced diet, adequate sleep, **seat belt** use | **Vaccines**, regular check-ups, screening for **STIs** and mental health; sexual health services |

`[Engage practice item]`

#### Which vaccines are due?
- Named: **HPV, Tdap, meningococcal**; catch-up as needed `[Engage]`. Typical timing `[ADDED — verify vs course materials]`: Tdap **11–12**; MenACWY **11–12** + booster **16**; HPV series from **11–12** (2 doses if started before 15, else 3).

### Nutrition

#### Key nutrients and why?
| Nutrient | Purpose `[Engage]` |
|---|---|
| ==**Calcium**== | Bone and teeth growth; ↓ later **osteoporosis** |
| Iron | RBC production; prevents **anemia** |
| Protein | Muscle and bone growth, repair |
| Vitamin C | Immune function |
| Sodium | Needed, but excess → ↑ **BP** |

- Balanced diet: fruits, vegetables, whole grains, lean protein, **low-fat dairy**; limit processed snacks, sugary drinks, **fast food** (calorie-dense, nutrient-poor → deficiencies, obesity, T2DM, HTN, mood problems).
- **Low SES** → cost barrier to fresh food → address barriers. Teach **label reading** and meal prep.

#### Eating disorder warning signs?
- **Rapid weight change**, obsession with weight/shape ("fat" talk), restriction or skipped meals, eating alone/excuses, ==secrecy and food hoarding==.
- Physical: fatigue, dizziness, fainting, **feeling cold**, ==**lanugo**==, brittle nails, dry skin, **thinning hair** `[Engage]`.
- Suspected → pediatrician, psychologist, or psychiatrist; early intervention = better recovery.

### Sleep and activity

#### Sleep: how much, and what disrupts it?
- ==**8–10 hr/night**== `[Engage]`; many fall short. Puberty hormones (melatonin, GH, cortisol, sex hormones, thyroid) **shift the sleep-wake cycle later**; early school start, activities, devices.
- Poor sleep → ↓ grades, depression, anxiety, ↓ immunity, poor decisions, **↑ substance use and unprotected sex**.
- Teach: regular routine, conducive environment, **relaxation/meditation**, ==limit devices before bed==, no caffeine or large meals near bedtime, daytime activity. Not: TV at bedtime, benzodiazepines `[Engage practice item]`.

#### Activity?
- Daily, **~1 hr** heart-pumping exercise (WHO) `[Engage]`: ↑ cardiorespiratory function, strength, bone health; ↓ emotional and metabolic disease risk.
- Technology → sedentary behavior and isolation. PE builds emotional intelligence. ==Include adolescents with disabilities==, tailored under medical guidance.

## IV. Health concerns — reproductive and physical

### Hypertension

#### Definition, risks, and prevention?
- Adolescent HTN = ==**≥ 130/80 mm Hg**== `[Engage]`; rising with obesity. Leads to heart disease, stroke, kidney failure.
- Risks: **family history, obesity**, inactivity, sodium **> 2,400 mg**. Screen routinely, especially with obesity or family history.
- Lifestyle first: ideal weight, regular activity, **sodium < 2,300 mg/day**, limit processed foods, read labels, **no smoking or alcohol**. Then meds: **ACE-I, ARB, CCB** `[Engage]`.

#### Lisinopril?
| | |
|---|---|
| Class / action | **ACE inhibitor**: blocks angiotensin I → II (vasoconstrictor); suppresses RAAS |
| Adverse effects | Fatigue, dizziness, headache, ==**dry cough**==, GI upset, **hypotension**, ==**hyperkalemia**== |
| Contraindications | Severe hyperkalemia, history of **angioedema**, ==**pregnancy**==, renal artery stenosis; aliskiren in diabetes; sacubitril/valsartan |
| Interactions | Diuretics/antihypertensives (↑ hypotension); K+ supplements, K-sparing diuretics, **salt substitutes** (↑ K+); **NSAIDs** (↓ effect, ↑ kidney injury) |
| Teaching | **No salt substitutes**, no NSAIDs, no alcohol; report chronic dry cough. Course: avoid potassium-rich foods (bananas, leafy greens) [NOTE: ACE-Is retain K+ by ↓ aldosterone; they are not diuretics. Verify the diet teaching vs course materials.] |

> [!danger] Report immediately
> Swelling of face, lips, tongue, or throat on an ACE-I = **angioedema** → airway emergency `[ADDED]`. Sexually active female → ==pregnancy test and contraception counseling==; ACE-Is are teratogenic `[ADDED]`.

### Male reproductive concerns

#### Testicular torsion?
- Spermatic cord **twists** → blood supply cut off; peak **12–18 yr**. ==Sudden, severe testicular pain==, swelling, discoloration `[Engage]`.

> [!danger] Report immediately
> ==Medical emergency==: seek care immediately. Surgery untwists the cord (orchiectomy if too damaged). Testicular salvage is best within ~**6 hr** of onset `[ADDED — verify vs course materials]`.

#### Penile disorders and gynecomastia?
- Common penile/scrotal problems `[Engage]`: lichen sclerosus of the foreskin (chronic inflammatory skin thinning/scarring `[ADDED]`), **varicocele** (dilated scrotal veins, "bag of worms" `[ADDED]`), undescended testes, epididymal cysts/**hydrocele** (fluid around the testis `[ADDED]`), penile anomalies → genital hygiene; seek evaluation for any concern.
- **Gynecomastia**: male breast tissue swelling, common in puberty (hormonal), usually ==resolves on its own==. Drug causes: **amphetamines, heroin, methadone**, antihypertensives, cardiac and psychoactive drugs. Treatment: androgens, anti-estrogens (tamoxifen, clomiphene), aromatase inhibitors, surgery `[Engage]`. Address **embarrassment/emotional distress**.

### Menstrual disorders

#### Common menstrual disorders?
| Disorder | What it is | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ==**Dysmenorrhea**== | Painful periods: cramps, N/V, fatigue | Causes: hormones, fibroids, endometriosis. Missed school, ↓ activities, sleep loss, irritability, anxiety/depression. First-line: **NSAIDs**, heat `[ADDED]` |
| **Menorrhagia** | Heavy/prolonged bleeding, clots, fatigue | Hormonal or structural cause |
| **Amenorrhea** | Absent periods; disrupted **HPO axis** (GnRH → LH/FSH) | Primary = no menarche by **15** `[ADDED — verify]` |

- Treatment: hormonal birth control, pain relievers, surgery if severe; exercise and balanced diet. ==Seek care for any menstrual concern.==

#### Endometriosis?
- Chronic, inflammatory, systemic, cycle-driven condition: **severe dysmenorrhea**, pain with sex, chronic pelvic pain, infertility `[Engage]`.
- Definitive diagnosis = **laparoscopy**, rarely done in adolescents (observation favored).
- Big psychosocial burden: missed school, **anxiety, depression**, substance use, fertility worries. Treatment: pain meds, hormones, surgery.

#### PMS vs. PMDD?
| | PMS | PMDD |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | Luteal phase; ==resolves once menses starts== | Same |
| Findings | Breast tenderness, **bloating**, appetite change | **Depression, irritability, anxiety**, aggression, mood swings, overwhelmed |
| Severity | Milder | Severe; ==may need meds== |

- Treatment: **SSRIs**, OTC pain relievers, birth control pills, exercise, stress reduction. Severe symptoms → provider.

### Vaginal infections and STIs

#### BV vs. yeast infection?
| | Bacterial vaginosis | Yeast (Candida) |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Bacterial imbalance → overgrowth | Candida overgrowth |
| Findings | Itching, burning, ==foul/fishy== thin gray discharge `[ADDED for discharge]` | Itching, burning; thick white curd-like discharge `[ADDED]` |
| Treatment | **Metronidazole** PO or gel **0.75%**, or clindamycin **2%** cream HS × **7 days**; alternatives secnidazole, tinidazole, clindamycin PO/ovules `[Engage]` | Antifungal |

- Prevention: hygiene, **clean breathable clothing**, condoms; regular gyn exams and STI testing.
- Metronidazole: ==**no alcohol**== during therapy and for ~**3 days** after (disulfiram-like reaction) `[ADDED — verify]`.

#### Common STIs?
| STI | Findings | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Chlamydia, gonorrhea** | ==Vaginal discharge, dysuria==, abdominal pain; often asymptomatic `[ADDED]` | Antibiotics |
| **Herpes** | **Painful** genital sores, flu-like symptoms | ==Not curable==; antivirals control outbreaks |
| **Syphilis** | **Painless chancre**, then rash, fever, swollen nodes | Treatable; untreated → long-term damage |
| **HPV** | Genital warts; ==cervical/anal cancer== | **Vaccine** 11–12 (9–26) |

- Risk: sexual activity patterns, low knowledge, reluctance to seek care, needle drug use → test (urine, blood, swab).
- ==Don't assume school sex ed covered STIs== (most states don't teach STI prevention or barrier methods `[Engage]`). Address **shame, guilt, embarrassment**; offer support.
- Treat partners and abstain until treatment is complete `[ADDED]`.

> [!tip] NCLEX tip
> 15-yr-old female, discharge + burning on urination → most likely ==chlamydia== `[Engage practice item]`.

## V. Health concerns — sexual health and mental health

### Contraception and pregnancy

#### Contraceptive options?
- **Hormonal:** pills, patch, injection, ring. **Barrier:** condoms, diaphragm, cervical cap. **LARC:** IUDs, implants; most effective, protect for years `[Engage]`.
- Open, **nonjudgmental**, ==assess knowledge and tailor info== (better than handing over everything or written materials only) `[Engage practice item]`. Encourage consistent use, **condoms for STIs**, regular STI testing.
- Confidentiality `[Engage]`: some states allow contraception **without parental consent**; STI testing need not be disclosed to guardians; ==some positive STI results must be reported to the health department==.

#### Levonorgestrel (Plan B, Aftera)?
| | |
|---|---|
| Class / action | Progestin; delays/inhibits ovulation, alters cervical mucus `[Engage]` |
| Use | After unprotected sex or contraceptive failure; ==does not end an existing pregnancy== |
| Adverse effects | Menstrual irregularity, nausea/vomiting, lower abdominal pain, fatigue, dizziness, breast tenderness, headache |
| Contraindication | Already pregnant |
| Teaching | ==Within **72 hr**== (moderate effect to **5 days**); the sooner the better · ==not regular birth control== · **no STI/HIV protection** · barrier or abstinence for **7 days** after · can be used any time in the cycle, more than once · period late **> 7 days**, persistent lower abdominal pain, or irregular bleeding → provider |

- [NOTE: Engage also says it may prevent implantation; current evidence: it works mainly by delaying ovulation and does not prevent implantation.]

#### Adolescent pregnancy?
- ↑ risk of ==preterm birth, low birth weight, high BP==; stigma, isolation, economic hardship `[Engage]`.
- Barriers to prenatal care: awareness, support, money, transportation, fear, provider availability → specialized prenatal care, nutrition, emotional support, help continuing school.
- Legal: adolescent parents are responsible for their child; in some states they can **consent for their child but not for themselves** `[Engage]`.

#### Abortion?
- **Induced:** medication or procedure (suction aspiration, D&C). **Spontaneous** (miscarriage): before **20 wk**, often 1st trimester; bleeding, cramping, passage of tissue `[Engage]`.
- ==Nonjudgmental==, accurate information; support informed decisions. Care aligned with preferences → better mental well-being.

### Rape

#### Nursing responsibilities after sexual assault?
- Adolescents have ==the highest risk of sexual assault of any age group== `[Engage]`. Consequences: injury, **PTSD**, depression, anxiety, isolation, stigma.
- Medical care, counseling, family support, legal support (reporting). Many don't disclose → watch for signs.
- Ideally a forensic (SANE) exam before bathing or changing clothes; offer emergency contraception and STI prophylaxis `[ADDED — verify]`.
- Prevention: healthy relationships, **consent**, boundaries, respect.

> [!danger] Report immediately
> ==All nurses are mandated reporters== of sexual assault, violence, and abuse `[Engage]`.

### Eating disorders

#### Anorexia vs. bulimia vs. binge eating?
| | Anorexia nervosa | Bulimia nervosa | Binge eating disorder |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core | Restriction, fear of weight gain, distorted image `[ADDED]` | **Binge + compensation** (vomiting, intense exercise) `[Engage]` | Binges **without** purging; may progress to purging `[Engage]` |
| Body | Low weight; **bradycardia, hypotension, hypothermia, amenorrhea**, lanugo `[ADDED for VS]` | Often normal weight; **hypokalemia**, dental erosion, knuckle calluses `[ADDED]` | Weight gain |
| Triggers | Emotional, socioemotional concerns | — | Hunger, interpersonal stress, negative emotions, weight preoccupation |

- Males may pursue **muscularity** → muscle dysmorphia `[Engage]`. Often comorbid **depression, anxiety, substance use**.
- Assess **vital signs and BMI**, body image, self-esteem. Treatment: interprofessional (individual + **family therapy**, PCP, **nutritionist**, ± meds); refer to child/adolescent psychiatrist. ==Early detection = better outcomes.==
- Prevention: positive body image; activity for **health, not weight loss**.

### Depression and anxiety

#### Depression: signs and treatment?
- Persistent **sadness, hopelessness, anhedonia** → affects social life, school, well-being.
- **Therapy + medication**: CBT (eliminates unwanted thoughts and acts) + **SSRI**, commonly ==**fluoxetine**==.

#### Fluoxetine?
| | |
|---|---|
| Class / action | SSRI: blocks serotonin reuptake → ↑ synaptic serotonin |
| Uses | MDD, OCD, panic disorder, **bulimia**, **PMDD** |
| Adverse effects | Nausea, diarrhea, headache, nervousness, **insomnia** or drowsiness, sweating, BP changes, **QT prolongation**, heavy menstrual bleeding, sexual dysfunction, ==**suicidal ideation**==, seizures, ==**serotonin syndrome**== |
| Interactions | **MAOIs**, other SSRIs/SNRIs, TCAs, benzodiazepines, **warfarin/NSAIDs** (bleeding), ==**St. John's wort**== |
| Teaching | ==Don't stop abruptly== · take **in the morning**, with or without food · report suicidal thoughts, palpitations, ↑ pulse, sweating, rigidity, mood/personality change |

> [!warning] Priority
> Antidepressants carry a **boxed warning** for ↑ suicidal thinking in children and young adults, highest early in therapy and after dose changes → close monitoring `[ADDED]`. **Serotonin syndrome**: agitation, tachycardia, hyperthermia, diaphoresis, tremor/hyperreflexia → hold and notify `[ADDED]`.

#### Anxiety: how does it present?
- Excessive worry (school, social, family, future) out of proportion; **somatic** (headache, stomachache, muscle tension, restlessness) → may be mistaken for medical illness.
- Sleep problems, nightmares; **avoidance** → isolation, school avoidance, ↓ grades; irritability, mood swings; **perfectionism**; social anxiety (blushing, trembling); **panic attacks** (racing heart, SOB); negative self-talk.
- Treatment: **psychotherapy/CBT** (replace negative thought patterns); SSRIs ==only in combination with therapy== and under supervision; individualized plan.

### Substance use

#### Warning signs and treatment sequence?
- Risks: early exposure, peer pressure, stress, easy access. Harms: SUD, toxicity, school, relationships, career.
- **Signs:** behavior change, ==mood swings, irritability==, sleep/eating changes, **declining grades**, withdrawal from family/friends; smell of substances, **bloodshot eyes, slurred speech** `[Engage]`.
- ==Treatment order==: **1** assessment/evaluation → **2** detoxification → **3** rehabilitation + counseling → **4** aftercare/relapse prevention (may be lifelong) `[Engage]`.
- Prevention: education, screening, early intervention.

### Self-harm and suicide

#### Risk factors and warning signs?
- Suicide is ==a leading cause of adolescent death== in the U.S.
- **Risks** `[Engage]`: mental illness (depression, bipolar, anxiety), **substance use**, family conflict or trauma, **bullying**, social isolation, academic/peer pressure.
- **Warning signs** `[ADDED]`: talking about death or wanting to die, hopelessness, giving away belongings, withdrawal, sudden calm after depression, self-injury.
- **Self-harm** (cutting, burning) signals overwhelming emotional distress; teens may not see its severity.

> [!danger] Report immediately
> Ask about self-harm and suicide ==directly== `[Engage]`; asking does not plant the idea `[ADDED]`. Any "I have thought about it" → ask about **plan and means**, keep the adolescent safe (don't leave alone), and **notify the provider**; refer to mental health `[ADDED for plan/means/safety]`. ==Suicide risk is the priority== over other findings `[Engage case]`.

## VI. Clinical judgment (nursing process)

#### Communication and exam approach?
- At this age ==**the adolescent is the main source of information**==; parents add insight `[Engage]`. Build **trust**; open, nonjudgmental conversation; open-ended questions.
- Interview the adolescent **privately** for part of the visit and explain confidentiality limits (risk of harm to self/others, abuse) `[ADDED]`.
- Ask about **family dynamics, social environment, trauma/abuse history**; don't avoid trauma questions `[Engage practice item]`.

#### Five clinical judgment domains: key points?
| Domain | Assess | Prioritize / act |
|---|---|---|
| **Biological development** | Growth, sexual maturation, pubertal stage, VS, BMI, family history, nutrition, chronic illness; ask about puberty (LMP, eating, sleep, school) | ==Treat the underlying chronic condition/nutrition deficit first==; endocrinology referral, support groups; involve teen + parents so goals are achievable |
| **Behavioral development** | Substance use, risk-taking, sexual activity (age at first sex, contraception, STIs), **self-harm/SI**, eating disorders (VS, BMI), **bullying/cyberbullying** | ==Immediate safety risks to self/others first==; measurable goals, interprofessional team (mental health, social work), coping skills |
| **Health promotion** | Height, weight, VS, nutrition, activity, **immunizations**, substance use, sexual health, emotional health, family/social support, school, **future goals** | Risks with immediate or long-term impact (e.g., obesity → HTN); nutrition, activity, vaccines, counseling |
| **Injury & disease prevention** | Lifestyle, peers, family, SES, community resources | Education matched to developmental stage and culture: seat belts, **no texting while driving**, safe sex, vaccines (HPV, Tdap, MenACWY) |
| **Health concerns** | Therapeutic relationship; physical + psychosocial + emotional; growth, puberty, chronic illness | Urgent/severe first; integrate mental health care; evidence-based interventions; evaluate vs. teen and family goals |

- **Evaluate:** objective measures + self-report + feedback; goal not met → reassess, modify plan; keep it **realistic** for the adolescent.

> [!example] Worked example `[Engage case]`

## Self-quiz

> [!question]- 1. When does puberty usually start in each sex, and what's the first sign in each?
> Females 8–13 (breast budding); males 9–14 (testicular enlargement).

> [!question]- 2. A caregiver asks when growth stops. What does the nurse say?
> Females ~2–2.5 years after menarche (menarche 12–14); males around 18–20.

> [!question]- 3. Which statements about adolescence are correct: concrete problem-solving, risk-taking is expected, growth slower than school age, males grow to 18–20, emotional regulation mastered?
> Risk-taking is expected and necessary for growth; males grow until 18–20.

> [!question]- 4. Hospitalized 14-yr-old after appendectomy: which psychosocial interventions?
> Bring schoolwork, video visits/social media with peers, chaplain if desired.

> [!question]- 5. Priority action for a withdrawn 16-yr-old with negative self-talk?
> Assess self-esteem with open-ended questions and observation.

> [!question]- 6. Best intervention to help a teen handle peer pressure?
> Open communication and active listening to understand their perspective.

> [!question]- 7. Which nutrient best supports bone and tooth growth?
> Calcium.

> [!question]- 8. Parent suspects an eating disorder. What should they watch for?
> Eating meals alone/in room, food hoarding, feeling cold all the time, rapid weight change, lanugo, thinning hair.

> [!question]- 9. Teen can't sleep. Which suggestions are appropriate?
> Daytime physical activity and relaxation techniques. Not TV at bedtime, benzodiazepines, or big meals before bed.

> [!question]- 10. What BP defines adolescent hypertension, and what's the sodium target?
> ≥ 130/80 mm Hg; sodium < 2,300 mg/day.

> [!question]- 11. A teen on lisinopril asks about salt substitutes and ibuprofen. Teaching?
> Avoid both: salt substitutes contain potassium (hyperkalemia); NSAIDs reduce effect and harm kidneys. Report dry cough or facial/tongue swelling.

> [!question]- 12. 15-yr-old male with sudden severe scrotal pain and swelling. Action?
> Testicular torsion, a surgical emergency: immediate medical care.

> [!question]- 13. PMS vs. PMDD?
> Both luteal-phase, ending with menses. PMS = milder, physical (bloating, breast tenderness). PMDD = severe mood symptoms; may need SSRIs.

> [!question]- 14. Painless genital sore vs. painful genital sores?
> Painless chancre = syphilis; painful sores = herpes (incurable, antivirals for outbreaks).

> [!question]- 15. When is the HPV vaccine given?
> Routinely at 11–12; can start at 9 and catch up through 26.

> [!question]- 16. Condom broke 2 days ago; levonorgestrel prescribed. Correct teaching?
> Take within 72 hr; period should come within 7 days of expected (else see provider); use barrier/abstinence 7 days after; no STI protection; can be used more than once a cycle.

> [!question]- 17. A 16-yr-old worries about privacy for birth control and STI testing. Accurate statements?
> Some positive STI results must be reported to the health department; guardians need not be told about STI testing; consent rules for contraception vary by state.

> [!question]- 18. Pregnant 15-yr-old: which complications is she at risk for?
> Preterm birth, low birth weight, high blood pressure.

> [!question]- 19. Fluoxetine teaching: which statements are correct?
> May cause nausea and drowsiness/insomnia; take in the morning with or without food; avoid St. John's wort; report palpitations and suicidal thoughts; don't stop abruptly.

> [!question]- 20. Order the stages of substance use treatment.
> Assessment/evaluation → detoxification → rehabilitation/counseling → aftercare/relapse prevention.

> [!question]- 21. A teacher smells alcohol on a student's breath. Signs of substance use to teach teachers?
> Behavior change, irritability, mood swings, speech changes, declining grades, withdrawal.

> [!question]- 22. An adolescent says, "I have thought about suicide." Priority?
> Ask about plan and means, keep them safe (don't leave alone), notify the provider, refer to mental health.

> [!question]- 23. Which findings indicate improvement in the T1DM teen on an SSRI?
> No suicidal thoughts and A1C down to 7.2%. School refusal and flat affect show ongoing concern.

> [!question]- 24. Who does the nurse collaborate with on an adolescent plan of care?
> The adolescent, guardians, and the interdisciplinary team.
