## I. Brain and neurotransmitters

> [!quote] Anchor
> Neurotransmitters cross the synapse and act at receptor sites. Most psych meds work by changing how much of a neurotransmitter is available or how strongly it acts.

#### How does neurotransmission work?
- **Neurons** process information, from breathing to complex thinking. Neurotransmission = electrochemical communication between neurons.
- Neurotransmitters cross the **synapse** and bind **receptor sites** (key and lock), exciting or inhibiting the next cell.
- After signaling: ==recycled by **reuptake**== or ==broken down by enzymes (**monoamine oxidase**)==. Many drugs target these two steps.

#### Major neurotransmitters?
| Neurotransmitter | Role | Drug link |
|---|---|---|
| **Dopamine** (excitatory) | Reward/pleasure, cognition, movement, emotion | ==Excess linked with psychosis== → antipsychotics block it |
| **Serotonin** (inhibitory `[Engage]`) | Mood, sleep, pain, temperature, sexual function; **> 14** receptor types `[Engage]` | SSRIs block its reuptake |
| **Norepinephrine** (excitatory) | Attention, memory, mood, learning, stress response | SNRIs/TCAs block its reuptake; trauma → ↑ NE `[Engage]` |
| **Histamine** | Alertness, wakefulness | ==Blockade → sedation, weight gain== |
| ==**GABA**== | Primary **inhibitory** ("brakes") | Benzodiazepines ↑ GABA → ↓ anxiety, sleep |
| ==**Glutamate**== | Major **excitatory** ("accelerator"); excess injures neurons (stroke, head trauma) | ↑ in alcohol withdrawal; high levels may precede psychosis `[Engage]` |
| **Acetylcholine** (both) | Muscle function, sleep-wake cycle; ==↓ in Alzheimer disease== | Blocking it → ==anticholinergic effects== |

## II. Neurobiological causes of mental illness

#### What causes mental illness?
- ==Multiple contributing factors==, not one cause: genetic vulnerability interacting with stress, trauma, environment, other biologic influences.
- ==Keep theories and associations separate from proven causation.==

#### Genetics and pharmacogenomics?
- **Family, twin, adoption** studies show a meaningful inherited component. E.g., a biological child of a parent with addiction is more susceptible than an adopted child in the same home `[Engage]`.
- Most conditions have no single predictive gene (early-onset Alzheimer is an exception `[Engage]`).
- **Pharmacogenomic testing** may guide medication selection and predict side-effect severity. ==It adds information; it doesn't replace assessment and clinical judgment.==

#### Stress, infection, neuroplasticity?
- **Psychoneuroimmunology**: stress ↔ nervous system ↔ immune function (inflammation linked with depression and mood dysregulation `[Engage]`).
- Infection (including **prenatal**) has been linked with later illness (e.g., schizophrenia), but ==findings aren't conclusive==.
- ==**Neuroplasticity**==: the brain changes its connections with learning, stress, injury. Sequence `[Engage]`: chemical change → structural change → functional change.

> [!tip] NCLEX tip
> ==Meds change neurotransmitter activity quickly, but the brain's adaptation takes **weeks**.== That's why antidepressant teaching includes "it takes weeks to feel better."

## III. Psychotherapeutic medications: overview

#### What's the nursing role with psych meds?
- Medications are a major part of treatment; ==medication + psychotherapy gives the best prognosis== `[Engage]`.
- Nursing priorities: ==monitor response, teach, support adherence, safety, adverse effects==.
- Meds may control symptoms ==without addressing every underlying cause==.

## IV. Anxiolytics

#### Benzodiazepines vs. buspirone?
| | Benzodiazepines | Buspirone |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | **Alprazolam, chlordiazepoxide, diazepam, lorazepam** | Buspirone |
| Mechanism | ==Enhance **GABA**== → ↑ chloride entry → ↓ neuronal firing | ==**Serotonin** receptors== (partial agonist); not GABA |
| Onset | ==**Rapid**== | ==**Several weeks**==; not for an acute panic attack |
| Use | When rapid relief is needed (acute) | ==Chronic== anxiety |
| Dependence | ==Tolerance, physical dependence, misuse== (↑ limbic dopamine → reward `[Engage]`) | ==Lower== |
| Adverse effects | Sedation, drowsiness, impaired concentration and memory | Dizziness, headache, nausea, sedation |

#### Benzodiazepine safety?
- **Half-life matters:** long-acting (diazepam, up to **100 hr** `[Engage]`) → ==**next-day sedation**, ↑ fall risk==. Lorazepam up to **20 hr** `[Engage]`.
- **Paradoxical reaction:** ==agitation, unusual behavior, hallucinations, seizures== → stop that agent `[Engage]`.
- **Fall precautions**; assess ability to do ADLs (ambulation, grooming) on long-acting agents `[Engage]`.

> [!warning] Priority
> **Toxicity → flumazenil** (with a provider prescription) reverses sedation, but ==can trigger **seizures**== in clients with a seizure history or on **TCAs**.

#### Benzodiazepine teaching?
- Treats symptoms, not the cause `[Engage]`.
- ==**No alcohol**== (potentiates CNS depression).
- ==**Never stop long-term therapy abruptly**: taper as prescribed.==
- Risk of addiction with prolonged use; plan activities around sedation.

## V. Antidepressants

#### Five classes and mechanisms?
| Class | Example | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **MAOI** | **Phenelzine** | Blocks monoamine oxidase → serotonin, NE, dopamine last longer |
| **TCA** | **Amitriptyline** | Blocks reuptake of serotonin + NE; ==also hits other receptors== (cholinergic) |
| ==**SSRI**== | **Fluoxetine** | Selectively blocks **serotonin** reuptake |
| **SNRI** | **Venlafaxine** | Blocks **serotonin + NE** reuptake |
| **Neurosteroid** | **Zuranolone** | ==Postpartum depression==; once daily × **14 days**, works in ~**3 days** `[Engage]` |

- **Other agents:** trazodone, nefazodone, ==bupropion==, mirtazapine (distinct mechanisms; used when SSRIs fail or aren't tolerated `[Engage]`).
- Zuranolone `[ADDED — verify]`: causes CNS depression; boxed warning against driving for **≥ 12 hr** after each dose.
- ==Benefit develops over **weeks**, not hours.==

#### Antidepressant adverse effects?
| Class | Key adverse effects |
|---|---|
| **TCAs** | ==Anticholinergic== (dry mouth, constipation, blurred vision, urinary retention); ==**orthostatic hypotension**==; tolerance to anticholinergic effects develops `[Engage]`. Dangerous in overdose (cardiac dysrhythmias) `[ADDED]` |
| **SSRIs** | ==Nausea, agitation, sexual dysfunction==; fewer AEs than TCAs |
| **SNRIs** | Same as SSRIs + ==↓ appetite== |
| **MAOIs** | Sedation, insomnia, weight gain, sexual dysfunction; ==hypertensive crisis== |
| **Bupropion** | ==Higher **seizure** risk== |

> [!danger] Report immediately
> ==After **starting or increasing** any antidepressant, watch closely for worsening mood, agitation, unusual behavior, or **suicidal thinking**.==

#### Anticholinergic effects: nursing care?
- Dry mouth → **hard candy, water, sugarless gum**; constipation → **fiber and fluids**; blurred vision/orthostasis → ==**fall precautions**==; severe urinary retention → may need catheterization `[Engage]`.

#### Antidepressant teaching?
- ==Takes weeks==: SSRIs up to **4–6 wk**; TCAs **2–4 wk** `[Engage]`.
- Sedating → take at **night** (per prescription). ==No driving or heavy machinery until effects are known==, especially **bupropion**.
- ==**Taper**; don't stop abruptly.==
- Sexual dysfunction: ask how the client feels about it; consult the provider for alternatives `[Engage]`.

> [!info] Key contrast
> Client: "I stopped my fluoxetine a few days ago because I hate how it makes me feel."
> **Therapeutic:** "It sounds like the side effects have been hard. Stopping suddenly can make you feel worse. Let's talk with your provider about tapering or other options." `[ADDED]`
> **Non-therapeutic:** "You have to take it no matter what." · "Just restart your usual dose." `[ADDED]`

#### MAOIs and tyramine?
- MAO normally breaks down **tyramine**. On an MAOI, tyramine builds up → NE release → ==**severe hypertension / hypertensive crisis**==.
- Avoid ==**aged, fermented, cured, smoked, overripe**== foods:

| Avoid `[Engage]` | Safer swaps `[Engage]` |
|---|---|
| **Aged/strong cheese** (Swiss, blue) | American, cottage, cream cheese |
| **Cured/processed/smoked meats** (hot dogs, bacon, sausage) | Fresh poultry, fish, beef |
| **Sauces**: soy, teriyaki, fish, shrimp; **tofu/soy** products | Mayonnaise, vinegar, ketchup, mustard |
| **Dried/overripe fruit**: raisins, prunes, avocado, bananas | Apples, grapes, oranges |
| **Red wine, beer**, chocolate | — |

- Also avoid ==**OTC decongestants/ephedrine**== and combining with **TCAs** or other interacting meds.
- Hypertensive crisis signs `[ADDED]`: severe (occipital) headache, stiff neck, palpitations, chest pain, sweating → report immediately.
- Switching to another serotonergic drug requires a washout of ~**14 days** `[ADDED — verify vs course materials]`.

## VI. Antipsychotics (neuroleptics)

#### FGA vs. SGA vs. "third generation"?
| | First-generation (typical) | Second-generation (atypical) | "Third-generation" `[Engage]` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Examples | **Haloperidol, fluphenazine, loxapine, chlorpromazine** | Risperidone, olanzapine (others) | **Aripiprazole, brexpiprazole, cariprazine** |
| Action | ==Strong **dopamine** blockade== | Dopamine (weaker) ==+ serotonin== effects | ==Dopamine **stabilizers**== (adjust low or high transmission) |
| Targets | ==**Positive** symptoms== | Positive **and negative** | Psychotic and mood disorders; brexpiprazole for **dementia agitation** |
| Main risk | ==EPS, NMS== (potency) | ==**Metabolic**== (↑ glucose, lipids, BP, weight) | — |

- Long-acting injectable (**depot**) maintenance is common.
- Antipsychotics in older adults with dementia-related psychosis carry a boxed warning for ↑ mortality `[ADDED]`.

#### Antipsychotic adverse effects?
- **Anticholinergic:** dry mouth, constipation, blurred vision, orthostatic hypotension → same comfort measures; tolerance in a few weeks `[Engage]`.
- ==**Photosensitivity** → sunscreen.==
- **↑ Prolactin:** breast enlargement, ↓ libido, menstrual irregularity, sexual dysfunction.
- **SGA metabolic:** ↑ glucose, lipids, BP, weight → watch clients with or at risk for **T2DM**.
- ==Fall precautions and monitoring.==

#### Antipsychotic teaching?
- ==Continue treatment even after symptoms resolve==; adherence reduces relapse. Don't stop independently.
- Report troublesome side effects to the provider (alternatives exist).
- **Labs:** ==glucose and lipids on SGAs==; ==WBC/ANC on clozapine==.
- A **support person** helps adherence and spotting early relapse.

## VII. Mood stabilizers

#### Which drugs stabilize mood?
- ==**Lithium**== is key for bipolar disorder: ↓ recurrence of manic and depressive episodes.
- **Valproic acid, carbamazepine** (anticonvulsants); **aripiprazole depot** for maintenance.
- ==Medication is only part of relapse prevention; stress management matters== (relapse is possible even at therapeutic levels under heavy stress `[Engage]`).

#### Lithium monitoring?
- ==**Narrow therapeutic window**== → regular serum levels (every **1–2 wk** at start `[Engage]`; then every few days to weeks).
- ==**< 0.5 mEq/L** generally subtherapeutic · **> 1.5 mEq/L** toxicity concern.== Usual therapeutic range **0.6–1.2 mEq/L** `[ADDED — verify vs course materials]`; levels drawn as a trough ~**12 hr** after the last dose `[ADDED — verify]`.
- ==**Renal function**== (cleared by the kidneys; lithium is a salt). Baseline LFTs if on other meds `[Engage]`.
- ==Keep fluid and sodium intake **consistent**==; **2–3 L** water/day `[Engage]`. Don't cut salt.

#### Lithium adverse effects?
- **Expected:** GI upset (nausea, diarrhea), ↑ thirst, ==**fine** hand tremor==, weight gain. Side effects drive many to quit `[Engage]`.
- ==Dehydration, vomiting, diarrhea, heavy sweating ↑ toxicity risk== → ==report significant fluid loss or illness==.

#### Lithium toxicity levels?
| Level | Findings | Action |
|---|---|---|
| ==**Early: > 1.5 mEq/L**== | Poor coordination, confusion, sedation, GI upset, N/V/D, ==**coarse** tremor== | ==**Hold lithium**, notify provider== |
| **Advanced: 2.0–2.5** | ==Seizures, stupor==, very dilute urine, blurred vision, **tinnitus**, jerking movements, respiratory problems | Increase renal excretion (urea/mannitol `[Engage]`); gastric lavage for intentional overdose `[Engage]` [NOTE: current practice: IV fluids and hemodialysis are favored; osmotic diuretics and lavage are rarely used.] |
| ==**Severe: > 2.5**== | Rapid decline → **coma**, respiratory failure, death | ==**Hemodialysis**== |

- **Triggers:** ==dehydration==, overdose, drugs that raise lithium (**loop diuretics** `[Engage]`; also thiazides, NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors `[ADDED]`).

> [!info] Key contrast
> ==**Fine** tremor = expected== side effect. ==**Coarse** tremor = toxicity== → hold and report.

#### Anticonvulsant mood stabilizers?
- **Valproic acid, carbamazepine:** ↓ neuronal ==**kindling**== (escalating seizure-like cascade linked to mania).
- **Valproic acid, topiramate:** ↑ GABA.
- ==Serum levels== for selected drugs (drawn **12 hr** after last dose `[Engage]`); ==**liver function** monitoring==.
- Adverse effects: sedation, dry mouth; weight **gain** (valproic acid) vs. weight **loss** (topiramate, lamotrigine).
- Valproic acid: hepatotoxicity, pancreatitis, teratogenic; carbamazepine and lamotrigine: serious rash (SJS) `[ADDED]`.

## VIII. Sedative-hypnotics

#### Hypnotics: types and use?
- ==Start with **nonpharmacologic sleep strategies**==.
- Categories: benzodiazepines, **nonbenzodiazepine hypnotics** (zolpidem, zaleplon, eszopiclone: act at the benzo site of the GABA receptor, fewer AEs), **melatonin receptor agonist** (ramelteon: trouble falling asleep).
- ==Mostly **short-term** use.==

#### Adverse effects and teaching?
- Dizziness, fatigue, headache, nausea.
- ==**Serious:** sleepwalking, **sleep-driving**, amnesia, hallucinations==; suicidal ideation `[Engage]` → monitor and report.
- ==Take when **ready for bed**==; ==never before driving== `[Engage]`; ==no alcohol or other CNS depressants==; sleep hygiene is the long-term foundation.

## IX. Stimulants

#### Stimulants: use and mechanism?
- **ADHD** and **narcolepsy**: **methylphenidate, amphetamine, dextroamphetamine**.
- ↑ release and ↓ reuptake of dopamine, serotonin, NE → ↓ hyperactivity, impulsivity, inattention.
- Extended-release products → steadier daytime effect. Misuse/excess → tolerance, addiction `[Engage]`.

#### Adverse effects and teaching?
- Nausea, dry mouth, **palpitations**, irritability; ==↓ appetite → weight loss==; ==**growth suppression** in children== if intake is poor.
- ==Monitor appetite, weight, growth==; caregivers help monitor intake.
- ==**Limit caffeine**== (soda, tea).
- ==Avoid **late-day** dosing of once-daily extended-release products== (insomnia).

## X. Herbal remedies

#### Why ask about supplements?
- Common: **St. John's wort, ginseng, chamomile, echinacea**.
- Herbals interact with prescription and OTC meds: ==**St. John's wort + serotonergic antidepressant (fluoxetine) → serotonin syndrome**==.
- ==Ask directly==; incorporate safe cultural/personal practices; discuss interactions so the client can decide `[Engage]`.

## XI. Serious medication reactions

> [!quote] Anchor
> Always connect the medication class with its hallmark emergency findings. Early recognition and prompt nursing action are critical.

#### Serotonin syndrome?
- **Cause:** excess serotonin: overdose, ==multiple antidepressants==, antidepressants + migraine/pain meds, **LSD, ecstasy**, ==St. John's wort==.
- **Findings:** ==agitation/restlessness, **dilated pupils**, tachycardia, hypertension, **sweating**, rigidity, poor coordination==.
- **Action:** ==**stop serotonergic meds**==, stabilize VS, supportive care, sedation with **benzodiazepines**, serotonin antagonist **cyproheptadine**.

#### Serotonin syndrome vs. NMS?
| | Serotonin syndrome | NMS |
|---|---|---|
| Culprit | Serotonergic drugs | ==Antipsychotics== (esp. **haloperidol**) |
| Hallmarks | Agitation, dilated pupils, sweating, poor coordination; hyperreflexia/clonus `[ADDED]` | ==**FEVER**==: fever, encephalopathy, vital-sign instability, ↑ CK, severe rigidity |
| Onset | Usually rapid (hours) `[ADDED]` | After **starting** or **abruptly stopping** an antipsychotic; develops over days `[ADDED]` |
| Treatment | Stop drug, benzos, **cyproheptadine** | Stop drug, cooling, fluids/electrolytes, cardiac monitoring; **bromocriptine** or **dantrolene** |

#### Activation syndrome?
- Soon after **starting** an antidepressant (esp. SSRIs): first **hours to weeks**.
- ==Irritability, anxiety, impulsivity, aggression, agitation, ↑ suicidal thoughts==.

> [!danger] Report immediately
> Watch for signs a suicide plan is being finalized `[Engage]`: ==giving away possessions, calling friends to say goodbye, requesting a will==.

#### Antidepressant discontinuation syndrome?
- After **> 1 month** of use, ==abrupt stop== → insomnia, anxiety, depression, **flu-like** symptoms, =="brain zaps"== (electric-shock sensations); can last **weeks** `[Engage]`.
- Abrupt MAOI stop → **psychosis** `[Engage]`.
- ==Teach gradual tapering under prescriber direction.==

#### Agranulocytosis (clozapine)?
- ==Most strongly linked to **clozapine**==; life-threatening.
- ==**Baseline** WBC/ANC==, then **weekly** labs at first (spaced out after months); testing continues after stopping `[Engage]`.
- ==**WBC < 3,500/mm³** → contact the provider==; provider reviews ANC. [NOTE: current practice: monitoring is ANC-based.]
- ==Fever, sore throat, fatigue, flu-like symptoms, muscle aches → prompt evaluation.==

#### Neuroleptic malignant syndrome?

> [!example] Mnemonic — FEVER (NMS)
> **F**ever (hyperthermia) · **E**ncephalopathy · **V**ital-sign instability (BP up or down, tachycardia) · **E**levated CK · **R**igidity
> **Action:** ==**stop the antipsychotic**==, fluids/electrolytes, ==cooling measures==, cardiac monitoring; provider may order **bromocriptine** (dopamine agonist) or **dantrolene** (muscle relaxant).

#### Extrapyramidal symptoms?
| EPS | Findings | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Acute dystonia** | ==Early== muscle spasm/rigidity: can't open jaw, locked gaze; **oculogyric crisis** | **Benztropine** or **diphenhydramine**; protect airway `[ADDED]` |
| **Akathisia** | ==Severe inner restlessness==, can't sit still, not relieved by moving | Same; ==risk of self-discontinuing or **suicidal ideation**== `[Engage]` |
| **Pseudoparkinsonism** | Shuffling gait, tremor, ==drooling==, rigidity, slumped posture, **pill-rolling** | Benztropine/diphenhydramine |
| ==**Tardive dyskinesia**== | Repetitive involuntary movements of face, tongue, neck, limbs (lip-smacking, tongue thrusting) after long-term therapy | ==Anticholinergics often don't help → **valbenazine**==; ==usually doesn't resolve after stopping== |

- ==**FGAs** cause the most EPS== (potency). EPS also ↑ anxiety and pain `[Engage]`.
- ==**AIMS**== assesses abnormal movements (TD) over time.

## XII. Detoxification protocols

#### Alcohol withdrawal: why and what?
- Chronic alcohol → brain adapts with ==**↓ GABA, ↑ glutamate**== (tolerance).
- Remove alcohol → overactive brain → ==tachycardia, hypertension, N/V, **hallucinations, seizures**==.
- ==**Benzodiazepines** enhance GABA== and control withdrawal.
- Engage: a short half-life benzo may be chosen for severe agitation `[Engage]` [NOTE: current practice: long-acting benzos (chlordiazepoxide, diazepam) are often preferred; short-acting (lorazepam) for liver disease or older adults.]

#### Opioid withdrawal and toxicity?
- Long-term use → receptor down-regulation and desensitization. Withdrawal → ==**↑ norepinephrine**==: muscle aches, ==gooseflesh==, sweating, GI distress.
- ==Severe vomiting/diarrhea → dehydration, electrolyte imbalance== (can be life-threatening).
- Treatment: ==**methadone** or **buprenorphine**== replacement.

> [!warning] Priority
> **Opioid toxicity → naloxone** (with prescription; IM, subcut, IV, intranasal) reverses respiratory depression and coma. Then ==monitor VS every **5–15 min** for several hours==; watch for ==**returning respiratory depression**== (naloxone wears off before many opioids `[ADDED]`), N/V, tachycardia, sweating; have emergency equipment ready for sudden full withdrawal `[Engage]`.

#### CIWA-Ar and COWS?
| Scale | Measures | Nursing use |
|---|---|---|
| ==**CIWA-Ar**== | ==**Alcohol**== withdrawal severity | Score symptoms → sum → ==give **benzodiazepine** dose per sliding-scale order== → reassess |
| ==**COWS**== | ==**Opioid**== withdrawal severity | Score → treat per order (opioid replacement) → reassess |

- ==Higher score → more intensive treatment.== Accurate scoring prevents both **undertreatment** and **oversedation**.
- CIWA-Ar steps `[Engage]`: administer the scale → rate each symptom → sum the categories → calculate the dose from the score and prescription → give the medication.
- CIWA-Ar bands `[ADDED — verify vs course materials]`: < **8–10** minimal · **8–15** moderate · > **15** severe.

## Key nursing priorities

#### What ties the unit together?
- Know each drug's ==mechanism, major adverse effects, and hallmark toxicity==.
- Teach adherence and safe use (alcohol and interaction precautions).
- ==Distinguish an expected effect from an emergency.==
- Use focused tools: **AIMS, CIWA-Ar, COWS**. Education and early recognition are central.

## Self-quiz

> [!question]- 1. Two ways a neurotransmitter's action ends?
> Reuptake into the presynaptic neuron, or breakdown by enzymes such as monoamine oxidase.

> [!question]- 2. Which neurotransmitter is the brain's "brakes," and which drug class enhances it?
> GABA; benzodiazepines.

> [!question]- 3. Why does an antidepressant take weeks to work?
> Neuroplasticity: the brain's adaptation (chemical → structural → functional change) takes weeks.

> [!question]- 4. Client on diazepam is drowsy the next morning. Term and priority?
> Next-day sedation (long half-life); fall precautions.

> [!question]- 5. Client with a seizure disorder has benzodiazepine toxicity. Concern with flumazenil?
> It can trigger seizures (also with TCAs).

> [!question]- 6. A client in acute panic asks for buspirone. Appropriate?
> No. Buspirone takes several weeks; it's for chronic anxiety.

> [!question]- 7. Which antidepressant carries the highest seizure risk?
> Bupropion.

> [!question]- 8. Client on phenelzine wants pepperoni pizza and red wine. Response?
> Avoid: cured meat, aged cheese, and wine are high in tyramine → hypertensive crisis.

> [!question]- 9. Expected anticholinergic effects of amitriptyline and nursing measures?
> Dry mouth (candy, gum, water), constipation (fiber, fluids), blurred vision/orthostasis (fall precautions), urinary retention.

> [!question]- 10. A teen started fluoxetine 1 week ago and is newly agitated and impulsive. Concern?
> Activation syndrome with ↑ suicide risk; report and monitor closely.

> [!question]- 11. Which antipsychotic generation causes more EPS, and which more metabolic effects?
> First-generation → EPS. Second-generation → metabolic.

> [!question]- 12. Teaching for a client on chlorpromazine going to the beach?
> Photosensitivity: wear sunscreen and protective clothing.

> [!question]- 13. Lithium level 1.7 mEq/L with coarse tremor and vomiting. Action?
> Early toxicity: hold lithium and notify the provider.

> [!question]- 14. Fine hand tremor on lithium at 0.9 mEq/L. Concern?
> Expected side effect, not toxicity.

> [!question]- 15. Lithium teaching about fluids and salt?
> 2–3 L water/day; keep sodium intake consistent (don't cut salt); report vomiting, diarrhea, heavy sweating.

> [!question]- 16. Lithium level > 2.5 mEq/L. Anticipated treatment?
> Hemodialysis (risk of coma, respiratory failure).

> [!question]- 17. Client on clozapine reports sore throat and fever. Action and lab threshold?
> Report promptly; check WBC/ANC. WBC < 3,500/mm³ → contact the provider.

> [!question]- 18. Haloperidol client: high fever, rigid, confused, labile BP, ↑ CK. Problem and first action?
> NMS: stop the antipsychotic; cooling, fluids, cardiac monitoring; bromocriptine/dantrolene per order.

> [!question]- 19. Fluoxetine + St. John's wort → sweating, dilated pupils, tachycardia, agitation. Problem and treatment?
> Serotonin syndrome: stop serotonergic agents, supportive care, benzos, cyproheptadine.

> [!question]- 20. Lip-smacking and tongue thrusting after years on fluphenazine. Name, treatment, prognosis?
> Tardive dyskinesia; valbenazine (anticholinergics often ineffective); usually doesn't resolve after stopping.

> [!question]- 21. Client on haloperidol can't open his jaw. Name and drug?
> Acute dystonia; benztropine or diphenhydramine.

> [!question]- 22. Abrupt stop of an SSRI after 6 months → "brain zaps," flu-like, insomnia. Name and prevention?
> Antidepressant discontinuation syndrome; gradual taper.

> [!question]- 23. Zolpidem teaching: when to take and the serious effect to report?
> When ready for bed; no alcohol or driving; report sleep-driving/walking or amnesia.

> [!question]- 24. When should a once-daily ER stimulant be taken, and what to monitor in a child?
> Morning (not late day); appetite, weight, growth.

> [!question]- 25. Alcohol vs. opioid withdrawal: neurochemistry and drug?
> Alcohol: ↓ GABA/↑ glutamate → benzodiazepines (CIWA-Ar). Opioid: ↑ norepinephrine → methadone/buprenorphine (COWS).
