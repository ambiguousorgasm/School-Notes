**Mnemonics:** **HARK**: Humiliation, Afraid, Rape, Kick · **5 P's**: Partners, Practices, Protection, Past history, Pregnancy intention · Abuse cycle: tension-building → battering → honeymoon → repeat

## I. Menstrual disorders

> [!quote] Anchor
> Find the bleeding that threatens stability first, rule out pregnancy, then treat the cause and the pain.

### Normal cycle and menopause

#### What drives the normal cycle?
- Four hormones: **estrogen, progesterone, FSH, LH**. They prepare the endometrium; without pregnancy, estrogen and progesterone fall → endometrium sheds.
- Cycle ~**28 days** from day 1 of menses; flow **3–5 days**, avg **30 mL** `[Engage]`. Normal = spotting up to ~**80 mL**.
- **Assess amount:** pads/tampons used, **clots**, breakthrough bleeding.

#### What does menopause bring? `[Engage]`
- About age **51**, from ↓ estrogen; earlier with endometriosis, chemo, or **removal of ovaries/uterus**.
- Hot flashes, night sweats · vaginal dryness → painful intercourse · urethral atrophy → incontinence · ↓ bone density · ↑ CVD risk · mood and sleep changes.

### Dysmenorrhea

#### Primary vs. secondary?
| | Primary | Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | No underlying condition | **Endometriosis, fibroids, PID** |
| Mechanism | ==Excess **prostaglandins** and **vasopressin**== → uterine contractions | The underlying disease |

- Findings: pelvic/lower abdominal pain, **back pain**, headache, irritability `[Engage]`, nausea, bloating, constipation or diarrhea.
- Risks `[Engage]`: tobacco, high BMI, depression/anxiety, **early menarche**, never pregnant, heavy/long flow, family history, poor social support.

#### Workup and treatment?
- Pelvic exam, ==**β-hCG**==, STI tests, Pap, ultrasound.
- ==**NSAIDs** ↓ prostaglandin production== (first line); **hormonal contraceptives** ↓ symptoms.
- **Heat, massage, exercise**, stress management, sleep. Low-fat vegetarian diet, ↑ low-fat dairy; supplements have little evidence `[Engage]`.

### Amenorrhea

#### Primary vs. secondary, and first cause to check?
- **Primary:** never menstruated. **Secondary:** established menses stop.
- ==**Pregnancy is the first cause to consider**== → β-hCG. Others: hormone imbalance, tumors, **weight loss**.
- ↓ estrogen → ==**osteoporosis, fracture**== (wrist, hip `[Engage]`).
- Assess for **eating disorders, stress, excessive exercise**.

#### Nursing priorities?
- Rule out pregnancy → hormone levels and structural causes (ultrasound) → treat the cause (e.g., **estrogen therapy** for imbalance `[Engage]`).
- ==**Calcium-rich** nutrition==; address weight loss, disordered eating, psychosocial concerns (dietitian, mental health); education and follow-up.

### PMS and PMDD

#### PMS vs. PMDD?
- **PMS:** physical + emotional symptoms tied to the cycle, starting **after ovulation** (~**14 days** before menses `[Engage]`): pain, breast tenderness, bloating, fatigue, mood and appetite changes.
- **PMDD:** ==severe form with significant mood symptoms and functional impairment==: PMS + sadness, extreme mood swings, or anger `[Engage]`. Linked to a serotonin deficit `[Engage]`.
- Affects relationships, work, quality of life.
- Workup `[Engage]`: estradiol, FSH, **TSH** (rule out thyroid disease).

#### Management?
- **SSRIs** (fluoxetine, sertraline, citalopram `[Engage]`) ↑ CNS serotonin → emotional symptoms. **NSAIDs** for discomfort. **Hormonal therapies** (OCs, GnRH agonists, spironolactone `[Engage]`); anxiolytics `[Engage]`.
- **Complex carbohydrates** (↑ serotonin), exercise, sleep, stress management; **CBT** for coping.

> [!danger] Report immediately
> SSRIs in **adolescents and young adults**: ==monitor for suicidal thoughts==. Suicidal thoughts need emergency intervention `[Engage]`.

### Dysfunctional uterine bleeding (DUB)

#### What is DUB, and the main complication?
- Bleeding outside expected **regularity, duration, frequency, or amount**; ==**> 80 mL/cycle**== is abnormal. [NOTE: current practice: now called **abnormal uterine bleeding (AUB)**.]
- Causes: structural anomalies, tumors, bleeding disorders, abnormal ovulation `[Engage]`. Most common around **menarche and perimenopause**, but any age.
- ==Major complication: **anemia**==; fatigue. Severe loss → **hypotension, shock**.

#### Priority assessment for heavy bleeding?

> [!warning] Priority
> Watch for ==dizziness, confusion, pallor, weakness, dyspnea, tachycardia, hypotension==. **Physiologic stability takes priority over teaching.** Teach the client to seek emergency care for these signs `[Engage]`.

- Workup: **β-hCG**, **CBC** (RBC, Hgb, Hct), **TSH, prolactin**, pelvic exam, transvaginal US, hysteroscopy, ==**endometrial biopsy**== (rule out cancer).
- Treatment by goals and cause: hormonal therapy (OCs), **hormone-releasing IUD**, **ablation**, **hysterectomy**.
- **Levonorgestrel IUD** `[Engage]`: prevents endometrial buildup → ↓ or stops bleeding; also contraception. Outpatient insertion; cramping after; provider removes/replaces.

### Endometriosis

#### What is it, and who gets it?
- ==Chronic, **estrogen-dependent** inflammatory condition==: endometrial-like tissue **outside the uterus** (ovaries, bowel, peritoneum).
- Cyclic bleeding → inflammation, pain, scarring, **adhesions** → infertility, bowel/bladder symptoms, chronic pelvic pain.
- Thought to come from **retrograde menstruation** `[Engage]`. Risk ↑ with **early menarche, short cycles, never pregnant** (more estrogen); ↓ with pregnancy, breastfeeding, tubal ligation, current OC use `[Engage]`.

#### Findings and diagnosis?
- **Dysmenorrhea, chronic pelvic pain, painful intercourse**, diarrhea or constipation, **infertility**; severity varies widely.
- Pelvic exam, transvaginal US, ==**laparoscopy**== (direct visualization). CBC, UA, STI tests to rule out other causes; CA-125 is nonspecific, not diagnostic `[Engage]`.

#### Treatment?
- **NSAIDs** for pain · **combined hormonal contraceptives** suppress ovulation · **GnRH analogs** suppress hormone fluctuations (temporary menopause-like state: hot flashes, bone loss `[ADDED]`).
- **Relugolix/estradiol/norethindrone** (Myfembree) for moderate–severe pain: ==limit to **24 months** (bone mineral density loss)==.
- **Laparoscopic excision or ablation** when meds aren't enough.
- Combined hormonal contraceptives `[ADDED]`: VTE risk; contraindicated in smokers **> 35**.

### Uterine fibroids

#### Fibroids: what and who?
- ==**Benign** tumors of uterine smooth muscle== (leiomyomas); grow with **estrogen**.
- Heavy bleeding, pelvic pain, **dyspareunia**, pressure; large → **urinary frequency, constipation**; can block implantation → infertility.
- Risks `[Engage]`: obesity, early menarche, never pregnant, **late menopause**, family history.

#### Treatment options?
| Option | What it does |
|---|---|
| Hormonal contraceptives, hormone IUD, GnRH agonists, NSAIDs | ↓ pain and bleeding |
| **Uterine artery embolization (UAE)** | ==Blocks blood flow to fibroids== → shrink. Femoral artery catheter; particles injected; interventional radiology `[Engage]` |
| **Radiofrequency ablation** | Heat shrinks fibroids (laparoscopic, US-guided `[Engage]`) |
| **Myomectomy** | ==Removes fibroids, **preserves the uterus**== |
| **Hysterectomy** | Definitive |

- Choice depends on **symptoms, age, fertility goals**. After UAE: check the femoral puncture site for bleeding/hematoma and distal pulses `[ADDED — verify]`.

### Gynecologic diagnostics

#### Which test does what?
| Test | Purpose | Nursing points |
|---|---|---|
| **Pelvic exam** | Inspection → speculum (Pap) → bimanual | Expect pressure/discomfort `[Engage]` |
| **Pap test** | Cervical cells, **HPV**-related changes | ==Not during menses==; light bleeding OK, notify if **> 24 hr** `[Engage]` |
| **Colposcopy** `[Engage]` | Magnified cervix exam ± biopsy after abnormal Pap | Report abdominal pain, heavy bleeding, fever, foul drainage |
| **Transvaginal US** | Pelvic structures; good for **fibroids** | — |
| **Laparoscopy** | Direct view of **endometriosis**; general anesthesia | — |
| **Hysteroscopy** | Inside uterus: diagnose/treat bleeding, fibroids | ==Contraindicated in pregnancy==; usually not with cervical cancer or PID `[Engage]` |

- **β-hCG:** negative = not pregnant; positive = pregnant, or threatened abortion, ectopic, molar pregnancy, cancer `[Engage]`.

### Hysterectomy

#### Types?
| Type | Removed |
|---|---|
| **Total** | Uterus + cervix |
| **Supracervical / partial** | Uterus; ==cervix remains== |
| **Radical** | More extensive (typically **cancer**): uterus, tubes, cervix, upper vagina, lymph nodes `[Engage]` |
| Oophorectomy / salpingectomy `[Engage]` | Ovaries / fallopian tubes |

#### Post-op care?
- Monitor **pain, infection** (fever, pelvic pain, purulent vaginal drainage `[Engage]`), **bleeding, VTE**, bowel/bladder function, respiratory status.
- ==**Early ambulation, VTE prophylaxis**, cough/deep breathing, **incentive spirometry**==.

#### Discharge teaching?
- Lifting and strenuous activity restrictions; stairs may be limited (dehiscence `[Engage]`). Incision care depends on approach (abdominal: wash daily, mild soap and water `[Engage]`).
- ==Nothing in the vagina (tampons, douching) for ~**6 weeks**==; **no sex until the provider clears it**.
- **Report** foul-smelling drainage or bleeding heavier than light spotting.
- ==**Oophorectomy** → abrupt surgical menopause== → call provider about a treatment plan.

### Postmenopausal bleeding

> [!danger] Report immediately
> ==**Any** bleeding or spotting after menopause needs evaluation.== Causes: **endometrial cancer**, hyperplasia, atrophy, polyps, hormone therapy. Don't assume it's benign. Priority action: **reinforce provider follow-up**.

### Nursing care: menstrual disorders

#### Six steps for menstrual disorders?
- **Cues:** menarche/menopause age; frequency, duration, regularity, amount; symptoms and timing; mood; ==**vital signs** (bleeding risk)==; psychosocial impact.
- **Prioritize physiologic stability** with heavy bleeding (signs of shock).
- **Goals:** hemodynamic stability, comfort goal met, understanding.
- **Actions:** med and self-management teaching; nonpharm pain relief; referrals (gynecology, dietitian, mental health, fertility, endocrinology).
- **Evaluate with teach-back.** Create an open, nonjudgmental space; clients often don't report symptoms `[Engage]`.

## II. Prostatitis

#### What causes prostatitis?
- **Inflammation of the prostate**: acute/chronic bacterial or nonbacterial. ==Only a minority are bacterial== (**5–10%** `[Engage]`); nonbacterial tends to be chronic.
- Bacterial → usually **ascending infection** (urethra/bladder); ==**E. coli**== is the major cause (**50–90%** `[Engage]`). Younger → sexual activity; older → **catheterization** `[Engage]`.

#### Risk factors?
- **UTI or indwelling catheter** · urethral stricture · **BPH** · sex without condoms / STI · **diabetes, immunocompromise** · history of sexual abuse · non-retractable foreskin `[Engage]` · ==prior prostatitis== (recurrence).

#### How do you prevent catheter-related infection?
- ==Avoid unnecessary catheters==; use alternatives (external catheter `[Engage]`); **smallest** appropriate size.
- **Aseptic** insertion; secure it (no traction); keep drainage unobstructed; ==bag below the bladder==; empty regularly.
- ==**Remove as soon as no longer needed.**==

#### Findings and diagnosis?
- Difficulty urinating, **dysuria, hematuria, fever**, pelvic/genital or **lower-back pain**; sexual dysfunction. Long-term: chronic pelvic pain, infertility `[Engage]`.
- ==Major safety concern: **urinary retention**== → **bladder scan**.
- **UA + urine culture**; blood/semen samples; CBC if sepsis risk; **CT** for abscess; **digital rectal exam** (pressure, not usually painful) `[Engage]`.
- **PSA** may rise during prostatitis → ==misleading; PSA testing not recommended during active prostatitis== `[Engage]`. Prostatitis does not cause cancer.

#### Treatment and teaching?
- **Antibiotics** for bacterial (fluoroquinolones, aminoglycosides, penicillins, cephalosporins `[Engage]`); STI-specific treatment when indicated; **abscess → drainage**.
- **NSAIDs** (naproxen, ibuprofen) + **warm baths**; ↑ fluids.
- Teach: ==finish antibiotics==; avoid bladder irritants (**alcohol, caffeine, spicy/acidic foods** `[Engage]`); condoms.

> [!danger] Report immediately
> ==Inability to urinate or empty the bladder → immediate care.== In hospital, notify the urologist/provider.

## III. Erectile dysfunction (ED)

#### What causes ED?
- Can't achieve or maintain an erection adequate for intercourse (usually **≥ 6 mo** `[Engage]`).
- **Vascular, neurologic, hormonal, medication, psychological** factors; usually combined.
- Associated: **HTN, hyperlipidemia, CVD, obesity, diabetes**; also MS, stroke, hypothyroidism, pelvic fracture/SCI, cycling pressure `[Engage]`; risk ↑ with age.

> [!tip] NCLEX tip
> ==ED may be an early warning sign of cardiovascular disease.== Review CV and metabolic risk factors.

#### Which drugs cause ED?
- **SSRIs** (fluoxetine, citalopram) · **cimetidine** · **ketoconazole** · **spironolactone** · **clonidine, methyldopa** · **thiazides** (HCTZ) · **beta blockers** (atenolol).
- Also assess **smoking, alcohol/substance use, depression, anxiety, neurologic disease**.

#### Assessment and diagnosis?
- Ask about medications and sexual history ==directly and without judgment==; ask about morning/nocturnal erections `[Engage]`. Say it's a routine part of care.
- Labs: **A1C, lipids, testosterone, TSH, CBC, electrolytes, LFTs**.
- **Nocturnal tumescence** testing separates physiologic from psychological causes. Penile biothesiometry (sensation) `[Engage]`. Ultrasound/arteriogram for blood flow.

#### Treatment and PDE5 safety?
- **Lifestyle first:** target CV/metabolic risk (activity; low-cholesterol, low-fat, low-salt diet; diabetes control; quit tobacco, alcohol, drugs `[Engage]`); adjust offending meds; counseling (partner with permission).
- **PDE5 inhibitors** (sildenafil) ↑ penile blood flow **during sexual arousal**. AEs: ==headache, flushing, nasal congestion, visual changes==. Use cautiously with antihypertensives `[Engage]`. Each PDE5 inhibitor has a different duration → teach timing before sex `[Engage]`.
- **Testosterone** if levels are low · **vacuum device** (cylinder + pump + elastic band at the base) · **prostaglandin** self-injection (erection **≤ 90 min**) · penile prosthesis/revascularization `[Engage]`.

> [!warning] Priority
> ==**Never** combine PDE5 inhibitors with **nitrates** (nitroglycerin): severe hypotension.== **Priapism > 4 hr = emergency.** Sexual-performance supplements may contain hidden PDE5 inhibitors → talk to the provider first.

#### Aging adults and sexuality? `[Engage]`
- Don't assume older adults aren't sexually active. ==STIs have doubled in adults **55+** since **2014**==. Include sexuality in care at every age.

## IV. Fibrocystic breast changes

#### What are fibrocystic changes?
- ==Most common **benign** breast finding==; from estrogen/progesterone fluctuation. Most common at age **30–50**; more likely with estrogen/antiestrogen therapy or **HRT** after menopause `[Engage]`.
- Tender, heavy, swollen, lumpy; ==**worse before/during menses**==, better after; usually resolves after menopause `[Engage]`.
- ==Not associated with increased breast cancer risk.==

#### Benign vs. concerning findings?
| Fibrocystic | Concerning: report |
|---|---|
| **Mobile**, tender | ==**Hard, fixed, nontender** mass== |
| Changes with the cycle | Skin **dimpling/puckering**, **nipple retraction** |
| — | **Bloody nipple discharge** |
| — | ==New **postmenopausal** lump== |

#### Assessment and teaching?
- **Clinical breast exam** → **mammogram** → **breast ultrasound** if more detail is needed → **fine-needle aspiration** of a cyst (confirms benign + relieves pain) `[Engage]`.
- **Mammogram prep:** ==no deodorant, lotion, perfume, or powder== on breasts/underarms; remove neck/chest jewelry; compression is uncomfortable `[Engage]`.
- **Breast self-awareness:** know your normal; report new lumps, dimples, puckers, ridges, rashes, nipple discharge.
- Comfort: **supportive bra**, warm or cold compresses, analgesics.
- **Acetaminophen:** max **3,000–4,000 mg/day**; ==read labels on combination products (cold medicines)==; contraindicated in severe liver disease `[Engage]`. Ibuprofen: tell the provider before surgery or if pregnant `[Engage]`.

## V. Gender-affirming care

> [!quote] Anchor
> Transgender clients have the same primary health needs as everyone else. Being transgender is not a mental health disorder.

#### Barriers, and the nurse's stance?
- Barriers: **stigma, discrimination**, lack of knowledgeable providers (about **one-quarter** report being denied care `[Engage]`) → delayed preventive care.
- Higher rates of anxiety, depression, substance use, self-injury, PTSD come from ==discrimination and trauma, not from transgender identity== `[Engage]`. ==Routinely screen for mental health, partner violence, self-injury, suicide risk== `[Engage]`.
- Respectful, competent, individualized care; accurate, unbiased information on all options; the client decides. Keep personal beliefs out `[Engage]`. Know the laws where you practice.

#### How do you create a safe environment?
- Give your own name and pronouns; ==ask the client's name and pronouns==. Avoid "ma'am/sir" `[Engage]`. Use inclusive language ("**partner**").
- Ask only what care requires, not out of curiosity.
- Name not matching the chart: "Is it possible that your information could be under a different name?" `[Engage]`.
- **Mistake:** ==acknowledge, apologize briefly, move on== (e.g., "I'm sorry, Ms. Brown." `[ADDED]`).
- **Two-step intake** `[Engage]`: sex assigned at birth + gender. Document both, plus name, pronouns, and **anatomy present**. ID band shows current name and gender.
- **Privacy:** don't assume they're comfortable disclosing; ==involve the client in room assignment== (private room if available, or by gender) `[Engage]`.
- Ask about **non-prescribed hormones** (online, friends); notify the provider `[Engage]`.

#### Screening and monitoring?
| Area | Feminizing therapy | Masculinizing therapy |
|---|---|---|
| Hormone targets `[Engage]` | Testosterone **< 50 ng/dL**; estradiol **100–200 pg/mL** | Testosterone **400–700 ng/dL** |
| Key labs | Hormones, lipids, glucose; ==**spironolactone → K+, BUN, creatinine**== | ==**Hematocrit**== (male range), lipids, glucose |
| Cancer screening | **Breast** (mammogram by age), **prostate** | **Breast** (if tissue remains), **cervical** |
| Bone | Osteoporosis screening from **65** (earlier if high risk) `[Engage]` | If testosterone stopped or inconsistent `[Engage]` |

- ==Screen by the organs present.== Vaginal bleeding on testosterone → follow-up `[Engage]`.

#### Hormone therapy: effects and risks?
| | Estrogen + antiandrogens (feminizing) | Testosterone (masculinizing) |
|---|---|---|
| Effects | ↓ muscle, ↓ facial/body hair, breast growth, ↓ libido, fat redistribution; voice unchanged `[Engage]` | Deeper voice, ↑ facial/body hair, acne, scalp hair loss, ↑ muscle, menses stop `[Engage]` |
| Major risk | ==**Thromboembolism**== (DVT, PE, stroke, MI); also gallstones, ↑ lipids, breast cancer `[Engage]` | ==**Erythrocytosis**== → monitor Hct |
| Drug-specific | **Spironolactone → hyperkalemia** | ==Topical gel transfers by skin contact== → wash hands; keep from partners, children, pets `[Engage]` |
| Contraindications `[Engage]` | Prior VTE/PE, hormone-sensitive cancer, uncontrolled conditions | Hormone-sensitive cancer |

- Estrogen teaching `[Engage]`: report leg pain/swelling, headache, blurry vision, SOB, chest pain; take daily; doesn't prevent STIs.
- Discuss **fertility preservation** before therapy (some changes are permanent). Smokers and clients with comorbidities: use cautiously `[Engage]`.
- ==**Continue hormones during hospitalization** unless contraindicated== `[Engage]`.

#### Surgery: pre- and post-op?
- Not every client wants hormones or surgery; goals are individual.
- **Pre-op:** counseling, **fertility discussion**, mental health, post-op care planning. WPATH: ≥ **6 mo** of hormones before surgery `[Engage]`.
- **Post-op priorities:** pain, ==**VTE prevention**==, activity restrictions, mental health follow-up.
- ==**Vaginoplasty:** dilation is essential as directed== (several times a day, tapering); yellow/brown discharge ~**1 mo** is expected `[Engage]`.
- ==**Phalloplasty:** monitor for **UTI** and **urethral stricture**== (report trouble voiding).
- **Chest reconstruction** `[Engage]`: drains **1–2 wk** (empty and measure q**12 hr**); compression vest/binder at all times; lift **≤ 5 lb**, no arms overhead.
- **Orchiectomy** `[Engage]`: no more androgen blockers needed; may notice ↓ libido, fatigue.

## VI. Violence and abuse

#### What counts as partner violence?
- **Sexual violence, stalking, physical violence, psychological aggression**: anyone, any age, gender, orientation, race, ethnicity, income.
- **Psychological:** humiliation, coercion, **isolation, control**. **Physical:** traumatic injury. **Sexual:** forced or attempted forced sexual activity.
- Higher-risk groups `[Engage]`: females, adolescents, military/veterans, immigrants, people with disabilities, LGBTQIA individuals.

#### Cycle of abuse?
- **Tension-building** → **battering** → **honeymoon** (apologies, gifts, promises) → ==repeats==.
- Fear, financial dependence, children, housing, safety make leaving hard. ==The period around leaving is especially dangerous.==

#### Injury patterns to recognize? `[Engage]`
- **Face, head, neck**; injuries hidden by clothing; ==**defensive** hand/forearm injuries==; **multiple stages of healing**; story doesn't match injuries; substance use as coping.

#### Human trafficking red flags?
- Fearful/avoidant; ==injury explanation doesn't match findings==; ==someone else controls communication or ID documents==; not allowed to speak for themselves; malnutrition, abuse signs.
- Also `[Engage]`: vague address or "just visiting"; brand or tattoo.
- Conditions: **bruises, fractures, burns, STIs, PID, tuberculosis**.

#### Elder maltreatment?
- Any intentional act or ==failure to act== that harms or risks harm to someone ==**≥ 60**==. Emotional/psychological, physical, sexual, **financial**, **neglect**. Often a caregiver or trusted person.
- ==Know your state's mandatory reporting rules.==

#### HARK screening?

> [!example] Mnemonic — HARK (within the last year, by partner or ex-partner)
> **H**umiliation or emotional abuse
> **A**fraid of partner/ex-partner
> **R**ape or forced sexual activity
> **K**ick, hit, slap, or other physical harm
> ==**≥ 1** "yes" = partner violence.== Screen **privately**, away from partners, family, friends.

#### Nursing priorities?

> [!warning] Priority
> 1. ==**Immediate safety first**==: ask if they or family have been threatened. Credible immediate danger → follow agency policy to notify law enforcement `[Engage]`.
> 2. **ABCs and acute injuries.**
> 3. **Interview privately**; use a ==**professional interpreter**== (not family or friends `[Engage]`). The visitor may be the perpetrator.
> 4. ==Don't force an adult to leave or accept help.== Offer resources and safety planning when ready.

- **Resources discreetly:** don't slip information into their bag; leave it somewhere private like the restroom `[Engage]`. Reassure them ==the abuse isn't their fault== and help is available anytime.
- **Safety plan** `[Engage]`: packed bag (IDs, birth certificates, insurance, financial papers, cash, cards, meds, clothes, prepaid phone), **code word** with trusted people, shelters and legal aid, transportation plan.
- **Document** objectively and accurately, ==in the client's own words== when appropriate (may be used legally).
- **Mandatory reporting** `[Engage]`: child abuse (all states); elder and disabled-adult abuse (most); weapon injuries (many); adult partner violence usually **not** mandated.
- Hotlines `[Engage]`: Domestic Violence **800-799-7233** (text START to 88788); Sexual Assault **800-656-4673**.

> [!info] Key contrast
> **Therapeutic:** "You don't deserve to be hurt. When you're ready, I can help you make a safety plan." `[ADDED]`
> **Non-therapeutic:** "Why don't you just leave?" (blames, pressures) · putting pamphlets in her purse (unsafe) `[ADDED]`

#### Care after sexual assault?
- **Trauma-informed care**; treat injuries; assess pregnancy and STI risk.
- Tests `[Engage]`: pregnancy; HIV, hep B, syphilis; trichomoniasis, BV, gonorrhea, chlamydia.
- ==**Emergency contraception**== when pregnancy is possible.
- **STI prophylaxis** (chlamydia, gonorrhea); **vaccines**: HPV if unvaccinated and in the age range; **hep B** if the assailant's status is unknown `[Engage]`.
- ==**HIV PEP** as soon as possible: ideally **≤ 24 hr**, no later than **72 hr**; taken for **28 days**== `[Engage]` (e.g., bictegravir/emtricitabine/tenofovir alafenamide).
- Arrange testing and follow-up.

## VII. Sexually transmitted infections

#### What's the big picture?
- Spread by **vaginal, oral, anal** contact; bacteria and viruses. ==Many are **asymptomatic**== → screening matters.
- Untreated: **infertility, chronic pain, ectopic pregnancy, cancer**, neuro/CV complications.
- ==Bacterial → antibiotics (cure); viral → managed, not cured.==
- **Older adults:** STIs rising in adults **> 65**; often lack current information `[Engage]`.

#### STI comparison?
| STI | Key findings | Diagnosis | Treatment / key teaching |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chlamydia** (*C. trachomatis*); most common bacterial STI `[Engage]` | ==Often asymptomatic==; cervicitis, purulent discharge, bleeding, dysuria, frequency, rectal symptoms; arthritis `[Engage]` | Swab or urine (self-collect OK) `[Engage]` | ==**Doxycycline 100 mg PO BID × 7 days**== (azithromycin alternative: pregnancy, adherence `[Engage]`); abstain **≥ 7 days**; retest ~**3 mo** |
| **Gonorrhea** (*N. gonorrhoeae*) | Women: none or mild. Men: dysuria, urethral discharge; ==rectum and throat== | Culture swab (cervix, urethra, rectum, throat) or urine | ==**Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM × 1**==; add **doxycycline** if chlamydia not ruled out; alt cefixime **800 mg PO** `[Engage]` |
| **Genital herpes** (HSV-2; HSV-1 also) | Vesicles → ==painful ulcers==; first outbreak fever, aches; tingling prodrome; recurrences milder; ==sheds from normal-looking skin== | Lesion swab; blood test if no lesions | **Acyclovir** ↓ symptoms and recurrences, ==doesn't cure==; no sex during lesions **or prodrome**; condoms otherwise |
| **Syphilis** (*T. pallidum*) | ==Primary: **painless chancre**== → secondary: rash on **palms/soles** + systemic → latent: none → tertiary: neuro, CV, ocular, organ damage | **VDRL / RPR**; lesion swab; test for **HIV** | ==**Penicillin G benzathine** IM== (1 dose early; 3 doses late `[Engage]`); treat partners |
| **HPV** (> **200** subtypes) | Mostly asymptomatic, clears on its own; some → **genital warts**; ==cervical and other cancers== (throat, anus, penis, vulva) | hrHPV test q**5 yr** age **30–65** (self-collect OK) → Pap/colposcopy if positive `[Engage]` | ==**Vaccination**==; continue cervical screening; warts: cryo, laser, surgery, topical; cervical lesions: LEEP or cone biopsy `[Engage]` |

- Chlamydia screening `[Engage]`: ==annually for sexually active females **< 25**==, and older with a new/multiple partners or a partner with an STI. Test extragenital sites based on sexual practices; **opt-out** screening improves detection `[Engage]`.
- Syphilis in pregnancy `[Engage]`: ==universal screening at the first prenatal visit, repeated later==, and at birth if prenatal care was incomplete (congenital syphilis rising).
- Untreated syphilis complications `[Engage]`: stroke, blindness, dementia, **aortitis**, carotid stenosis.

#### Bacterial vaginosis vs. PID?
| | Bacterial vaginosis | Pelvic inflammatory disease |
|---|---|---|
| What | ==Altered vaginal flora; **not an STI**== | ==Ascending infection== of uterus, tubes, ovaries, usually from **untreated chlamydia/gonorrhea** (**10–15%** of cases `[Engage]`) |
| Findings | ==Thin white/gray discharge, **fishy odor**==; itching, burning; may be none | ==Pelvic/lower abdominal pain==, discharge/bleeding, **dyspareunia** |
| Risk/complication | New/multiple partners, **douching**, no condoms; ↑ risk of other STIs `[Engage]` | ==**Infertility** (up to half), **ectopic pregnancy**, chronic pelvic pain== (up to one-third) `[Engage]`; risk ↑ with multiple partners, IUD, prior PID |
| Diagnosis | **Clue cells**; positive **whiff test** (KOH) `[Engage]` | Clinical + pelvic exam; ==**rule out pregnancy**==; STI tests; ultrasound |
| Treatment | **Metronidazole or clindamycin** (PO/vaginal); partners usually not treated `[Engage]`; no alcohol with metronidazole `[ADDED]` | Antibiotics (cefoxitin, doxycycline `[Engage]`); ==**hospitalize if severe, pregnant, or failed oral therapy**== |

#### Sexual history: the 5 P's?

> [!example] Mnemonic — 5 P's
> **Partners**: number and gender (past **12 mo** `[Engage]`), partners' risks
> **Practices**: oral, vaginal, anal
> **Protection**: condoms and other prevention; always or sometimes?
> **Past history**: prior STI testing and diagnoses (theirs and partners')
> **Pregnancy intention**: current or future plans
> ==Explain these questions are a **routine** part of health care==; ask permission before sensitive questions; neutral face and body language `[Engage]`.

#### Prevention teaching?
- Vaccines: ==**HPV, hepatitis A, hepatitis B**==. HPV at **11–12** (from **9**; generally not after **26**) `[Engage]`.
- Correct condom use; ==**new condom every act**==; check expiration; ==**water-based lubricant only** with latex==. Mutual monogamy lowers risk.
- **Doxy-PEP** `[Engage]`: for men who have sex with men and transgender women with a bacterial STI in the past **12 mo**: doxycycline **200 mg × 1** within **72 hr** of condomless sex. ==Not standalone==: combine with screening, PrEP, counseling.

#### General STI nursing care?
- ==Abstain until treatment is complete==; partners evaluated and treated; retest as needed.
- Know state and local **reporting** requirements.
- **Nonjudgmental** communication: shame and stigma delay care `[Engage]`.
- Standard precautions; hand hygiene; safe injection practices `[Engage]`.
- **Evaluate with teach-back.**

> [!warning] Priority
> Encourage partner notification, **except** when it would ==increase the client's risk of partner violence== `[Engage]`.

## VIII. Common threads

#### What runs through every topic?
- **Communication:** private, welcoming, nonjudgmental.
- ==**Safety first**==: find the immediate threat or physiologic priority.
- ==**Rule out pregnancy**== in gynecologic presentations when indicated.
- Individualized education + **teach-back**; collaborate with the right interprofessional team.

## Self-quiz

> [!question]- 1. Primary vs. secondary dysmenorrhea, and the first-line drug class?
> Primary: no underlying condition (prostaglandins/vasopressin). Secondary: caused by endometriosis, fibroids, PID. NSAIDs (↓ prostaglandins).

> [!question]- 2. A 22-year-old hasn't menstruated in 6 months. First test?
> β-hCG to rule out pregnancy.

> [!question]- 3. Why teach calcium-rich foods in amenorrhea?
> Low estrogen → osteoporosis and fracture risk.

> [!question]- 4. A 17-year-old starts fluoxetine for PMDD. Key monitoring?
> Suicidal thoughts (adolescents/young adults on SSRIs); emergency intervention if present.

> [!question]- 5. Client with heavy menses is dizzy, pale, HR 118, BP 88/54. Priority?
> Physiologic stability (possible shock) over teaching; notify, support hemodynamics.

> [!question]- 6. Endometriosis vs. fibroids: what's the tissue in each?
> Endometriosis: endometrial-like tissue outside the uterus (estrogen-dependent inflammation). Fibroids: benign uterine smooth-muscle tumors.

> [!question]- 7. Maximum duration for relugolix/estradiol/norethindrone, and why?
> 24 months, because of bone mineral density loss.

> [!question]- 8. Which fibroid procedure preserves the uterus by removing the fibroids?
> Myomectomy.

> [!question]- 9. Three Pap test teaching points?
> Not during menses; mild pressure; light bleeding is OK, notify if it lasts > 24 hr.

> [!question]- 10. Post-hysterectomy discharge: two vaginal and one menopause teaching point?
> Nothing in the vagina ~6 weeks; no sex until cleared; oophorectomy → abrupt surgical menopause.

> [!question]- 11. A 68-year-old reports light spotting. Response?
> Any postmenopausal bleeding needs provider evaluation (possible endometrial cancer); reinforce follow-up.

> [!question]- 12. Most important safety concern in acute prostatitis, and assessment tool?
> Urinary retention; bladder scan.

> [!question]- 13. Why not check PSA during active prostatitis?
> Prostatitis can elevate PSA, making it misleading.

> [!question]- 14. Client on sildenafil has chest pain. What drug is contraindicated, and why?
> Nitrates (nitroglycerin): severe hypotension.

> [!question]- 15. Which breast mass needs prompt follow-up: mobile and tender before menses, or hard, fixed, nontender?
> Hard, fixed, nontender. Also report dimpling, nipple retraction, bloody discharge.

> [!question]- 16. Mammogram prep?
> No deodorant, lotion, perfume, or powder on breasts/underarms; remove jewelry.

> [!question]- 17. Lab to monitor with spironolactone in feminizing therapy? With testosterone?
> Spironolactone: K+ (hyperkalemia), BUN, creatinine. Testosterone: hematocrit (erythrocytosis).

> [!question]- 18. Major safety teaching for estrogen therapy?
> Thromboembolism: report leg pain/swelling, chest pain, SOB, headache, blurry vision.

> [!question]- 19. A hospitalized transgender woman takes estradiol. Continue it?
> Yes, unless contraindicated.

> [!question]- 20. You misgender a client. What do you do?
> Acknowledge, apologize briefly, use the correct name/pronouns, move on.

> [!question]- 21. HARK: what's a positive screen, and where do you screen?
> One or more "yes" answers; screen privately, away from partners/family.

> [!question]- 22. Client with bruises in various stages; a companion answers every question and holds her ID. First nursing action?
> Assess immediate safety, alone with the client. Suspect trafficking or partner violence; leave resources discreetly; don't force help.

> [!question]- 23. Time window for HIV PEP after sexual assault, and duration?
> As soon as possible, ideally within 24 hr, no later than 72 hr; 28 days.

> [!question]- 24. Chlamydia regimen and follow-up?
> Doxycycline 100 mg PO BID × 7 days; abstain ≥ 7 days; treat partners; retest ~3 months.

> [!question]- 25. Painless genital chancre: which STI and drug?
> Primary syphilis; penicillin G benzathine IM.
