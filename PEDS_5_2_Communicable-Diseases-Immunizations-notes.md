# Communicable Diseases and Immunizations — PEDS 5.2

## Cram sheet

**Precautions**

| Type | Setup | Course diseases |
|---|---|---|
| **Airborne** | ==Negative-pressure room==; **N95** on before entering | ==Measles==, ==varicella (+ contact)==, TB |
| **Droplet** | Single room; standard mask within **< 3 ft**; child wears surgical mask for transport | ==Diphtheria (+ contact)==; mumps, meningococcal meningitis, pertussis, rubella `[ADDED]` |
| **Contact** | Single room; ==gown + gloves on entry, off before exit== | ==Polio==, varicella, diphtheria; scabies, lice, impetigo `[ADDED]` |

**PPE:** on = hand hygiene → **gown → mask → goggles → gloves** · off = ==**gloves → goggles → gown → mask**== → hand hygiene

**Numbers**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Measles in air | Up to **2 hr** · incubation **10–14 days** |
| Varicella | Contagious ==until all lesions crusted== · rash **5–7 days** · **2-dose** vaccine |
| Mumps | Contagious **1–2 days before** to **5 days after** onset · incubation **7–21 days** |
| Hepatitis incubation | A **4 wk** · B **12 wk** · C **8 wk** |
| Mono | Ages **15–24** · incubation **3–6 wk** · ==no contact sports **4–6 wk**== |
| Roseola | **6–12 mo** · fever ==**≥ 104°F** × **3–5 days**==, then rash · home until **24 hr** after fever gone |
| Fifth disease | **5–15 yr** · ==not contagious once rash appears== · arthropathy up to **3 wk** |
| HFMD | **< 5 yr** · most contagious first **7 days** · stool shedding **~6 wk** |
| COVID-19 | Isolate until ==fever-free **24 hr** without meds==; mask **5 days** · Paxlovid **≥ 12 yr + ≥ 40 kg** · remdesivir **≥ 28 days + ≥ 3 kg** |
| Lice / pinworm retreatment | Permethrin again in **1 wk** · mebendazole again in **2 wk** |
| Giardia | **3** stool samples over several days |

**Drugs:** **Acetaminophen** for viral fever — ==**never aspirin**== (Reye) · **Erythromycin / PCN G + antitoxin (DAT)** — diphtheria · **Acyclovir** — viral meningitis (IV), herpetic stomatitis · **Vitamin A × 2 days** — measles · **Mupirocin** — topical, nonbullous impetigo; **cephalexin** PO if many lesions/systemic; bullous → PO · **Permethrin** — scabies, lice · **Mebendazole** — pinworm; ==not with metronidazole== · **Metronidazole** — giardia

**Report now:** gray throat pseudomembrane + airway compromise (diphtheria) · ==petechial rash + fever== (meningococcal) · ↑ ICP signs · sudden LUQ/left-shoulder pain in mono (splenic rupture) · febrile seizure → 911 · leg weakness/paralysis (polio) · tea-colored urine, puffy eyes after impetigo (APSGN) `[ADDED]`

**Contrasts:** measles (Koplik spots, hairline → down) · roseola (high fever **then** rash on trunk) · fifth (slapped cheek → lacy rash) · varicella (itchy vesicles → scabs, trunk/face first) · HFMD (mouth ulcers + hand/foot rash) · bacterial conjunctivitis (thick purulent, crusted lashes) vs. viral (watery) vs. allergic (itchy, both eyes, no drainage)

**Mnemonic:** ==Hepatitis "vowels from the bowel"==: **A and E** = fecal-oral

**Vaccines:** only standard contraindication = ==severe allergic reaction== to vaccine/component · mild illness ≠ delay · moderate/severe illness = delay · live: MMR, varicella, rotavirus, LAIV → ==not in pregnancy or immunocompromise==

## I. Infection control

> [!quote] Anchor
> The nurse's main job is minimizing spread through infection control, plus health promotion like immunization.

#### Nurse's infection-control role?
- Prevent spread with proper **PPE**; teach child and family **hand hygiene, cough etiquette**, clean environment, and ==adherence to immunization recommendations==.

#### Contact vs. droplet vs. airborne?
| | Contact | Droplet | Airborne |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spread | Direct/indirect contact with child or environment | Close (**< 3 ft**) contact with respiratory secretions; don't stay infectious over distance | Particles suspended in air, ==travel long distances== |
| Room | Single preferred | Single preferred; no special ventilation | ==**Negative-pressure**== |
| PPE | ==Gown + gloves before entering==; remove all PPE in room right before exit | Standard face mask on entering | ==**N95**== or respirator per disease, on **before** entering; remove after leaving and closing door `[ADDED]` |
| Transport | — | Child wears surgical mask as tolerated | Child wears surgical mask `[ADDED]` |

#### PPE: order on and off?
| Donning | Doffing |
|---|---|
| 1 Hand hygiene | 1 ==**Gloves**== (most contaminated) |
| 2 Gown (full torso, fastened) | 2 Goggles/face shield: lift band from **back** |
| 3 Mask/respirator (snug) | 3 Gown: untie, pull from neck/shoulders, ==turn inside out==, roll into ball |
| 4 Goggles/face shield | 4 Mask **last**: ==bottom ties, then top== |
| 5 ==**Gloves**== over gown cuffs | 5 Hand hygiene |

- Front/sleeves of gown, outside of goggles, front of mask = **contaminated**. Hands contaminated mid-step → hand hygiene before the next step.

#### Nationally notifiable diseases?
- Must be reported to the **National Notifiable Diseases Surveillance System**: ==COVID-19, diphtheria, giardiasis, *H. influenzae*, hepatitis A/B/C, meningitis, pertussis, polio, rubella, tetanus, varicella== (and measles, slide 23).
- Three on the list with no slides `[ADDED]`:

| Disease `[ADDED]` | Key finding | Precaution / prevention |
|---|---|---|
| **Pertussis** (*Bordetella*) | Paroxysmal cough with inspiratory **"whoop"**, post-cough vomiting; ==infants: apnea== | Droplet; DTaP/Tdap, cocooning |
| **Rubella** (German measles) | Mild pink rash face → down, lymphadenopathy; ==danger = congenital rubella syndrome== | Droplet; keep away from pregnant people; MMR |
| **Tetanus** (*C. tetani* toxin in wound) | Muscle rigidity, **trismus** (lockjaw), spasms | Not person-to-person; DTaP/Tdap; tetanus IG + toxoid after dirty wound if not up to date |

## II. Communicable diseases

### Chickenpox (varicella)

#### Varicella: key facts?
| | |
|---|---|
| Cause / spread | **Varicella-zoster virus**; ==inhaled aerosolized virus or contact with vesicle fluid==; starts in upper respiratory mucosa |
| Course | Bloodstream at **2–6 days**; viremia + vesicles at **10–13 days**; rash lasts **5–7 days** |
| Findings | Itchy small blisters → scabs; starts on ==chest, back, face== then spreads; fever, fatigue, sore throat, headache. Lesions in all stages at once (macule → papule → vesicle → crust) `[ADDED]` |
| Diagnosis | Clinical; confirm with vesicle fluid or crust scraping; blood for acute immune response |
| Latency | ==IgG gives lasting immunity==; virus stays dormant in sensory nerves → can reactivate as **shingles (herpes zoster)** |

#### Treatment and nursing?
- ==**Acetaminophen**== for fever and pain. ==**Avoid aspirin**== in all childhood viral infections → **Reye syndrome**.
- Pruritus: **calamine**, lukewarm **oatmeal or baking soda baths**, oral/topical **antihistamines**; light cotton clothing; ==trim nails short== → ↓ scratching and **secondary infection**.
- Hydration (oral sores make eating hard), rest. Keep away from others ==until all blisters crusted==.
- Hospital: ==**airborne (negative-airflow) + contact**==. ==Pregnant nurse does not care for the client.==
- Prevention: **2-dose vaccine**, ↓ exposure, hand hygiene.

> [!danger] Report immediately
> **Reye syndrome** `[ADDED]`: acute encephalopathy + liver failure after a viral illness (varicella, flu) treated with aspirin. Early: ==persistent vomiting==, lethargy → confusion, combativeness, seizures. Teach families to check OTC labels for salicylates (e.g., bismuth subsalicylate).

### Diphtheria

#### Diphtheria: key facts?
| | |
|---|---|
| Cause / spread | *Corynebacterium diphtheriae*, gram-positive bacillus; ==exotoxin== causes the illness; inhaled particles |
| Risk | ==Not vaccinated with DTaP== (main risk); crowding, low SES, travel to endemic areas (SE Asia, Africa) |
| Incubation | **2–5 days** (range **1–10**) |
| Findings | Flu-like: sore throat, malaise, headache, cervical lymphadenopathy; ==**gray pseudomembrane** over throat/tonsils== |
| Complications | Pseudomembrane can ==obstruct the airway==; toxin spreads → **myocarditis**, **neuritis** |
| Diagnosis | Throat swab culture, **PCR**, toxin testing |

#### Treatment and nursing?
- ==**Contact + droplet** precautions==. Antibiotics ASAP: **erythromycin** or **penicillin G**; **vancomycin/linezolid** if resistant. Plus **diphtheria antitoxin (DAT)** to neutralize circulating toxin.
- Hospitalize to ==monitor respiratory and cardiac complications==. Soft diet + fluids (painful swallowing), rest, emotional support, acetaminophen.
- Prevention: **DTaP**.

### Mumps

#### Mumps: key facts?
| | |
|---|---|
| Cause / spread | Paramyxovirus; upper airway mucosa + regional nodes; ==respiratory droplets==, saliva, fomites |
| Timing | Contagious ==**1–2 days before** to **5 days after**== onset; incubation **7–21 days** |
| Findings | **Parotitis** (swollen parotid gland at the angle of the jaw, unilateral or bilateral; earache, painful chewing `[ADDED]`); fever, headache, anorexia, myalgia, fatigue |
| Complications | **Orchitis** (painful testicular swelling `[ADDED]`; ovaries can also inflame), **meningitis**, **encephalitis** |
| Diagnosis | Exposure history + findings; **RT-PCR** from buccal swab or **IgM** serology |

#### Treatment and nursing?
- Rest, hydration, analgesics, ==hot/cold compresses==, **soft diet** (chewing hurts). Isolation until the contagious period ends (droplet `[ADDED]`).
- Prevention: **MMR**; outbreak control (isolation, vaccination campaigns).

### Measles (rubeola)

#### Measles: key facts?
| | |
|---|---|
| Cause / spread | Morbillivirus; enters nasopharynx/conjunctiva → lymph nodes → liver, spleen, marrow; ==breath, cough, sneeze; stays in air up to **2 hr**== |
| Incubation | **10–14 days** |
| Findings | Prodrome: ==fever, cough, rhinorrhea, conjunctivitis== ("3 Cs" + fever `[ADDED]`) → ==**Koplik spots**== (red spots with white centers in mouth) → rash ==from hairline downward== |
| Danger | **Immune amnesia**: wipes out existing immune memory → vulnerable to other infections for months–years `[ADDED for definition]`; major global mortality |
| Diagnosis | Clinical; confirm with serology or PCR; plaque reduction neutralization assay |

#### Treatment and nursing?
- ==No antiviral → supportive only==. **Airborne precautions** if hospitalized.
- **Vitamin A × 2 days**: ↓ severity and death `[ADDED for rationale]`; age-based dosing (**50,000 / 100,000 / 200,000 IU** for < 6 mo / 6–11 mo / ≥ 12 mo) `[ADDED — verify vs course materials]`.
- Fever: antipyretics, light clothing, cool room. Cough/congestion: ==cool-mist humidifier== or shower steam. Rest; fluids (water, ORS, diluted juice, popsicles, gelatin). Seek care for trouble breathing or severe dehydration.
- Prevention: **MMR**. ==Nationally notifiable==; public health: case finding, isolation, contact tracing, vaccination campaigns.

> [!tip] NCLEX tip
> Client admitted with rubeola → ==**airborne**==: negative-pressure room, N95 on before entering. `[slide 24]`

#### Rubeola vs. rubella?
| | Rubeola (measles) | Rubella (German measles) `[ADDED]` |
|---|---|---|
| Severity | Serious; immune amnesia | Mild in children |
| Key sign | Koplik spots, 3 Cs | Postauricular/occipital lymph nodes |
| Precaution | **Airborne** | **Droplet** |
| Big risk | Pneumonia, encephalitis `[ADDED]` | ==Congenital rubella syndrome== in pregnancy |

### Poliomyelitis

#### Polio: key facts?
| | |
|---|---|
| Cause / spread | Enterovirus; enters mouth → oropharynx + GI tract → ==nervous system (spinal motor neurons)==; ==**fecal-oral**== + nasopharyngeal secretions |
| Shedding | Nasopharynx **1–2 wk**; ==stool for weeks== |
| Risk | Unvaccinated/under-vaccinated infants, young children, immunocompromised |
| Findings | Flu-like **2–5 days**; ==muscle weakness or paralysis, especially legs==; severe → respiratory failure |
| Diagnosis | Exam; throat swab, stool, CSF for virus; **MRI/EMG** for nerve/muscle damage |

#### Treatment and nursing?
- Supportive. ==**Contact precautions**==. Rest, nutrition, hydration, pain control, emotional support.
- **PT** (prevent atrophy and deformity), **OT** (adapt to disability), braces/wheelchair, orthopedics; ==respiratory support up to mechanical ventilation== if breathing muscles affected.
- Prevention: **IPV** (oral polio vaccine no longer used in U.S.).

### COVID-19 (SARS-CoV-2)

#### COVID-19: key facts?
| | |
|---|---|
| Spread | Direct contact, respiratory droplets; multisystem; replication damages tissue; immune response (T cells, monocytes, neutrophils, cytokines) |
| Findings | Fever, cough, respiratory issues; often ==asymptomatic== in children |
| **MIS-C** | ==Weeks after infection==: widespread inflammation of heart, lungs, kidneys, GI; rare but severe → ==immediate care== |
| Long COVID (PASC) | Persistent fatigue, breathing trouble, neurologic issues |
| Diagnosis | **PCR** or rapid antigen (NP swab); antibody testing |

#### Treatment and prevention?
| Drug | Who | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Nirmatrelvir/ritonavir (Paxlovid)** | ==Nonhospitalized, **≥ 12 yr** and **≥ 40 kg**==, mild–moderate, high risk | Start within **5 days** of symptoms; many CYP3A drug interactions → reconcile meds; altered taste `[ADDED]` |
| **Remdesivir** | ==Hospitalized needing O₂==; **≥ 28 days** and **≥ 3 kg** | IV; monitor LFTs, infusion reactions `[ADDED]` |

- Supportive: respiratory support, **supplemental O₂**, OTC antipyretics, rest, fluids, emotional support.
- Isolate until ==fever-free **24 hr** without medication==; mask **5 days** after testing positive. Hand hygiene, **6 ft** distance, well-fitting masks. [NOTE: current practice: CDC's 2024 guidance is stay home until symptoms improve and fever-free 24 hr without meds, then extra precautions (masking, distancing) for the next 5 days.]
- Vaccine for ==everyone **≥ 6 months**==; doses depend on age and immune status. [NOTE: current practice: the January 2026 CDC schedule no longer recommends COVID-19 vaccine universally for children (individual decision / high-risk); AAP still recommends it. Verify.]

### Meningitis

#### Viral vs. bacterial: cause and spread?
| | Viral | Bacterial |
|---|---|---|
| Organisms | Enteroviruses, herpesviruses, adenoviruses | ==*N. meningitidis*, *S. pneumoniae*, group B strep== |
| Spread | Mouth/nose; mucus, saliva, stool | Saliva, nasal discharge, feces, shared drinks/utensils; nasopharynx → blood → across BBB; or ==spread from sinusitis/otitis media== |
| Course | ==Usually self-limiting==; serious in newborns, **< 5 yr**, immunocompromised | ==More severe==: rapid **sepsis, hearing loss, hydrocephalus, death**; ==medical emergency== |

#### What does meningitis look like?
- Both: acute onset from meningeal inflammation + ==↑ intracranial pressure==. Meningococcal → ==**petechial rash**==.
- Classic signs `[ADDED]`: fever, headache, **nuchal rigidity**, photophobia, vomiting, **Kernig** (pain extending knee with hip flexed) and **Brudzinski** (neck flexion → hips/knees flex). ==Infants: bulging fontanel==, high-pitched cry, poor feeding, irritability; may lack neck stiffness. Infants can't report headache; older children can.
- ↑ ICP `[ADDED]`: early irritability, headache, vomiting, ↓ LOC; infants bulging fontanel, ↑ head circumference; ==late: Cushing triad== (bradycardia, ↑ SBP with widening pulse pressure, irregular breathing).

#### How is it diagnosed?
- **Lumbar puncture** for CSF (culture, cells, glucose, protein) + CBC. Position side-lying knee-chest (infant may sit flexed) `[ADDED]`.

| CSF `[ADDED]` | Bacterial | Viral |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance | Cloudy | Clear |
| WBC | ↑↑ (neutrophils) | ↑ (lymphocytes) |
| Glucose | ==↓== | Normal |
| Protein | ↑ | Normal / slightly ↑ |

#### Treatment and nursing?
| | Viral | Bacterial |
|---|---|---|
| Treatment | ==Antibiotics until bacterial is ruled out==; IV **acyclovir** | Hospitalize; ==IV antibiotics== (empiric, then tailored to culture); antipyretics/analgesics; **corticosteroids** (↓ brain inflammation, ↓ hearing loss); ==prophylactic antibiotics for close contacts== |
| Nursing | Assist with diagnostics, manage symptoms, hydration, watch for complications, prevent spread | ==Immediate hospitalization==, IV antibiotics, supportive care, VS/pain/temp, hydration, ==monitor for ↑ ICP== |
| Prevention | Hygiene, avoid contact with infected | **Vaccine** (Hib, PCV, MenACWY `[ADDED]`); early treatment of respiratory/throat infections |

- Bacterial (meningococcal) → **droplet precautions** until **24 hr** of effective antibiotics `[ADDED]`.

> [!danger] Report immediately
> Fever + ==petechial/purpuric rash== = possible meningococcemia → emergency. Any ↑ ICP signs (bulging fontanel, ↓ LOC, Cushing triad) → notify now `[ADDED for ICP specifics]`.

### Hepatitis

#### Hepatitis A, B, C: compare?
| | Hep A | Hep B | Hep C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spread | ==Contaminated food/water (fecal-oral)== | ==Blood/body fluids== | ==Blood/body fluids== |
| Incubation | **4 wk** | **12 wk** | **8 wk** |
| Findings | N/V, diarrhea | Prodrome: malaise, ↓ appetite → illness: ==RUQ pain== → icteric: ==jaundice==, painful hepatomegaly, ==pale stool, dark urine== | Mostly ==asymptomatic==; malaise, ↓ appetite |
| Tests | Viral markers, LFTs | **HBsAg** (+ = current infection `[ADDED]`), immunoglobulins | **Anti-HCV**; liver biopsy |
| Vaccine | ==Yes== | ==Yes== | ==No==: avoid blood exposure |

- Types A–E exist; A, B, C most common. Risks: unsafe sex, IV drug use, poor sanitation, blood/fluid exposure. Some Hep B → **chronic**.

> [!example] Mnemonic — hepatitis spread
> ==If it starts with a vowel, it comes from the bowel==: **A** and **E** are fecal-oral.

#### Treatment and nursing?
- Manage symptoms, prevent complications, ↓ viral load in chronic cases: **antivirals, immunomodulators**, liver transplant.
- Nursing: VS, hydration, nutrition; ==bed rest in acute hepatitis==; education; hand hygiene.
- Newborn of HBsAg+ mother: **hep B vaccine + HBIG within 12 hr** of birth `[ADDED — verify vs course materials]`.

### Mononucleosis

#### Mono: key facts?
| | |
|---|---|
| Cause / spread | ==**Epstein-Barr virus**==; ==saliva ("kissing disease")==, shared cups/utensils |
| Who | Adolescents and adults ==**15–24 yr**== |
| Timing | Incubation **3–6 wk**; high viral levels up to **6 mo** |
| Findings | Fever, **pharyngitis**, ==fatigue==, lymphadenopathy, headache, malaise; ==enlarged tonsils with exudate==; ==enlarged liver/spleen== |
| Complications (rare) | Airway obstruction, ==**splenic rupture**==, encephalitis, Guillain-Barré |
| Diagnosis | **Monospot** (heterophile antibody); ↑ lymphocytes |

#### Treatment and nursing?
- Self-limiting: rest, fluids (water, broth, electrolytes), cool soft foods, **NSAIDs/antipyretics**, salt-water gargles, lozenges. Limit contact while contagious; no sharing cups/utensils.
- ==No active or contact sports **4–6 wk**== (splenomegaly).
- Adolescent impact: social isolation, missed school, mood → ==keep peer contact digitally==, schoolwork at home, enjoyable activities.

> [!danger] Report immediately
> Splenic rupture `[ADDED for signs]`: sudden sharp ==LUQ pain radiating to the left shoulder== (Kehr sign), dizziness, tachycardia, pallor (shock). Teach family to report at once.

### Roseola infantum (exanthem subitum, "sixth disease")

#### Roseola: key facts?
| | |
|---|---|
| Cause / spread | **HHV-6**; saliva and respiratory droplets; replicates in leukocytes, salivary glands |
| Who | ==Infants **6–12 mo**==; **90%** < 2 yr; ↑ in females, children with older siblings; spring/fall |
| Findings | ==Sudden fever **≥ 104°F (40°C)** for **3–5 days**==; irritability, periorbital edema, ear pain, anorexia, cough, rhinorrhea, cervical nodes → ==rash appears **after fever breaks**==: small rose-pink maculopapular, trunk → neck, face, limbs |
| Contagious | Highly, ==before diagnosis== (nonspecific irritability) → handwashing |
| Diagnosis | Clinical: fever then rash |
| Immunity | Lifelong after recovery; reinfection rare except immunocompromised |

#### Treatment and nursing?
- Supportive: fluids (breast milk/formula; electrolyte solution), antipyretics, loose light clothing.
- ==Limit contact until **24 hr** after fever subsides.==
- Teach ==febrile seizure== signs and actions; ==call emergency services== if one occurs.

> [!warning] Priority
> Febrile seizure `[ADDED for actions]`: stay with child, side-lying, ==nothing in the mouth==, loosen clothing, time it; call 911 (course instruction). Usually ages 6 mo–5 yr, brief, generalized.

### Fifth disease (erythema infectiosum)

#### Fifth disease: key facts?
| | |
|---|---|
| Cause / spread | **Parvovirus B19**; viremia **5–10 days** after exposure; droplets, direct contact, blood |
| Who | Children **5–15 yr**; spring/early summer |
| Findings | Flu-like (fever, malaise, headache, myalgia, N/V/D) → ==**"slapped cheek"**== facial rash (~**5 days**) → ==non-itchy lacy== maculopapular rash on trunk/limbs; **arthropathy** (more in females, up to **3 wk**) |
| Contagious | ==Before the rash==; once rash appears, ==no longer contagious== |
| Diagnosis | Characteristic rashes; B19 **IgM** serology |
| High risk | ==Pregnancy (2nd trimester)== → fetal anemia/hydrops; ==sickle cell / hemolytic disorders== → aplastic crisis `[ADDED for mechanisms]`; immunocompromised |

#### Treatment and nursing?
- Supportive: fever, joint pain, rest, fluids. Child can return once rash appears (not contagious); avoid contact with infected people.

### Hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD)

#### HFMD: key facts?
| | |
|---|---|
| Cause / spread | Enterovirus / **coxsackievirus**; respiratory/oral secretions, vesicle fluid; ==stool shedding **~6 wk**==, oropharynx **~4 wk** |
| Who | ==**< 5 yr**==; late spring–early summer; schools, daycare |
| Timing | Incubation **3–6 days**; ==most contagious first **7 days**==; illness **7–10 days** |
| Findings | Low-grade fever, sore throat, malaise; ==painful mouth ulcers== (red border, white/gray-yellow base); maculopapular or vesicular rash on ==hands and feet== (also buttocks, perineum, limbs) |
| Diagnosis | Clinical; stool sample; vesicle scrapings to rule out varicella/HSV |

#### Treatment and nursing?
- Self-limiting. ==Fluids== (ORS, popsicles), cold soft foods; ==avoid hot drinks and acidic juices==; analgesics/antipyretics; skin hygiene; rest.
- Prevention: hygiene, especially handwashing after diapers `[ADDED for diapers]`.

### Impetigo

#### Impetigo: key facts?
| | |
|---|---|
| Cause | Bacterial skin infection: ==**group A beta-hemolytic strep**== and/or ==***Staph aureus***==; highly contagious |
| Who | **2–5 yr**; summer and fall |
| Primary vs. secondary | Primary: intact skin. Secondary: infects an existing wound |
| Diagnosis | Clinical; culture; biopsy |

#### Nonbullous vs. bullous?
| | Nonbullous (most common) | Bullous |
|---|---|---|
| Lesions | Vesicles/pustules rupture → ==**honey-colored crusts**==, face first then elsewhere | Small vesicles merge into **large bullae**; fluid clear/yellow → purulent/dark; rupture ==without honey crust== |
| Other | ± nearby lymphadenopathy | ± lymphadenopathy, **fever** |
| Treatment | Topical **mupirocin**; + oral **cephalexin** if many satellite lesions or systemic signs | ==Oral antibiotics== |

#### Nursing and prevention?
- ==Parent education + medication adherence== (finish course) to ↓ complications, including rheumatic fever. [NOTE: current practice: rheumatic fever follows strep **pharyngitis**, not skin infection; APSGN is the impetigo complication.]
- Teach **acute post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis (APSGN)** signs `[ADDED]`: ==tea/cola-colored urine==, periorbital/facial edema, ↑ BP, ↓ urine output, about **1–3 wk** after infection `[ADDED — verify]`.
- Hygiene, ==cover lesions==, wash clothing and linens to prevent reinfection; nails short `[ADDED]`.

### Conjunctivitis (pink eye)

#### Four types: compare?
| | Bacterial | Viral | Allergic / foreign body |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cause | *M. catarrhalis*, *S. pneumoniae*, *H. influenzae*; ==chlamydia at vaginal birth== | ==**Adenovirus**== (most), HSV, enteroviruses | IgE reaction to allergen / object in eye |
| Spread | Contact, touching eye | Hand-eye, contaminated surfaces | ==Not communicable== |
| Findings | Redness, gritty/itchy; ==thick purulent drainage that crusts lashes== | Redness, ==watery== discharge, itching/tearing; recent URI | Allergic: itching, tearing, lid edema, burning, ==no drainage, both eyes==. FB: sudden redness, tearing, gritty |
| Diagnosis | Discharge sample | ELISA | History, exam; FB: ==check for corneal abrasion== |
| Treatment | ==Topical antibiotic drops/ointment== | Resolves on own; artificial tears; antiviral drops if HSV | Allergic: antihistamine drops, topical NSAIDs, oral antihistamines. FB: ==flush with water/saline==, remove if possible; NSAIDs for pain |

- Newborn prophylaxis: **erythromycin eye ointment** at birth `[ADDED]`.
- Comfort: cool/warm compresses with a ==clean washcloth each time==, gentle cleansing, artificial tears. Bacterial and viral are ==highly contagious==: hand hygiene, no eye rubbing, ==don't share towels or eye makeup==; temporary isolation for viral. Allergic: avoid allergens, allergy shots.

#### Teaching hygiene by age?
| Age | Approach |
|---|---|
| Toddler | Simple phrases ("germs make eyes sick"); demonstrate with a toy/puppet |
| Preschool | Story about a character who washes hands; colorful charts/stickers |
| School-age | Explain germs and infection; role-play handwashing |
| Adolescent | ==Personal responsibility==; peer discussions |

### Stomatitis

#### Herpetic vs. aphthous?
| | Herpetic gingivostomatitis | Aphthous stomatitis (canker sore) |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | ==**HSV-1/HSV-2**==; dormant, reactivates with illness/stress | Inflammatory; genetic, immune, environmental; triggers: stress, nutrient deficits, oral trauma |
| Contagious | ==Yes== (saliva, lesions) | ==No== |
| Findings | Painful vesicles/ulcers on gums, tongue, cheeks; swollen bleeding gums, ==high fever==, irritability, trouble eating/drinking | Painful ==flat, round ulcers==, white-gray/yellow center, red border; inner lips, cheeks, tongue, floor of mouth |
| Diagnosis | Viral culture of ulcer | Clinical; biopsy to rule out other causes |
| Treatment | ==Acyclovir== (topical/PO), pain meds, infection control | Topical/oral analgesics, topical corticosteroids, avoid spicy/acidic foods, stop smoking; soft toothbrush |
| Prevention | Avoid contact with active lesions | Manage triggers; B vitamins, vitamin C, zinc |

> [!warning] Priority
> Both are very painful → ↓ oral intake → ==dehydration==. Encourage fluids; foods ==neutral temperature==, not spicy or acidic.

### Rash discrimination

#### Which rash is which?
| Disease | Rash | Clue |
|---|---|---|

## III. Immunity, immunotherapy, and vaccines

#### Antibodies: types and roles?
- Proteins made by **B cells**: neutralize pathogens, tag them for destruction, trigger immune response.

| Type | Role |
|---|---|
| **IgG** | ==Long-term immunity==; most abundant; crosses placenta `[ADDED]` |
| **IgM** | ==First produced== in new infection |
| **IgA** | Secretions (saliva); mucosal immunity |
| **IgE** | ==Allergy== and parasites |

- Vaccines → **plasma cells** (antibodies) + ==**memory B cells**== (long-term protection).

#### Antigen, antitoxin, toxoid, attenuated?
| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Antigen** | Provokes immune response; activates T cells. Exogenous (vaccine target) vs. autoantigen (autoimmune disease) | Vaccine antigens |
| **Antitoxin** | Antibodies that ==bind and inactivate toxins==; given = ==immediate passive immunity== | Botulism, tetanus, snake venom, diphtheria (DAT) |
| **Toxoid** | ==Inactivated bacterial toxin== that still triggers immunity (antitoxin production) | ==Diphtheria, tetanus== |
| **Attenuated** | ==Live, weakened== pathogen; long-lasting immunity; ==may not suit immunocompromised== | MMR, varicella; rotavirus (oral) |

#### Cocooning?
- Protect vulnerable people (newborns, infants, immunocompromised) by ==vaccinating close contacts and family==. Classic: Tdap for pregnant people and household contacts of newborns (pertussis) `[ADDED]`. Limits: hard to reach full coverage, time to organize.

#### Types of immunity?
| Type | How | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| **Natural (innate)** | From birth; skin, mucous membranes, secretions (tears, saliva, stomach acid); phagocytes; inflammation, cytokines | Rapid, nonspecific; no long-term protection |
| **Acquired active** | Body makes antibodies after infection or ==vaccine== | Long-term (memory cells) |
| **Passive natural** | ==Placenta, breast milk== | Temporary |
| **Passive artificial** | ==Immune globulin== (human or animal) | Immediate, temporary; ==no memory cells== |
| **Herd** | Enough of population immune → indirect protection | Threshold depends on contagiousness; threatened by hesitancy, access, waning immunity |

#### Immunobiologics and immunoglobulin therapy?
- Immunobiologics: vaccines, **immunomodulators** (suppress/enhance immunity: autoimmune, immunodeficiency), **monoclonal antibodies** (cancer, autoimmune, infections), adoptive T-cell therapy.
- **Immunoglobulin therapy**: antibodies from donor plasma, ==IV (IVIG)== or injection (e.g., **HBIG** after hep B exposure); for those who can't make enough antibodies (e.g., **SCID**) and **Kawasaki disease**.
- **SCID** `[ADDED]`: inherited absence of T/B-cell function → severe infections; ==no live vaccines==.

#### Vaccine types?
| Type | What | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Combination** | Several vaccines in one shot → fewer injections, better adherence | ==MMRV== |
| **Conjugate** | Polysaccharide linked to carrier protein → better response to encapsulated bacteria, ==young children==; may need booster | ==Hib== |
| **Monovalent** | One pathogen/strain | SARS-CoV-2 |
| **Polyvalent** | Multiple strains | ==Influenza== |

- Barriers to immunization: ==vaccine hesitancy==, limited access, misinformation.

### Giving vaccines

#### Contraindication vs. precaution?
- **Contraindication** = permanent exclusion. ==Only standard contraindication: severe allergic reaction to a prior dose or component.== Ask allergy status and prior reactions ==before every vaccine==.
- **Precaution** = ↑ risk of adverse reaction; e.g., ==personal/family history of seizures → MMRV==.
- ==Mild illness (ear infection) → vaccinate anyway.== **Moderate/severe illness → delay.**

> [!tip] NCLEX tip
> Child with a mild cold or ear infection, with or without low-grade fever, ==still gets scheduled vaccines==. Moderate/severe illness = postpone.

#### Atraumatic care during immunization?
- Named in objective 3, not taught in the deck. Fill `[ADDED]`:
- **Atraumatic care** = minimizing physical and psychological distress of procedures.
- Infants: ==breastfeed or give sucrose== during shots; swaddle/hold. All ages: **topical anesthetic** (EMLA/lidocaine) ahead of time, ==comfort positioning on caregiver's lap==, distraction (bubbles, video), honest brief preparation, praise after; give ==most painful vaccine last==; inject quickly without aspirating.
- Site: ==**vastus lateralis**== for infants/toddlers; **deltoid** once muscle is adequate (~3 yr+).
- Give the **Vaccine Information Statement**, document lot/site, observe **15 min** (adolescent syncope), keep **epinephrine** ready for anaphylaxis.

#### Routine schedule?
| Vaccine | Schedule | Key cautions |
|---|---|---|
| **Hep B** | ==Birth (within **24 hr**)==, **1–2 mo**, **6–18 mo** (4th dose OK with combos) | — |
| **Rotavirus** (live, oral) | **2, 4** (± **6**) mo; ==start before **15 wk**, finish by **8 mo**== | ==Contraindicated: history of intussusception, SCID== |
| **DTaP → Tdap** | DTaP **2, 4, 6, 15–18 mo, 4–6 yr**; Tdap **11–12 yr**, then every **10 yr** | Precaution: history of **Guillain-Barré** |
| **Hib** | **2, 4** (± **6**) mo; booster **12–15 mo** | — |
| **PCV** | **2, 4, 6** mo; booster **12–15 mo** | High-risk may add PPSV |
| **IPV** | **2, 4, 6–18 mo, 4–6 yr** (≥ **6 mo** between 3rd and 4th) | — |
| **MMR** (live) | **12–15 mo**, **4–6 yr** | ==Contraindicated: pregnancy, immunocompromise, neomycin allergy== |
| **Varicella** (live) | **12–15 mo**, **4–6 yr** | ==Contraindicated: pregnancy, gelatin or neomycin allergy== |
| **Hep A** | **12–23 mo**; 2nd ≥ **6 mo** later | — |
| **Influenza** | ==Yearly, **≥ 6 mo**==; single dose if **≥ 9 yr** | **LAIV** (live, nasal): no if gelatin allergy or immunosuppressed |
| **HPV** | Ages **9–45**; 2 or 3 doses by start age | Precaution: severe **yeast** allergy |
| **Meningococcal** | **11–12 yr** + booster **16**; one dose if first at ≥ **16** | — |
| **COVID-19** | Varies by age, history, product | ==**Myocarditis**== reported, esp. **≥ 12 yr** |

- Fills `[ADDED]`: **Intussusception** = bowel telescoping into itself: sudden ==crampy pain with knees to chest, "currant jelly" stools==, sausage-shaped mass → report (teach after rotavirus vaccine). **Guillain-Barré** = ascending paralysis after infection/vaccine. Flu vaccine: children **6 mo–8 yr** need **2 doses** the first season `[ADDED]`.
- Follow current CDC guidance and manufacturer instructions.
- [NOTE: current practice: the January 2026 CDC childhood schedule moved influenza, COVID-19, hepatitis A, hepatitis B, rotavirus, and meningococcal vaccines from universal to high-risk / shared decision-making; the AAP 2026 schedule still recommends them routinely. Course schedule is what's tested.]
- [NOTE: current practice: CDC lists severe yeast allergy as an HPV **contraindication**; routine HPV is **11–12** (from 9), catch-up through **26**, shared decision **27–45**.]

#### Why are vaccine rates lower in some groups, and what helps?
| Barrier | Strategy |
|---|---|
| Cultural beliefs, language | Cultural sensitivity training, ==interpreters and multilingual materials==, community leaders |
| Cost, access, transportation, rigid work hours | Free/low-cost clinics, ==evening/weekend hours== |
| Low health literacy, misinformation | Targeted education; ==actively correct myths with accurate information== |

## IV. Parasitic infestations and infections

#### Scabies?
| | |
|---|---|
| Cause | *Sarcoptes scabiei* mite ==burrows under skin and lays eggs==; lives **1–2 mo** |
| Spread | ==Prolonged skin-to-skin contact (~**10 min**)==, linens; crowded settings (schools, healthcare) |
| Findings | ==Intense itching, worse at night==; wrists, genitals, ==between fingers==; tiny linear burrows |
| Diagnosis | Exam + history; skin scraping microscopy (mites, eggs, feces) |
| Treatment | ==**Permethrin cream**==; ==treat close contacts too== |
| Nursing | Handwashing, avoid skin contact; ==wash clothes/bedding in hot water==; cool compresses, oatmeal baths; nails short (scratching → impetigo, cellulitis); isolation protocols in facilities |

- Permethrin `[ADDED — verify]`: apply neck down (include head in infants), leave **8–14 hr**, rinse, repeat in **1 wk**; ==itching can last **2–4 wk** after cure== (not treatment failure).

#### Lice (pediculosis)?
| | |
|---|---|
| Types | Head (scalp), body (lives in clothing), pubic ("crab"; also eyebrows, armpits) |
| Spread | Close contact, ==sharing hats, brushes, bedding==; schools |
| Findings | ==Pruritus== (hypersensitivity), **2–6 wk** after exposure; behind ears, nape of neck; ==**nits**: oval, firmly stuck to hair shaft near scalp== (dandruff flakes off); red sores from scratching |
| Treatment | OTC/prescription pediculicide; ==**permethrin**, repeat in **1 wk**== (resistance reported) |
| Nursing | ==Fine-tooth nit comb==; launder in hot water, vacuum; items that can't be washed: seal in a bag **2 wk** `[ADDED — verify]`; ==don't share headgear== |

> [!warning] Priority
> ==**Lindane** and **ivermectin**: neurotoxicity → avoid or use with caution in children.== [NOTE: current practice: lindane carries a boxed warning; topical ivermectin 0.5% lotion is FDA-approved for head lice in children ≥ 6 mo.]

#### Pinworms?
| | |
|---|---|
| Cause | *Enterobius vermicularis*: small white threadlike worms; ==swallowed eggs== from hands, bedding, surfaces |
| Cycle | Ingestion → adult **4–8 wk**; ==females lay eggs around the anus at night== → scratching moves eggs under nails → reinfection |
| Findings | ==Intense perianal itching at night==; irritability from poor sleep |
| Diagnosis | ==**Tape test** or pinworm paddle on perianal area, early morning== before bathing/toileting `[ADDED for before bathing]` |
| Treatment | ==**Mebendazole**==, 2nd dose in **2 wk**; ==treat the whole household==; not with **metronidazole** (risk of severe skin reaction, SJS/TEN `[ADDED]`) |
| Nursing | ==Morning shower== removes eggs; handwashing, nails short; hot-water laundry |

#### Giardiasis?
| | |
|---|---|
| Cause | *Giardia duodenalis* (protozoan): damages intestinal lining → diarrhea, malabsorption |
| Spread | ==Fecal-oral==; contaminated water and food |
| Findings | Asymptomatic → acute or chronic watery diarrhea, cramps, bloating, gas, fatigue, weight loss; kids: mild diarrhea + abdominal discomfort |
| Diagnosis | Stool DFA/EIA for cysts or trophozoites; ==**3 samples** over several days== |
| Treatment | **Metronidazole**; hydration, nutrition |
| Nursing | Hand hygiene; ==dairy sensitivity may last a few months== → limit dairy |
| Prevention | ==Safe water==: filters or boiling; sanitation |

## V. Clinical judgment: priorities across the module

#### Which findings come first?
| Finding | Why first |
|---|---|
| Diphtheria with stridor/drooling | ==Airway obstruction== from pseudomembrane |
| Fever + petechial rash, nuchal rigidity, bulging fontanel | Bacterial meningitis = emergency; ↑ ICP |
| Mono with sudden LUQ/shoulder pain | Splenic rupture → shock |
| Polio with weak breathing | Respiratory failure |
| Febrile seizure | Airway/safety, then 911 per course |
| Measles/varicella admission | ==Airborne room before anything else== |
| Pregnant staff + varicella (or fifth `[ADDED]`) | Reassign |

- Evaluate with outcomes: afebrile, hydrated (moist mucosa, UO), lesions healing, no spread to contacts, family teach-back on hygiene and meds.

## Self-quiz

> [!question]- 1. Order for removing PPE?
> Gloves → goggles/face shield → gown → mask (bottom ties, then top) → hand hygiene.

> [!question]- 2. Precautions for a child admitted with measles?
> Airborne: negative-pressure room, N95 on before entering.

> [!question]- 3. Precautions for varicella? For diphtheria? For polio?
> Varicella: airborne + contact. Diphtheria: contact + droplet. Polio: contact.

> [!question]- 4. Parent asks to give aspirin for a child's chickenpox fever. Response?
> No: aspirin in viral illness risks Reye syndrome. Use acetaminophen.

> [!question]- 5. When can a child with chickenpox return to school?
> When all lesions have crusted over.

> [!question]- 6. Hallmark finding of diphtheria, and the main danger?
> Gray pseudomembrane over throat/tonsils; airway obstruction (also myocarditis).

> [!question]- 7. What are Koplik spots, and which disease?
> Small red spots with white centers in the mouth; measles.

> [!question]- 8. What supplement is given in measles?
> Vitamin A for 2 days.

> [!question]- 9. Infant with 3 days of 104°F fever, now afebrile with a pink trunk rash. Likely diagnosis and teaching?
> Roseola. Rash after fever breaks; contagious until 24 hr after fever; teach febrile-seizure response.

> [!question]- 10. Child with slapped-cheek rash: can she go to school? Who is at risk?
> Yes; no longer contagious once the rash appears. Risk: pregnant people, sickle cell/hemolytic disorders, immunocompromised.

> [!question]- 11. 17-yr-old with mono asks about football. Teaching?
> No active or contact sports for 4–6 wk; report sudden LUQ or left-shoulder pain (splenic rupture).

> [!question]- 12. CSF is cloudy with low glucose and high neutrophils. Which meningitis, and what for contacts?
> Bacterial; close contacts get prophylactic antibiotics.

> [!question]- 13. Which hepatitis types spread fecal-oral? Which has no vaccine?
> A and E ("vowels from the bowel"); hepatitis C has no vaccine.

> [!question]- 14. Honey-crusted lesions around a 3-yr-old's nose. Diagnosis, treatment, complication to teach?
> Nonbullous impetigo; topical mupirocin (± oral cephalexin); watch for APSGN (tea-colored urine, puffy eyes).

> [!question]- 15. Eye with thick yellow drainage crusting the lashes vs. watery, itchy eyes in both eyes with no drainage?
> Bacterial conjunctivitis (antibiotic drops) vs. allergic (antihistamine, avoid allergen).

> [!question]- 16. Which stomatitis is contagious, and what treats it?
> Herpetic gingivostomatitis (HSV); acyclovir.

> [!question]- 17. Only standard contraindication to all vaccines?
> Severe allergic reaction to a previous dose or a vaccine component.

> [!question]- 18. 12-mo-old with an ear infection and low-grade fever is due for MMR. Action?
> Vaccinate; mild illness isn't a reason to delay.

> [!question]- 19. Which vaccines are live, and who can't get them?
> MMR, varicella, rotavirus, LAIV; not in pregnancy or immunocompromise.

> [!question]- 20. Rotavirus vaccine: age limits and contraindications?
> Start before 15 wk, finish by 8 mo; contraindicated with history of intussusception or SCID.

> [!question]- 21. Toxoid vs. antitoxin?
> Toxoid = inactivated toxin in a vaccine (active immunity: diphtheria, tetanus). Antitoxin = antibodies against a toxin (immediate passive immunity).

> [!question]- 22. Breastfed infant's protection is which type of immunity?
> Natural passive.

> [!question]- 23. When is a pinworm tape test done, and who is treated?
> Early morning before bathing; mebendazole for the whole household, repeat in 2 wk.

> [!question]- 24. Scabies teaching after permethrin?
> Treat close contacts too, hot-water laundry, itching may persist for weeks, nails short.

> [!question]- 25. Child recovering from giardiasis has cramps after milk. Explanation?
> Temporary dairy sensitivity after infection; limit dairy for a few months.
