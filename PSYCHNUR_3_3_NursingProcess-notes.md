# The Nursing Process in Mental Health — PSYCHNUR 3.3

## Cram sheet

**Nursing process ↔ clinical judgment (CJAM)**

| RN process | PN process | CJAM step |
|---|---|---|
| Assessment | Data collection | Recognize cues |
| Analysis | — (RN) | Analyze cues + prioritize hypotheses |
| Planning | Planning | Generate solutions |
| Implementation | Implementation | Take action |
| Evaluation | Evaluation | Evaluate outcomes |

**Scope:** RN = comprehensive/initial assessment, analysis, priorities · PN = collects data, observes, **reports**; data collection ≠ assessment

**Priority:** ABCDE first → then **risk of harm to self/others** · "What causes the greatest harm if action is delayed?" · safety > finishing the interview

**Mood vs. affect:** mood = client's **reported** state · affect = nurse's **observed** expression → check congruence

**Process vs. content:** process = **how** ideas connect · content = **what** (delusions, SI/HI)

**Circumstantial vs. tangential:** circumstantial eventually **reaches** the point · tangential **never returns**

**THINC-MED (medical mimics):** Tumors · Hormonal · Infection · Nutritional · CNS · Misc · Electrolytes/Environmental toxins · Drugs

**Tools:** MMSE / MoCA (cognition) · FAD (family) · FICA (spiritual) · CFI (culture) · STAMP/Brøset (aggression, see 3.4)

**SMART:** Specific · Measurable · Attainable · Realistic · Timely — MH outcomes are **behavioral/functional**

**BIRP:** Behavior · Intervention · Response · Plan

**Discharge planning starts at admission.**

**Prevention:** when → primary/secondary/tertiary · who → universal (everyone) / selective (elevated risk) / indicated (very high risk or symptomatic)

## I. Framework: process + judgment + relationship

> [!quote] Anchor
> Clinical knowledge and human connection work together. The nursing process describes the steps; clinical judgment drives the reasoning.

#### Why does trust matter to assessment?
- Trust determines the quality of **disclosure**; the relationship can be therapeutic in itself.
- The process is **continuous and circular**: when the client changes, go back to assessment. Example `[Engage]`: calm client planned for rec therapy suddenly becomes combative → reassess, re-analyze, new plan (possibly medication); original outcome = not met.

#### CJAM six steps?
- **Recognize cues → analyze cues → prioritize hypotheses → generate solutions → take action → evaluate outcomes.**
- Strong nurses keep reassessing whether their interpretation still fits the evidence.
- `[Engage]` NCSBN grouping: recognize + analyze = **form** a hypothesis; prioritize + generate = **refine** it. Tanner's skills: noticing, interpreting, responding, reflecting.

#### RN vs. PN role?
- RN: comprehensive assessment, analysis, nursing judgments, priorities; **RN completes the initial/admission assessment** `[Engage]`.
- PN: collects data, observes change, reports; participates in planning, implementation, evaluation **within scope**.

> [!warning] Scope
> PN data collection **contributes to** the RN assessment but **does not replace it**. New admission → assign the RN.

#### What environmental and nurse factors affect judgment?

| Environmental | Nurse (the nurse is a clinical instrument) |
|---|---|
| Physical setting: privacy, safety, space, hazards | Knowledge and specialized skills |
| Staffing, equipment, resources | **Self-awareness** → catch bias before it affects care |
| Health record, labs, prior documentation | Experience → recognizes subtle change |
| Time pressure/urgency (STAT, change in condition) | **Stress and cognitive overload** impair reasoning |
| Cultural context, communication needs | Knowing when to ask for help = safe practice |
| Task complexity, risk to client/staff | |

## II. Assessment: foundations

#### Subjective vs. objective in psych?
- **Subjective** = client's reported experience; **objective** = what the nurse observes or measures.
- Sources: interview, observation, physical assessment, chart review. Psych assessment leans heavily on the client's description of **internal experience**. Ongoing, not a one-time admission task.

#### Purpose of the MH assessment?
- Clarify the presenting concern · identify **risks, needs, strengths, coping, supports** · explore history/contributors · anticipate needs and start the plan.
- ==Assess strengths — not just symptoms and deficits.== (attributes, resources, past successes, goals `[Engage]`)

#### How is rapport built?
- Consistency, empathy, respect, collaboration; client is an **active participant**. Stay calm, nonjudgmental; manage your own reactions so the client feels psychologically safe.
- `[Engage]` Introduce self and role · offer interpreter · watch nonverbal cues · no jargon · check own bias. Clients disclose less if the nurse seems rushed or judgmental.

#### Which communication technique when?

| Technique | Use |
|---|---|
| **Open-ended** | Elicits the client's story; lets you assess thought process |
| **Clarification** | Confirms you understood the meaning |
| **Closed** | Specific information — **suicide/self-harm questions**; clients with cognitive impairment `[Engage]` |
| **Summarizing** | Organizes and confirms understanding |

- Adapt to cognition, attention, development, clinical status.

#### Suicide and self-harm assessment?
- Ask **clearly and directly** about suicidal thoughts and self-harm when safety is uncertain. Don't let discomfort block it — clients want someone to ask.

> [!danger] Safety before completion
> Safety concerns take priority over finishing the routine interview. Findings drive **immediate protective actions and escalation**.

#### What to observe?
- Appearance/hygiene · behavior/activity · speech · mood/affect · thought process and content · perception, cognition, insight, judgment.

## III. Physical health, co-occurring conditions, trauma

> [!warning] Don't assume it's psychiatric
> Pain, sedation, or acute illness change behavior and participation. Rule out **medical causes, medication effects, substance use, comorbidities** before labeling a new symptom psychiatric. **>100** medical conditions can mimic mental illness `[Engage]`. Address physical distress before the psychosocial interview.

#### THINC-MED — what can mimic mental illness?
- **T** Tumors · **H** Hormonal/endocrine · **I** Infection (e.g., uremia, sepsis `[Engage]`) · **N** Nutritional deficiencies · **C** CNS conditions (neuro, head injury) · **M** Miscellaneous medical · **E** Electrolytes/environmental toxins · **D** Drugs, substances, med effects, polypharmacy.

> [!example] Worked example — sedated after a new med `[Engage]`
> Adolescent admitted for anger, started a new med for frustration. Next morning: lethargic, slow to answer, skipped breakfast; P **60**, BP **110/72**, RR **12**, pupils dilated and sluggish, reflexes sluggish.
> Reasoning `[ADDED]`: don't read this as behavior (avoidance, oppositionality). Cluster cues → possible **adverse drug effect/oversedation** ("D" in THINC-MED). Physiologic safety comes first: complete the neuro/respiratory check, withhold further sedating doses pending provider contact, report.

#### Co-occurring conditions?
- More than one psychiatric/medical condition is common; **screen for alcohol and substance use** on every client.
- One diagnosis distracts from another → the untreated one drives **relapse** `[Engage]`.

#### Trauma-informed assessment?
- Safety and trust are central; consider client, staff, family, environment, milieu.
- Predictable, respectful communication · avoid unnecessary pressure or **loss of control** · offer choice and collaboration.

#### Physical risk areas in clients with mental illness?

| Area | Why `[Engage]` |
|---|---|
| Oral/dental | Fear of dentist, gum disease, poor nutrition, substances, tobacco, med side effects → infection, CV disease, diabetes risk |
| Vision | Some psychotropics affect vision; some contraindicated in **glaucoma** |
| Sexual/reproductive | ↑ STI and sexual-violence risk (mania, substance use → impulsivity); meds affect sexual function or pregnancy |
| Tobacco | ↑ respiratory, CV, cancer risk; advise and support **cessation** |
| Alcohol/substances | Higher risk than general public; screen with tools |
| Metabolic/chronic | People with SMI have ↑ physical illness (metabolic syndrome) |

## IV. Mental status examination (MSE)

> [!quote] Anchor
> A structured snapshot of cognitive, emotional, behavioral, and perceptual functioning — combines subjective reports and objective observation, and sets a baseline for recognizing change.

- Private, safe, comfortable setting. If acute risk is suspected (e.g., SI), do an **immediate safety assessment** before finishing the MSE.

#### MSE domains and what to note?

| Domain | Assess |
|---|---|
| **Appearance** | Grooming, hygiene, clothing, presentation |
| **Alertness** | LOC, attention, ability to engage |
| **Behavior** | Cooperation, withdrawal, agitation, aggression, interaction |
| **Motor** | Slowed (**psychomotor retardation**), abnormal movements, rigidity, **catatonia** |
| **Speech** | Rate, tone, rhythm, volume, clarity, amount |
| **Mood** | Client's **reported** internal emotional state |
| **Affect** | Nurse's **observed** emotional expression |
| **Thought process** | Organization, logic, goal direction |
| **Thought content** | Delusions, preoccupations, **suicidal thoughts, thoughts of harming others** |
| **Perception** | Hallucinations, altered sensory experiences |
| **Insight** | Recognition of illness, situation, need for treatment |
| **Cognition** | Orientation, attention, memory, processing |
| **Judgment** | Ability to make safe, appropriate decisions |

#### Mood vs. affect — how to document?
- Compare for **congruence** (client says "fine" while tearful = incongruent). `[ADDED]` Affect terms: **flat** (no expression `[Engage]`), **blunted** (reduced range), **labile** (rapid shifts), **inappropriate/incongruent**.
- ==Document what you observe, not what you assume it means.==

#### Key terms `[ADDED]`?
- **Delusion** = fixed false belief not changed by evidence (e.g., "aliens are watching me"). **Hallucination** = sensory perception with no external stimulus (auditory most common). **Psychomotor retardation** = generalized slowing of movement and speech.

> [!info] Key contrast: process vs. content
> **Process = how** the client connects ideas (flight of ideas, loose associations). **Content = what** they think about (delusions, SI/HI). Keep them separate in charting and on exams.

#### Thought-process vocabulary?

| Term | Definition | Clue |
|---|---|---|
| **Thought blocking** | Sudden interruption in flow of ideas | Stops mid-sentence |
| **Loose associations** | Weak/unclear links between ideas | Topics jump without logic |
| **Flight of ideas** | Rapid skipping from idea to idea (links still traceable) | Pressured, fast `[ADDED]`: classic in mania |
| **Tangentiality** | Moves away, **never returns** to the point | Question never answered |
| **Circumstantiality** | Excessive detail, **eventually reaches** the point | Less severe |
| **Perseveration** | Persistent repetition of one word/idea across questions | Same answer to everything |
| **Echolalia** | Repeats another person's words | Parrots the nurse |
| **Neologism** | New word with private meaning | Made-up words |
| **Clang association** | Words linked by **sound** (rhyme) | "Play ball, call, fall" |
| **Word salad** | Severely disorganized; little meaning | Most severe |

#### Cognitive screening tools?

| Tool | Covers |
|---|---|
| **MMSE** | Orientation, attention, language, memory, calculation, visuospatial (**12** areas, **6** domains `[Engage]`); max **30**, **< 24** suggests impairment `[ADDED — verify vs course materials]` |
| **MoCA** | Broader: adds **executive** and conceptual abilities (**14** areas `[Engage]`); **≥ 26** normal `[ADDED — verify vs course materials]` |

- Use when impairment is suspected; interpret within the whole clinical picture.

## V. Psychosocial, family, community, spiritual, cultural, crisis assessment

#### What goes in a psychosocial assessment?
- Presenting concern · health and **psychiatric history** (diagnoses, hospitalizations, treatment) · **trauma** · meds (incl. OTC, herbals) and **allergies** · relationships, family history, education, employment, social functioning · substance use (tobacco, alcohol incl. **last drink** and withdrawal history) · coping, sleep, diet, activity, supports, spirituality, culture · **safety: SI, harm to others, abuse history**.
- Look for both **strengths and risk factors**. May take more than one session `[Engage]`.

#### How to conduct the interview?
- Private setting that feels **safe, not confining**; no rushing; active listening, **therapeutic silence**, empathy, appropriate eye contact; sit **at eye level**.
- **Funnel approach**: start broad → move to sensitive topics as trust develops (open-ended → clarifying → closed `[Engage]`).

> [!info] Key contrast: open first vs. ask directly
> Open with a broad question ("What brings you here today?") even if the chart shows recent SI — then ask about suicide **directly and clearly** within the interview. If acute risk is apparent on arrival, safety assessment comes first.

#### Therapeutic vs. non-therapeutic responses
Rows 1–3 follow the textbook's answers/dialogue; non-therapeutic phrasing is `[ADDED]`.

| Situation | Therapeutic | Non-therapeutic | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening a psychosocial interview (chart shows recent SI admission) | "What brings you here today?" `[Engage]` | "Why do you feel so depressed?" | Open-ended start builds rapport; "why" demands justification |
| Male Muslim client says a female nurse can't enter without a hijab | "Would you be more comfortable with a male nurse?" `[Engage]` | "I don't share your beliefs and can't wear one." | Respects culture; client-centered |
| Paranoid client (cocaine) refuses to talk, trying to leave | "You're right, you don't have to tell me anything. I've asked our tech to sit with us." `[Engage]` | "Nobody is after you — sit down." | Don't argue with paranoia; keep calm, add staff for safety |
| Client gives a vague answer about self-harm | "Are you thinking about killing yourself?" | "You're not thinking of doing anything silly, are you?" | Direct, clear closed question; leading/minimizing invites "no" |

#### Family, community, spiritual, cultural tools?

| Domain | Focus | Tool |
|---|---|---|
| Family | Functioning, communication, roles, support | **FAD** (McMaster; ~**60** items, Likert **1–5**; problem solving, communication, roles, responsiveness, affect-involvement, behavior control `[Engage]`) |
| Community | Population needs, barriers (e.g., transportation) | Community-based participatory assessment |
| Spiritual | Beliefs/practices that support coping (protective: ↓ depression, suicide risk) | **FICA** — Faith, Importance/influence, Community, Address `[Engage]`; on admission and with changes |
| Cultural | Beliefs about illness, symptoms, coping, help-seeking | **Cultural Formulation Interview** (DSM-5-TR); CFI-I for an informant |

#### Situational/crisis assessment?
- Focus on **now and next**; watch client and environment simultaneously.
- Staff safety plan `[Engage]`: communicate whereabouts · nothing wearable that could be used as a weapon · clear path to exit · position **near the door** · quick access to security · de-escalation skills.

> [!danger] Prompt action required
> Escalating agitation, threats, property destruction · severe disorientation · **new/increased hallucinations** · paranoia/distrust · **self- or other-directed harm**. Earlier cues `[Engage]`: declining hygiene, rapid mood swings, withdrawal (missing work/school). Intervene **before** crisis — far more effective.

## VI. Analysis and prioritization

#### Analysis tasks?
- **Analyze cues** `[Engage]`: recognize patterns · link cues · determine what's concerning · determine if more data needed.
- **Prioritize hypotheses** `[Engage]`: cluster · narrow possibilities · order priorities · weigh risk of action vs. inaction · provide evidence.
- Identify safety risks and protective factors. Ask what findings mean for the **next hour, shift, plan**.

#### What might an observation mean? `[Engage]`

| Observation | Possible meaning |
|---|---|
| Unbathed, unkempt | Self-neglect → depression or psychosis |
| Sudden flamboyant dress, heavy makeup, elevated speech | Mania (bipolar) or psychosis |
| Pacing, can't sit, watching the door | Anxiety or paranoia |
| Frozen, statue-like | Catatonia (e.g., schizophrenia) |
| Believes aliens watch; covers windows | Delusion → psychosis |
| Confused, slurred, stumbling, poor judgment | Intoxication/substance **or** medical (tumor, stroke) |

#### Priority framework?
- **ABCDE** first: airway, breathing, circulation, then disability (`[ADDED]` neuro status/LOC) and exposure (`[ADDED]` full inspection for injury while preventing hypothermia).
- In mental health: ==**risk for self-harm or harm to others**== is the psychological equivalent of the airway → immediate attention.
- Ask: **what could cause the greatest harm if action is delayed?** Reprioritize whenever condition changes.

#### Milieu as an intervention?
- The therapeutic environment around the client; **standard of care for RNs** (ANA PMH Scope and Standards 2022 `[Engage]`).
- Promote physical and psychological safety; trauma-informed interactions; clear expectations; teach **routines, rights, responsibilities**. Maintaining it is ongoing.

## VII. Planning (generate solutions)

#### What does planning require?
- Translate findings into **client-centered outcomes**; choose interventions for the priority problem; use evidence; **anticipate risks/negative effects** of interventions; account for staff, equipment, meds, time; collaborate with client, family, team.
- Define the problem from assessment data: what nursing action is needed (monitor, supervise, teach, promote recovery, a task like BP before a med) and what personnel/resources.

#### Interdisciplinary treatment plan vs. nursing plan?
- Treatment plan: collaborative (client, family, providers, therapists, social workers); written by one identified member with team input `[Engage]`. Includes history, reason for treatment, who does what, measurable goals, interventions, progress.
- **Nursing plan of care** = the nursing-specific interventions within it. Documentation shows progress toward shared outcomes. Care planned in the **least restrictive environment** `[Engage]`.

#### SMART outcomes — example?
- **S**pecific · **M**easurable · **A**ttainable · **R**ealistic · **T**imely. MH outcomes are mostly **behavioral and functional**. Client helps set them.

> [!example] Worked example — client with daily panic attacks `[Engage]`
> Short-term: client will **verbalize using a relaxation breathing technique** when anxious **1** time during the current shift.
> Long-term: client will report **fewer than 3** panic attacks in the next **14 days**.

#### Check the plan before implementing?
- Aligns with the interdisciplinary plan? · client goals/preferences visible? · evidence-based? · ==could it **retraumatize** or increase distress?== · outcomes realistic and measurable?

#### Discharge planning?
- **Starts at admission.** Identify outpatient, family, social, community supports (e.g., **ACT**, outpatient `[Engage]`); practical needs (housing, employment, food, transportation); **early-warning/relapse plan**; strong communication for continuity.
- Clients included in discharge planning use outpatient care more and are readmitted less `[Engage]`.

## VIII. Implementation

#### Implementation principles?
- Act by **priority**; monitor response during care; teach; coordinate with team; use evidence and resources; keep **therapeutic boundaries** and a person-centered approach; document action + response.
- Therapeutic use of self: e.g., **spending time with a depressed client** so they aren't alone `[Engage]`.

#### Core MH nursing interventions?
- Therapeutic communication and **purposeful presence** · med administration and monitoring · coping/relaxation/stress teaching · relapse-prevention education · health promotion incl. **smoking cessation** · family collaboration and advocacy · management of psychiatric emergencies.

#### Client and family teaching?
- Meds: expected effects, precautions, **adherence**. Families: symptoms and how to respond. Clients: **early signs of relapse** and what to do. Reinforce therapy skills. Include coping, self-care, **crisis planning**, community resources.
- Adapt to **readiness, cognition, language, culture, learning needs**.

#### Continuum of care?
- Know local resources; **social workers** connect clients with services.
- Continuum: outpatient → partial hospitalization → crisis services → inpatient. Recovery continues after discharge; follow-up (e.g., consistent primary care) supports stability and ↓ readmissions `[Engage]`.

#### Documentation essentials?
- Legal document; supports continuity, safety, quality, accountability.
- Record physical and mental status findings · interventions · response · progress/changes. **Objective, professional language.**

#### BIRP note?

| Part | Content | Example `[Engage]` |
|---|---|---|
| **B**ehavior | What client says + what you observe | Arrived unwashed, agitated, refused group: "This is a total waste of time." |
| **I**ntervention | What the nurse did | Stepped out with client; redirected to room with staff escort |
| **R**esponse | How client responded | Paced ~**10 min**, then apologized: "I just need more sleep." |
| **P**lan | What's next | Continue monitoring; explore sleep barriers |

## IX. Evaluation

#### What does evaluation involve?
- Compare current status with **expected outcomes**; decide if interventions worked; identify barriers; **continue, change, or stop** based on evidence; communicate and revise. Evaluation feeds the next assessment.

#### Barriers to effective intervention?
- Limited **insight/denial** · low confidence or motivation · language/literacy · cognitive or mobility limits · comorbidity/**polypharmacy** · worsening anxiety, depression, fear. Adapt the plan when the approach isn't working.

> [!tip] Five rights of clinical judgment `[Engage]`
> Right cues · right priority · right time · right action · right reason (ethical, legal, professional).

## X. Levels of prevention

#### When is the intervention? (public health model)

| Level | Goal | Example `[Engage]` |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Prevent before it develops | Teaching children about emotions; anti-stigma programs |
| **Secondary** | Identify risk/early illness, intervene promptly | Substance-use screening; support for trauma-exposed; crisis care |
| **Tertiary** | Reduce complications, disability, **relapse** | Relapse plan; support groups; housing/med-management programs |

#### Who receives it? (IOM model)

| Level | Target | Example `[Engage]` |
|---|---|---|
| **Universal** | Entire population/group | Social-emotional curriculum for all elementary students; 6th-grade drug awareness |
| **Selective** | Groups with **elevated risk** | Group therapy for children of parents with depression; stress classes for first responders |
| **Indicated** | Individuals at **very high risk or with significant symptoms** | Group for clients with bipolar disorder; early-schizophrenia treatment |

- The two frameworks complement each other: **when** vs. **who**.

## Self-quiz

> [!question]- 1. A new client arrives at a partial hospitalization program. Who performs the admission assessment?
> The staff RN. The PN contributes data collection but it doesn't replace the RN assessment.

> [!question]- 2. Map "prioritize hypotheses" to a nursing process step.
> Analysis.

> [!question]- 3. Client says she feels "great" while crying. What is this called and how is it documented?
> Incongruent mood and affect — document the stated mood (quote) and the observed affect objectively.

> [!question]- 4. Client answers a question with long, detailed side stories but eventually answers. Term?
> Circumstantiality (tangentiality would never return to the point).

> [!question]- 5. Client responds "cat, hat, bat, sat." Term?
> Clang association.

> [!question]- 6. Client believes the TV is sending him messages. Thought process or content?
> Thought content (delusion).

> [!question]- 7. A client on the psych unit has new confusion and a fever. First thought?
> Rule out a medical cause (THINC-MED: infection) before calling it psychiatric.

> [!question]- 8. Four clients: one pacing and anxious, one refusing lunch, one stating a plan to hang himself tonight, one with flat affect. Who first?
> The client with a suicide plan — risk of self-harm is the top psychological priority after ABCs.

> [!question]- 9. Which tool assesses spirituality, and what does it stand for?
> FICA — Faith, Importance/influence, Community, Address.

> [!question]- 10. Which cognitive screen adds executive function?
> MoCA.

> [!question]- 11. Is "Client will feel less anxious" a good outcome? Fix it.
> No — not measurable or timed. E.g., "Client will verbalize using a relaxation breathing technique when anxious 1 time during the current shift."

> [!question]- 12. When does discharge planning begin?
> At admission.

> [!question]- 13. Which planning check protects trauma survivors?
> Asking whether an intervention could retraumatize the client or increase distress.

> [!question]- 14. In a BIRP note, where does "client paced 10 min, then apologized" go?
> R — Response.

> [!question]- 15. Before entering a room with an agitated client, name three situational-safety steps.
> Tell staff where you are, keep a clear path to the exit / stay near the door, remove anything wearable that could be used as a weapon (also: quick access to security).

> [!question]- 16. Psychotherapy group for children of parents with depression — which IOM level?
> Selective.

> [!question]- 17. A relapse-prevention plan for a client with schizophrenia — which prevention level?
> Tertiary.

> [!question]- 18. Two diagnoses are present but only one is treated. What's the risk?
> Relapse driven by the untreated condition — screen comprehensively (always screen substance use).

> [!question]- 19. Which interview approach moves from broad questions to sensitive topics?
> The funnel approach.

> [!question]- 20. After a new med, a client is groggy with sluggish pupils and reflexes. Behavioral or physiologic priority?
> Physiologic — suspect medication effect (THINC-MED "D"), check airway/breathing and neuro status, hold further sedating doses, and report.
