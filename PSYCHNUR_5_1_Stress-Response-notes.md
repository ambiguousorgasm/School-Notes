## I. What stress is

> [!quote] Anchor
> Stress is a biological and psychological response to an actual or perceived threat. It protects in the short term but becomes harmful when prolonged.

#### What is stress?
- ==Biological + psychological response== to an **actual or perceived** stressor; the body responds even when the threat is only perceived (or remembered `[Engage]`).
- ==Not automatically harmful==: the response is designed for survival.

#### Helpful vs. harmful stress?
- **Short-term stress** ↑ alertness, energy, adaptation (e.g., preparing for a speech or exam `[Engage]`).
- ==**Resilience**== = ability to recover and return toward balance.
- ==**Chronic stress**== = no adequate opportunity to recover → body stays activated → physical, emotional, psychological harm.
- Assess the person's **stress load, resources, supports, coping**.

#### Three types of stressors?
| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Psychological** (emotional, cognitive) | Loss of self-esteem or role, internal conflict, hard decisions, grief, loss of security, fear, perceived threat, cognitive overload |
| **Physiological** (physical) | Illness, injury, **pain**, extreme temperatures, **substance use/withdrawal**, hunger, dehydration, poor nutrition, noise, light, pollution |
| **Psychosocial** (relational, social) | Divorce, loss, prolonged illness, relationship changes; work, school, financial, role stress; trauma, disaster; spiritual distress |

- Stress symptoms `[slide graphic]`: memory problems, irritability/short temper, eating or sleep changes, social withdrawal, difficulty trusting others.

#### Why assess total stress load?

> [!tip] NCLEX tip
> ==One client can have all three types at once.== Physical illness also brings fear, loss of security, and separation from support. ==Assess the **cumulative burden**==, not one stressor.

## II. Physiology of the stress response

#### What is the goal of the stress response?
- Stress threatens **homeostasis**; the response aims to meet the threat and ==restore balance==.
- The brain starts **rapid neurologic** and **slower hormonal** responses.

#### Amygdala → hypothalamus?
- **Amygdala** = command center: evaluates information for emotional significance and danger.
- A perceived threat activates the **hypothalamus**, which ==links the nervous system and endocrine system==.

#### SNS vs. PNS effects?
|                             | Sympathetic ("fight or flight")               | Parasympathetic ("rest and digest") |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Messenger                   | ==**Epinephrine, norepinephrine**== (seconds) | ==**Acetylcholine**==               |
| HR, BP, RR                  | ↑                                             | ↓                                   |
| Pupils                      | **Dilate**                                    | Constrict `[ADDED]`                 |
| Digestion, urine production | ↓                                             | ↑ digestion                         |
| Purpose                     | Mobilize for action                           | Rest, digestion, recovery           |

`[slide graphic, PNS side partly unreadable]`

#### HPA axis?
- Hypothalamus → ==**CRH**== → anterior pituitary → ==**ACTH**== → adrenal cortex → ==**cortisol**==.
- **Cortisol** maintains energy availability (↑ glucose) and **blood pressure**, and modifies immune and inflammatory activity.
- Epinephrine also comes from the adrenal gland (medulla `[ADDED]`) → high alert `[Engage]`.

#### Fast vs. sustained response?
- **Sympathetic:** rapid, immediate. **HPA:** slower, longer-lasting.
- ==**Cortisol negative feedback** normally shuts the response down==; ==chronic stress disrupts this regulation==.
- Stress chemicals affect ==multiple body systems==, not just the brain.

#### Five stress responses?
| Response | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Fight** | Confront the threat; may look like ==anger or aggression== |
| **Flight** | Escape or avoid the threat |
| **Freeze** | Unable to respond or act; ==involuntary==, may occur with trauma |
| **Faint** | Loss of consciousness, often **vasovagal** (syncope) |
| **Fawn** | ==Appease or please== the source of threat |

- All are ==automatic survival strategies==. When the threat ends, the hypothalamus returns systems to normal; if not, prolonged activation harms the body `[Engage]`.

> [!warning] Priority
> **Fawn** behavior may raise concerns about ==understanding and **informed consent**==: a client who agrees just to please may not truly consent. Confirm understanding.

## III. General adaptation syndrome (GAS)

#### What is GAS, and who described it?
- **Hans Selye**: the body's response to ongoing stress; ==Alarm → Resistance → Exhaustion==. Emphasizes adaptation and the **limits** of the body's resources. Selye was first to explain the HPA axis `[Engage]`.
- **Walter Cannon** described the "fight or flight" response `[Engage]`.

#### Three stages?
| Stage | What happens | Findings | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| ==**Alarm**== (acute) | **Sympathetic activation**. Engage substages: **shock** (↓ BP, temp, muscle tone, fluid loss) → **countershock** (adrenaline release) `[Engage]` | ↑ HR, BP, RR; ↑ **blood glucose**, alertness, senses; ↓ digestion and nonessential functions | Stressor resolves → recovery |
| ==**Resistance**== (adaptation) | Body tries to **adapt** to continued stress | Visible alarm signs ↓ but ==body remains activated==: irritability, fatigue, poor sleep, poor concentration, frustration | Stressor ↓ → recovery possible |
| ==**Exhaustion**== | ==Resources depleted==; adaptation fails | Immune and CV effects; fatigue, **burnout**, anxiety, depression, worsening illness | Significant illness, ==even death== |

#### Why does perception matter?
- The response depends on ==how the person interprets the threat==; physical, psychological, social, real, or perceived stressors all trigger it.
- ==**Rumination and worry** keep the response active== even with no immediate threat.

## IV. Peplau's anxiety continuum

> [!quote] Anchor
> As anxiety rises, the perceptual field narrows, problem-solving and learning drop, physical symptoms grow, and nursing interventions must change with the level.

#### Four levels of anxiety?
| Level | Perception / problem-solving | Physical / behavior | Nursing approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| ==**Mild**== | ↑ awareness, attention (hypervigilance `[Engage]`); ==problem-solving at its strongest; learning possible== | Restlessness, fidgeting, nail-biting, foot-tapping `[Engage]` | ==**Best time for teaching**== and supportive problem-solving |
| **Moderate** | Attention narrows to the immediate concern; problem-solving possible but harder; limited new learning `[Engage]` | ↑ HR, BP, RR, sweating, muscle tension | ==**Short, concrete teaching**==; simple calming strategies |
| **Severe** | Perceptual field ==greatly reduced/distorted==; focus on one detail `[Engage]`; problem-solving and learning severely impaired | Nausea, dizziness, trembling, ==chest discomfort==; impending doom, withdrawal, loud speech `[Engage]` | ==**Safety**==, calm communication, ↓ stimulation, **simple directions** |
| ==**Panic**== | ==Severely distorted==; can't process environment; possible hallucinations/delusions `[Engage]` | Disorganization, **dissociation**, immobility, **fleeing**, **aggression** | ==Safety is the immediate priority==; **stay with the client**, ↓ stimulation, brief calm statements; ==teaching waits== |

> [!info] Key contrast
> **Therapeutic (panic):** "I'm staying with you. You're safe. Breathe with me." `[ADDED]`
> **Non-therapeutic:** "Calm down." · "Why are you so upset?" · a long explanation or teaching session `[ADDED]`

#### How do you integrate stress management by level?
- ==Mild/moderate → teaching and skill-building are possible.==
- ==Severe/panic → reduce anxiety and ensure safety **before** teaching.==

## V. Stress, immunity, and stress types

#### How does stress affect immunity?
- **Short-term** stress temporarily ==activates== immune defenses (infection fighting, wound healing `[Engage]`).
- **Cytokines** coordinate immune-cell communication (released by immune cells; recruit other immune cells `[Engage]`).
- **Cortisol** provides a ==braking effect on inflammation==.
- ==**Chronic** stress suppresses immune function and contributes to persistent inflammation== (immune-stress vicious cycle).

#### Nursing implications?
- Prolonged stress → ==↑ susceptibility to illness and infection==; ==slower healing and recovery==.
- ==Stress reduction is part of **health promotion**, not simply a comfort measure.==

#### Eustress vs. distress?
| | Eustress | Distress |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | **Positive** stress: motivation, energy, meaningful change | Stress that ==overwhelms coping and impairs functioning== |
| Feels like | Motivated, determined, excited; ↑ focus | Anxious, overwhelmed, frustrated; ↓ focus |
| Examples `[Engage]` | Birth of a child, marriage, buying a home, retirement, new job, promotion, graduation | Death of a loved one, financial burden, divorce, legal problems, abuse, conflict, hospitalization, illness |

- ==The **same** physiological response occurs with positive or negative events==; the body can't tell them apart without the person's **appraisal** `[Engage]`.

#### Why do people react differently?
- **Appraisal**: whether an experience feels manageable or overwhelming.
- Previous experiences, genetics, environment shape **stress reactivity**.
- ==**Diathesis-stress model**==: biological/genetic vulnerability (**diathesis**) + significant stress → ↑ mental illness risk. ==Reducing stress and strengthening coping can be protective.==

#### Acute vs. chronic vs. toxic stress?
| | Acute | Chronic | Toxic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cause | ==Sudden== exposure to a stressor | ==Repeated or prolonged== exposure; rumination/recall can maintain it | ==Prolonged activation **without adequate buffering** support== |
| Findings | ==Resemble the alarm stage== | Persistent overwhelm; physical and mental disease | Biological and neurological changes; ==most harmful in early development== |
| Course | Usually resolves; intervene if symptoms persist or impair function | Many physical and mental problems | ↑ later-life risk with greater cumulative adversity |

#### Health effects of chronic stress?
| System | Effects |
|---|---|
| **Cardiovascular** | Persistent ↑ BP, HR; CVD |
| **Metabolic** | Appetite change, ==abdominal fat==, glucose dysregulation, ==insulin resistance==; obesity, T2DM `[Engage]` |
| **Immune/inflammatory** | ↓ defense, ==persistent inflammation==; ↑ infection; autoimmune (fibromyalgia, lupus) `[Engage]` |
| **GI / reproductive** `[Engage]` | IBD-type dysfunction; sexual dysfunction |
| **Mental health** | ==Anxiety, depression, sleep disturbance==, cognitive effects; eating, mood, substance use disorders `[Engage]` |

#### ACEs and toxic stress?
- **Adverse childhood experiences** affect the developing brain and body → lifelong problems with learning, behavior, physical and mental health; ↑ mental illness `[Engage]`.
- ACE categories `[slide graphic]`: **household instability** (mental illness, incarcerated relative, mother treated violently, substance abuse, divorce); **neglect** (physical, emotional); **abuse** (physical, emotional, sexual).
- ==**Safe, stable, nurturing relationships buffer adversity.**==

#### Physical vs. psychological manifestations?
| Physical | Psychological / behavioral |
|---|---|
| Headache, muscle tension, fatigue, ==insomnia== | Anxiety, irritability, depression, ==panic==, sadness, excessive worry |
| Palpitations, ↑ BP, sweating, SOB | Changes in concentration, language, problem-solving |
| GI: nausea, diarrhea, constipation, heartburn | Substance use, gambling, eating disorders, compulsive shopping/sex `[Engage]` |
| Dry mouth, rash, sexual dysfunction, weakened immunity `[Engage]` | Delayed development or **regression** `[Engage]` |

> [!danger] Report immediately
> ==**Chest pain, palpitations, and shortness of breath require medical evaluation before being attributed to stress.**==

## VI. Mediators of the stress response

#### What changes how people respond?
- **Protective factors** reduce the impact; **risk factors** ↑ vulnerability; **mediators** = individual characteristics + environmental conditions.
- ==Nursing assessment identifies factors that can be **strengthened or changed**.==

#### Individual factors?
- **Perception and attitudes** · **temperament and personality** · genetics, development, age, underlying health · lifestyle (sleep, nutrition, activity, substances).
- Personality `[Engage]`: ==**neuroticism** and rumination → negative thinking== (risk); **openness**, forward-looking sense of control → protective. Big Five: neuroticism, extroversion, openness, conscientiousness, agreeableness.

#### Hardiness and resilience?
- ==Sense of **control, hope, self-efficacy**== supports coping.
- ==**Hardiness** = commitment, control, viewing change as a challenge.==
- ==**Resilience** = capacity to adapt and recover, **not** the absence of stress.==
- **Low self-esteem and hopelessness** ↑ vulnerability (→ burnout `[Engage]`).

#### Lifestyle factors?
- **Risk:** poor sleep, inactivity, unhealthy eating, smoking, drugs/alcohol.
- **Protective:** sleep, exercise, nutrition, **hydration**, mindfulness, gratitude, breathing practices.
- ==Lifestyle factors are key targets for nursing education.==

#### Environmental and cultural factors?
- Safety, housing, workplace, school, community conditions shape stress; safe school/work environments are protective `[Engage]`.
- Culture shapes beliefs, coping, support, and how distress is expressed; connection to culture is protective `[Engage]`. Use **cultural humility** `[Engage]`.
- ==**Acculturative stress**==: language barriers, discrimination, adapting to a new environment.

#### Support systems, spirituality, SDOH?
- Identify support people who are ==safe, available, and genuinely helpful==.
- Spiritual/religious practices → meaning, hope, connection, community. ==Respect the client's beliefs.==
- **SDOH:** poverty, housing insecurity, unemployment, discrimination, violence → chronic stress, ==often outside the client's control== → **social work, community resources, advocacy, discharge planning**.

## VII. Coping

#### What is coping, and how do you assess it?
- ==**Coping** = adjusting to stress while trying to maintain physical and mental equilibrium.==
- Ask what the client is doing to manage stress ==and whether it's working==.
- Strategies should fit the **stressor, client, culture, resources**.

#### Holmes-Rahe Life Stress Inventory?
- Measures ==cumulative life change in the previous year==; includes ==both positive and negative== events.
- Higher score → ==greater vulnerability to stress-related illness==.
- Scoring `[ADDED — verify vs course materials]`: < **150** low · **150–299** moderate · ≥ **300** high risk.
- ==One part of assessment, **not a diagnosis**.==

#### Three coping styles?
| Style | What it does | Example | Adaptive? |
|---|---|---|---|
| ==**Problem-focused**== | Plans, gathers information, ==takes action to change the stressor== | Makes a list to tackle the problem `[Engage]` | **Adaptive** |
| ==**Emotion-focused**== | ==Manages emotional response==: support, relaxation, journaling, prayer | Seeks emotional support `[Engage]` | **Adaptive** |
| ==**Avoidant**== | Minimizes, ignores, escapes the stressor | Drinking or sleeping to avoid it `[Engage]` | ==**Maladaptive**==: short-term relief, longer-term problems |

#### Brief COPE Inventory?
- Assesses strategies used for a **current** stressor: active coping, planning, support-seeking, positive reframing, acceptance, humor, religion, self-distraction, denial, substance use, self-blame.
- ==Identifies which strategies help vs. worsen the problem.==

## VIII. Defense mechanisms

#### What are defense mechanisms?
- Ways a person ==consciously or unconsciously== reduces internal or external stress; protect against overwhelming fear, anxiety, conflict.
- Exist on a ==continuum from adaptive to maladaptive==.

#### Adaptive vs. maladaptive?
- **Adaptive (mature):** support functioning ==without significantly distorting reality==.
- **Maladaptive (immature, "escape"):** rely on ==avoidance, denial, distortion==; linked to ↑ mental illness symptoms, ==suicide risk==, personality disorders `[Engage]`. Often normal in children `[Engage]`.
- ==The same mechanism can work differently depending on how, when, and how often it's used.==

#### Defense mechanisms: definitions and examples?
| Mechanism | Definition | Example `[Engage]` |
|---|---|---|
| **Repression** | ==**Unconscious** blocking== of distressing material | Can't remember childhood abuse; struggles with relationships |
| **Suppression** `[Engage]` | ==**Conscious**== choice to set aside unwanted thoughts | Angry at a parent but won't let it spoil the holiday |
| **Denial** | ==Refusing to accept reality== | Insists the terminal diagnosis is a mix-up |
| **Displacement** | Redirecting feelings toward a ==safer target== | Angry at the boss, yells at spouse |
| **Projection** | Attributing ==own== unacceptable thoughts/feelings to someone else | Child who lies assumes the parent lies |
| **Dissociation** | Disruption in awareness, memory, or identity | Can't recall details of an accident or own identity |
| **Identification** | Taking on characteristics of another person | Acts as their parent would; child copies violence seen at home |
| **Intellectualization** | Focus on facts/logic to ==distance from emotion== | Nurse focuses on tasks with a violence victim |
| **Rationalization** | Explanations to ==avoid uncomfortable emotions== | "It wouldn't have happened if I'd paid more attention to them" |
| **Splitting** | ==All-good / all-bad== extremes | "All the other nurses are terrible; you're the only good one" |
| **Sublimation** | Redirecting energy into a ==constructive== activity | Goes for a walk after a hard day |
| **Undoing** | Cancels an act/thought with an ==opposite== act | Showers partner with affection after abuse |
| Altruism `[Engage]` | Helping others with a similar experience | Bereaved parent supports others |
| Conversion `[Engage]` | Psychological stress → ==physical symptoms== | Temporary blindness after seeing a partner's affair |
| Compensation `[Engage]` | Builds strength in one area to offset a weakness | Broken leg → learns chess |
| Reaction formation `[Engage]` | Acts the ==opposite== of true feelings | Person quitting substances lectures others on dangers |
| Regression `[Engage]` | Return to an earlier developmental stage | Yelling and throwing objects in an argument |

#### Nursing approach to a defensive client?

> [!warning] Priority
> ==**Don't strip away a client's defenses abruptly.**== Build trust, boundaries, respect; assess body language, tone, words, fear, anxiety; ==help the client **gradually** move toward healthier coping==.

- **Communicating with a defensive client:** stay calm, respectful, nonjudgmental; ==no sarcasm or demeaning talk==; manage your own tone, face, posture, frustration.
- Avoid words that dismiss the client's view: =="but," "however," "although," "instead"== can feel judgmental and ↑ defensiveness `[Engage]`.

## IX. Stress management

#### What's the goal of stress management?
- ==Stress can't be eliminated; the goal is **healthier adaptation**.==
- **Mind-to-body:** change thoughts, attention, appraisal. **Body-to-mind:** change breathing, muscle tension, activity.
- ==Choose techniques that fit the individual client.==

#### What is the relaxation response?
- ==Physiological opposite of fight-or-flight== (Herbert Benson `[slide graphic]`).
- ==Activates the PNS== → ↓ HR, RR, BP, muscle tension.
- Regular practice improves the response to future stressors.

#### Stress-management techniques?
| Technique | How | Key teaching |
|---|---|---|
| **Adjusting expectations** | Prepare for a range of outcomes | ==Anticipatory guidance== → realistic expectations for recovery and treatment |
| **Biofeedback** | Sensors display HR, muscle tension, temperature, sweating, breathing in real time | Goal: ==awareness and self-regulation== |
| ==**Deep breathing**== | Slow, deliberate, **diaphragmatic** breaths; ==longer, slower exhale== enhances calm | ==Practice when calm== so it works under stress. Box/square breathing: **4-4-4-4** `[Engage]` |
| **Positive self-talk** | Replace harsh self-criticism with balanced, supportive statements | Builds self-efficacy and hope; ==not pretending everything is fine== |
| **Guided imagery** | Relax, breathe, imagine a calming place using ==multiple senses== | For anxiety, **pain**, procedural stress |
| **Mindfulness meditation** | Attention on the present; notice thoughts ==without judgment==, gently return | Interrupts **rumination**; supports emotional regulation |

#### Closing: what must the nurse remember?
- Assess the whole person: ==stressors, physiology, anxiety level, coping, defenses, supports, environment==.
- ==Calm, respectful nursing communication itself helps regulate the client's stress response.==
- Nurses need rest too: rest is a necessity, not a weakness `[slide graphic]`.

## Self-quiz

> [!question]- 1. Trace the HPA axis.
> Hypothalamus (CRH) → anterior pituitary (ACTH) → adrenal cortex (cortisol).

> [!question]- 2. Which response is faster: sympathetic or HPA? What shuts the HPA response off?
> Sympathetic (seconds). Cortisol negative feedback; chronic stress disrupts it.

> [!question]- 3. Major parasympathetic neurotransmitter?
> Acetylcholine.

> [!question]- 4. Which brain structure evaluates danger and signals the hypothalamus?
> The amygdala.

> [!question]- 5. A client agrees to every proposed treatment to please staff. Which stress response, and what's the concern?
> Fawn. Informed consent may not be genuine; confirm understanding.

> [!question]- 6. In which GAS stage do visible signs fade while the body stays activated?
> Resistance stage.

> [!question]- 7. What happens in the exhaustion stage?
> Resources depleted; immune/CV effects, burnout, depression, worsening illness, possibly death.

> [!question]- 8. A client with mild anxiety is fidgeting. Good time to teach?
> Yes. Problem-solving and learning are at their best.

> [!question]- 9. A client in panic is fleeing and disorganized. Priority?
> Safety: stay with the client, reduce stimulation, brief calm statements; teaching waits.

> [!question]- 10. Severe anxiety and moderate anxiety: which allows short, concrete teaching?
> Moderate.

> [!question]- 11. How does chronic stress affect immunity?
> Suppresses immune function and promotes persistent inflammation → ↑ infection, slower healing.

> [!question]- 12. Is the physiological response to a wedding different from a house fire?
> No. Same response; appraisal determines eustress vs. distress.

> [!question]- 13. Explain the diathesis-stress model.
> Vulnerability (diathesis) + significant stress → ↑ mental illness risk; reducing stress and building coping is protective.

> [!question]- 14. What makes stress "toxic," and what buffers it?
> Prolonged activation without adequate buffering support, especially in childhood. Safe, stable, nurturing relationships buffer it.

> [!question]- 15. A stressed client reports chest pain and palpitations. First action?
> Medical evaluation before attributing symptoms to stress.

> [!question]- 16. Name the three components of hardiness.
> Commitment, control, viewing change as a challenge.

> [!question]- 17. What does a high Holmes-Rahe score mean, and what time frame does it cover?
> Greater vulnerability to stress-related illness; life events in the past year. Not a diagnosis.

> [!question]- 18. A client copes with a layoff by sleeping all day. Coping style?
> Avoidant (maladaptive).

> [!question]- 19. "I'm angry at my boss," then yells at the spouse. Defense mechanism?
> Displacement.

> [!question]- 20. "All the nurses here are awful except you." Defense mechanism?
> Splitting.

> [!question]- 21. Repression vs. suppression?
> Repression is unconscious blocking; suppression is a conscious choice to set aside thoughts.

> [!question]- 22. A grieving client channels anger into running a marathon fundraiser. Defense mechanism?
> Sublimation (altruism also fits helping others with the same loss).

> [!question]- 23. Why not confront a client's defenses directly?
> Stripping defenses abruptly ↑ anxiety and defensiveness; build trust and move gradually toward healthier coping.

> [!question]- 24. Breathing teaching point that enhances the calming effect, and when to practice?
> Longer, slower exhalation; practice when calm so it's available under stress.
