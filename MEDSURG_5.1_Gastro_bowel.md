## Digestive A&P essentials

*Not in the deck. Supplied from `[Engage: The Digestive System]` for objective 1.*

#### Upper vs. lower GI?
- **Upper:** mouth, pharynx, esophagus, stomach, **duodenum**. **Lower:** jejunum → ileum → colon → rectum → anus. **Accessory:** liver, gallbladder, pancreas.

#### What do the esophageal sphincters do?
- **UES** (cricopharyngeus) blocks air from entering the esophagus and prevents aspiration. It relaxes reflexively on swallowing (vagus + glossopharyngeal nerves).
- **LES** prevents acid regurgitation into the esophagus, with help from the diaphragm. It opens briefly on each swallow. ==LES failure = GERD.==

#### Where is B12 absorbed?
- ==Only the terminal ileum==, and only with **intrinsic factor** from gastric parietal cells.

#### Normal transit times?
- Gastric emptying **2–5 hr** · small bowel **2–6 hr** · colon **10–59 hr**.

## I. Oral & esophageal conditions

### HSV-1 vs. aphthous ulcers

#### How do you tell them apart?
| | HSV-1 (cold sore) | Aphthous ulcer (canker sore) |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Virus; ==latent, reactivates== | Not fully understood; linked to bacteria `[Engage]` |
| Location | **Lips** | **Inside the mouth**, painful |
| Look | Fluid-filled vesicles → crust → heal `[Engage]` | Round, red; white/gray/yellow base; **7–10 days** `[Engage]` |
| Triggers | Stress, immune suppression, UV, close contact | Stress, trauma (biting, braces), hormonal changes/menses, irritating hot/spicy foods |
| Diagnosis | Clinical. Prodrome: burning, tingling, fever `[Engage]` | Clinical: history + oral exam |

#### HSV-1 teaching and comfort?
- Contagious: **hand hygiene**; avoid oral sexual contact while lesions are active `[Engage]`.
- Moist cold or warm compress on lip lesions; rinse open oral lesions with warm water or the prescribed solution; OTC or topical antivirals; ibuprofen for pain `[Engage]`.

### Oral candidiasis (thrush)

#### Who gets thrush?
- Candida overgrowth. Highest risk: **immune suppression** and **age ≥ 65** (recurrent, from weaker immunity); also infants/toddlers `[Engage summary]`.
- **Antibiotics** disrupt normal flora · **corticosteroids** (including inhaled when the mouth isn't rinsed) · ill-fitting **dentures** · **tobacco**.

#### What does it look like?
- Raised **white plaques** on the tongue and cheeks, with burning, redness, and pain → poor intake. It can extend into the pharynx/esophagus `[Engage]`.

#### How is it diagnosed?
- History + oral exam. **Culture/scraping** (Gram stain) if unclear or not responding to treatment; **biopsy** if it resembles a malignancy `[Engage]`.

> [!tip] NCLEX tip
> Inhaled corticosteroid → ==rinse the mouth with water after every use== (spacer too). This is the classic thrush-prevention teaching item.

### Periodontal disease

#### Gingivitis vs. periodontitis?
| | Gingivitis | Periodontal disease |
|---|---|---|
| Stage | Early, ==reversible== with aggressive oral hygiene | Progression |
| Findings | Usually painless; red, puffy gums that bleed on brushing; persistent bad breath (plaque) `[Engage]` | Gum recession, dark red/purple gums, pus pockets, bleeding, sensitivity, loose teeth |
| Diagnosis | Dental exam + **pocket-depth** measurement | Same; x-ray if pockets are deep (bone involvement) `[Engage]` |

#### Why does it matter beyond the mouth?
- Chronic inflammation raises the risk of **atherosclerotic CVD**: CAD, PAD, HF, aneurysm `[Engage]`. Access to dental care (SDOH) drives risk.

### Oral cancer

#### Warning signs to report?
- An oral sore or ulcer that **doesn't heal** · a **white or red patch** · a lump or growth · **cervical lymphadenopathy**
- Mouth or ear pain, numbness, dysphagia, trouble chewing or opening the mouth · voice changes · unexplained weight loss
- **Risks:** tobacco (smoked or chewed), alcohol, **HPV**, UV, poor oral health / chronic inflammation, candida infection, immunosuppression `[Engage]`.

#### How is it diagnosed?
- There is no lab test. Visualization → **pharyngoscopy / laryngoscopy** (indirect with mirrors, or direct with a fiber-optic scope) → scraping on a stained slide `[Engage]` → ==**biopsy** confirms== → HPV testing (especially **HPV16**) on confirmed tissue.

> [!danger] Report immediately
> A persistent sore, a white/red patch, or a new lump: the nurse **advocates for follow-up**. Don't chart it and move on.

### Barrett's esophagus

#### What is it?
- **Chronic GERD** injures the distal esophagus → normal **squamous lining is replaced by intestinal-type epithelium** (goblet cells) → ==precancerous==.
- ↑ risk of **esophageal adenocarcinoma**; Barrett's is the only known precursor change `[Engage]`.

#### Who's at risk?
- Chronic GERD (weekly for **≥ 5 yr**), age **≥ 50**, male, smoking, obesity, White, family history of Barrett's or esophageal cancer `[Engage]`.
- **5–12%** of clients with GERD have Barrett's `[Engage]`.

#### What findings suggest it?
- Subtle: frequent heartburn, dysphagia/odynophagia, food feeling stuck, constant sore throat, bad breath, weight loss, blood in stool, vomiting `[Engage]`.

#### Diagnosis and management?
- **EGD + biopsy**, then ==regular surveillance EGD==.
- **Endoscopic eradication therapy (EET)** as needed `[Engage]`: endoscopic removal or ablation (e.g., radiofrequency) of dysplastic tissue to prevent progression to cancer `[ADDED]`.
- Invasive cancer → **esophagectomy**, usually after chemo/radiation to shrink the tumor. The esophagus is rebuilt from stomach or a bowel segment `[Engage]`.

#### Barrett's teaching?
- Report signs of worsening: **↑ dysphagia, blood in stool, weight loss, more frequent heartburn** `[Engage]`.
- Reflux control: small frequent meals, finish eating ≥ **3 hr** before bed, avoid trigger foods, acid reducers as prescribed.

### Impact, aging adult, nursing role

#### How do these disorders affect intake and quality of life?
- Pain → hard to chew and swallow → **dehydration, nutritional deficits**. The provider may check folate, B1, B2, B6, B12, Mg, zinc `[Engage]`.
- Speech, appearance, breath, and tooth loss affect self-esteem and eating in public. GERD disrupts sleep, meals, work, and social life.

#### Aging adult: what to watch?
- Recurrent candidiasis (immune changes) · tooth loss and dentures interfere with eating and cause gum irritation · ↑ HSV-1 reactivation `[Engage]`.
- **Assess oral tissues and denture fit**; watch for weight loss, poor intake, pain, and swallowing problems.

#### What goes in the history and exam?
- Oral health and routine, orthodontic devices, swallowing, nutrition, weight change, speech change; **tobacco, alcohol, vaping**, medications, immunizations (HPV).
- Inspect lips, palate, gums, cheeks, tongue, teeth, and throat with a **direct light + tongue blade** (standard precautions); palpate lymph nodes `[Engage]`.
- **Priority** `[Engage]`: abnormal oral or esophageal findings → ↓ fluid and nutrition → weight loss and dehydration.
- Team: SLP, dietitian, dentist, hygienist, ENT, oral surgeon `[Engage]`.

#### Prevention teaching?
- **Soft-bristled** toothbrush, replaced regularly (**≥ q3 mo**, after illness) · brush and floss · warm saline rinse for discomfort `[Engage]`
- Clean dentures; soak overnight `[Engage]` · ==rinse after inhaled corticosteroids==
- Avoid tobacco, limit alcohol, limit sugar · SPF lip protection · **HPV vaccination** · routine dental care

#### Oral-condition meds?
| Drug | Use / action | Adverse effects | Teaching |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acyclovir**, famciclovir, valacyclovir `[Engage]` | HSV-1 outbreaks; force faulty viral DNA → no replication | Headache, N/V, paresthesia; ==renal dysfunction/failure== | **Stay well hydrated**; finish the full course even if better |
| **Nystatin** suspension `[Engage]` | Thrush (antifungal) | GI upset `[ADDED]` | Swish and hold, then swallow; after meals/oral care; remove and clean dentures first `[ADDED]` |
| **Dexamethasone** mouthwash `[Engage]` | Aphthous ulcers (topical steroid) | — | Swish and spit; don't swallow `[ADDED — verify]` |
| Antineoplastics `[Engage]` | Esophageal cancer | — | — |

Episodic HSV-1 doses `[Engage]`: acyclovir **200 mg PO 5×/day** or **400 mg PO 3×/day** · famciclovir **1500 mg PO once** · valacyclovir **2 g PO twice for 1 day**.

## II. GERD

> [!quote] Anchor
> Gastric contents back up because the LES doesn't hold; repeated acid exposure damages the esophagus and can lead to Barrett's or stricture.

#### Why does GERD happen?
- The **LES** normally limits reflux. When it fails to close tightly, acid refluxes and irritates the lining. Psychological stress can worsen severity `[Engage]`.
- Chronic GERD → **Barrett's esophagus**, **stricture**, and dental enamel erosion.

#### Risk factors?
- **Obesity / ↑ intra-abdominal pressure** · smoking · alcohol · caffeine and trigger foods · **lying down soon after meals**
- **Hiatal hernia** · pregnancy · stress · meds: **anticholinergics, NSAIDs, aspirin** `[Engage]` · age ≥ 50, connective tissue disorders `[Engage]`

#### Clinical presentation?
- **Heartburn** (may be described as chest pain), **regurgitation**, sour taste after meals, belching, N/V
- **Dysphagia** (difficulty swallowing), **odynophagia** (painful swallowing), **dyspepsia** (upper abdominal indigestion/discomfort) `[ADDED]`
- **Hoarseness, chronic cough**, throat clearing from throat irritation

> [!warning] Priority
> Chest pain = ==cardiac cause first==. Don't label it reflux automatically. Assess **onset, location, radiation, duration, severity, character, triggers/relief** and associated SOB, diaphoresis, dizziness, N/V. Review CV risks: CAD, HTN, cholesterol, smoking, DM, family history. For a GERD client reporting chest discomfort, the first action is to **assess the pain** (location, intensity, duration), before calling the provider or medicating `[Engage practice item]`.

#### GERD diagnostics?
| Test | What it does | Nursing point |
|---|---|---|
| **Ambulatory esophageal pH / reflux monitoring** | ==Confirms== abnormal acid exposure (current standard) | Catheter through the nose, **~24 hr**; or **Bravo** capsule attached above the LES during EGD, records **48–96 hr**, falls off and passes in stool `[Engage]` |
| **EGD** | Finds complications: Barrett's, ulcer | Also used to place Bravo and take biopsies |
| Pregnancy test | Baseline before imaging/treatment | Clients of childbearing age `[Engage]` |

pH testing can be done before a PPI starts or while on one (to check whether it's working) `[Engage]`.

#### EGD care?
- Before: **NPO**; sedation → needs a ride home. After: ==nothing by mouth until the gag reflex returns==; report severe pain, fever, bleeding, or trouble swallowing (possible perforation) `[ADDED]`.

#### Nursing assessment?
- **Ask:** heartburn, regurgitation, cough, throat clearing, sour taste, belching, bloating.
- **Inspect:** oropharynx for irritation; teeth for **enamel erosion**.
- **Abdomen:** inspect → auscultate (**~1 min per quadrant** `[Engage]`) → palpate.
- **Assess** hydration and nutrition when dysphagia or vomiting is present.

#### Sudden "food is stuck" in long-standing GERD?
- Usually food eaten fast or poorly chewed, revealing a **stricture** from chronic acid scarring. Ask about milder prior episodes `[Engage]`.
- The client vomits everything → check for **dehydration and electrolyte imbalance**: dry mucosa, ↓ urine output, dizziness, tachycardia, hypotension `[Engage]`.

#### Lifestyle teaching?
- Healthy weight · **no tight clothing** · avoid personal triggers (spicy/fried food, caffeine, chocolate, alcohol, carbonated drinks `[Engage]`) · stop smoking
- Finish meals **≥ 3 hr** before bed · **don't lie down after eating**
- **Elevate the HOB** (**6–9 in** with blocks or a wedge, or extra pillows `[Engage]`); **left-side** sleeping may help
- Good oral hygiene, because acid erodes enamel · stress reduction `[Engage]`

> [!tip] NCLEX tip
> Correct teaching: "sleep with an extra pillow under my head." Wrong: "eat 30 min before bed," "lie down after eating" `[Engage practice item]`.

#### GERD meds?
| Class (examples) | Action | Adverse effects / monitoring | Teaching |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PPIs**: pantoprazole, omeprazole, lansoprazole, esomeprazole | ==Suppress== gastric acid secretion | Headache, diarrhea/constipation, abdominal pain, flatulence; long-term: see below | ==Before meals==; lowest effective regimen as directed |
| **H2 blockers**: famotidine, cimetidine | ==Reduce== acid production by blocking gastric histamine receptors | Headache, diarrhea/constipation, fatigue, muscle aches; **monitor kidney/liver function** `[Engage]` | Daily dose at bedtime; BID morning and evening `[Engage]` |
| **Antacids**: aluminum hydroxide, calcium carbonate (Tums), magnesium hydroxide (MOM) | ==Neutralize== acid already present | Constipation, ↓ appetite, N/V, flatulence, dry mouth `[Engage]`; Al/Ca → constipation, Mg → diarrhea `[ADDED]` | OTC; **chew completely**; drink fluid after; before meals and at bedtime `[Engage]`; separate from other meds `[ADDED — verify]` |
| **Baclofen** (skeletal muscle relaxant) | ↓ transient LES relaxations → fewer reflux episodes after meals and during sleep | **Drowsiness, dizziness**, constipation | TID; no driving until effects are known; **alcohol ↑ sedation**; ↑ fiber `[Engage]` |

> [!warning] Long-term PPI safety
> Monitor for ==↓ magnesium, ↓ calcium, ↓ vitamin B12== (reduced acid → poor absorption) · **fracture** risk (osteoporosis; calcium supplement often added `[Engage]`) · **C. difficile** infection · **pneumonia**. Teach magnesium-rich foods: spinach, almonds, yogurt, avocado `[Engage]`.

#### Procedures and surgery?
- **Endoscopic dilation**: widens benign strictures; may need repeating.
- **Nissen fundoplication** (laparoscopic): the gastric fundus is wrapped around the lower esophagus → ==reinforces the LES==. For severe/refractory GERD or a large hiatal hernia.
- Post-procedure: **bleeding, swallowing, respiratory status**.

## III. Hiatal hernia

#### Sliding vs. paraesophageal?
| | Sliding | Paraesophageal (PEH) |
|---|---|---|
| What happens | Upper stomach **intermittently** slides up through the diaphragmatic hiatus | Stomach (± esophagus, intestines) **stays displaced** in the chest |
| Frequency | ==Most common== | Rare, more advanced `[Engage]` |
| Typical client | Increased abdominal pressure, age | **≥ 50** (median 65–75), central obesity, smoking `[Engage]` |
| Danger | Mostly GERD symptoms | ↓ blood supply → **emergency surgery** `[Engage]` |

#### Risk factors?
- ↑ intra-abdominal pressure: **chronic constipation, chronic cough / COPD, pregnancy** (multiple pregnancies)
- Age-related muscle weakness and ↓ elasticity (**55–60%** of people over 50 `[Engage]`) · central obesity · smoking · bone-degenerating skeletal disorders `[Engage]`

#### Findings?
- GERD-like: heartburn, regurgitation, sour taste, dysphagia, odynophagia, belching, nausea, hoarseness, **chronic cough**. Plus **early satiety, bloating**, postprandial fullness. Diarrhea is not expected `[Engage practice item]`.
- **Large hernias:** "heavy/full" epigastric feeling; **Cameron lesions** (erosions where the hernia rubs the diaphragm) → ==occult blood loss → iron-deficiency anemia== `[Engage]`.
- **Complications:** bleeding, obstruction, **volvulus** (the stomach twists → strangulation), impaired blood supply. If it presses on the lungs: dyspnea, atelectasis `[Engage]`.

#### Diagnostics?
- No lab test; sometimes found incidentally on chest x-ray `[Engage]`.
- **EGD** (anatomy + mucosa) · **manometry** (sensor catheter through the nose; measures esophageal muscle and sphincter function) · pH monitoring · **barium swallow** · **CT** for complex hernias or surgical planning.

#### Nursing care?
- ==Same as GERD:== **small, frequent meals**; avoid foods and habits that worsen reflux; no tight clothing; no tobacco; **HOB up**; exercise and weight loss.
- Ask whether regurgitation is **digested or undigested** food (undigested → consider other causes) `[Engage]`. Ask about chronic cough and asthma.
- Priority: relieve **pain, nausea, vomiting** `[Engage]`. Meds are the same as for GERD.
- After repair (laparoscopic Nissen): monitor **bleeding and respiratory status**.

> [!danger] Report immediately
> Hernia + sudden severe pain, vomiting/inability to tolerate intake, or signs of bleeding → suspect **obstruction, volvulus, or strangulation** (↓ blood supply). This is a surgical emergency.

## IV. Gastroparesis

#### What is happening?
- **Delayed (or absent) gastric emptying** from impaired motility, a kind of stomach paralysis. Causes: **vagal nerve dysfunction** + injury to gastric **pacemaker cells**.
- Food stays in the stomach too long → can harden into a **bezoar** (a mass of undigested material).

#### Causes?
- ==**Poorly controlled diabetes**== is a major (leading) cause (autonomic neuropathy).
- Vagal injury after **surgery** or other neurologic injury · scleroderma, Sjögren's, MS, hypothyroidism, stroke, myasthenia gravis, Parkinson's · CMV, EBV, VZV, Chagas `[Engage]`
- **Meds that slow emptying:** **opioids**, antidepressants (TCAs `[Engage]`), **calcium channel blockers**, **cannabis**, **GLP-1 agonists** (e.g., semaglutide `[ADDED]`). Usually temporary, but long-term opioids and antidepressants can make it chronic `[Engage]`.
#### Clinical findings?
- **Early satiety**, poor appetite, **nausea and recurrent vomiting**, bloating, abdominal pain, reflux, fatigue, weight loss.
- Classic clue: ==vomiting **undigested food several hours** after eating==.
#### Diagnosis?
- ==**Gastric-emptying scintigraphy**== is the reference ("gold standard") test: imaging tracks a labeled meal leaving the stomach.
- **Gastric emptying breath test:** noninvasive and nonradioactive; up to **4 hr**; can be done at home `[Engage]`. EGD for a baseline.

#### Diet and teaching?
- **Small, frequent meals** · **lower fat** · **lower insoluble fiber**; cooked/steamed fruits and vegetables `[Engage]` · chew thoroughly · don't lie down after eating
- Avoid **carbonated drinks** (bloating), alcohol, smoking, opioids · walk after meals `[Engage]`
- Fluids **1–1.5 L/day**; liquid supplements when intake is low; constipation → soluble fiber + fluids `[Engage]`
- **Monitor blood glucose**; work on A1C and avoid glucose swings `[Engage]`

#### Nursing priorities?
- ==Nutrition and hydration== · **BG monitoring** in diabetes · pain relief
- **Review meds** that worsen delayed emptying
- Recurrent vomiting → watch **electrolytes** and **vitamins** (thiamine, B12, fat-soluble) `[Engage]`
- Collaborate with the **dietitian** and GI

#### Gastroparesis meds?
| Drug | Action | Adverse effects | Teaching |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Metoclopramide** (Reglan) | Antidopaminergic prokinetic; stimulates GI muscle to move food | Drowsiness, agitation, ==**extrapyramidal effects**== (spasms, twitching); includes **tardive dyskinesia**, which can be permanent `[ADDED]` | **Empty stomach**, full glass of water; **≤ 12 wk** unless directed; ==report muscle twitching==; watch for depression / self-harm thoughts; no driving if drowsy `[Engage]` |
| **Erythromycin**, azithromycin | Motilin agonist → ↑ motility and emptying | Diarrhea, tachycardia, rash, fatigue `[Engage]`; QT prolongation (macrolide) `[ADDED]` | Report drug/food allergies; with or without food |
| **Ondansetron** (Zofran) | Serotonin (5-HT3) blocker → ↓ N/V | Headache, lightheadedness, blurred vision, fatigue, constipation, ==**prolonged QT**== | Report dizziness/fainting; **monitor K+ and Mg**; limit alcohol `[Engage]` |
| **Prochlorperazine** (Compazine) | Antiemetic; dopamine antagonist (phenothiazine) `[ADDED]` | See Source notes (error) | Same as above `[Engage]` |

- Domperidone is also listed with metoclopramide `[Engage]` [NOTE: current practice: not FDA-approved in the U.S.; available only through FDA expanded access. Verify.]
- Severe cases: temporary **NG tube feeding** → **jejunostomy tube** for long-term enteral nutrition `[Engage]`.

## V. Gastritis

#### What is it?
- **Inflammation of the stomach lining**, acute or chronic. The mucosa looks red and swollen on EGD.
- Severity ranges from mild inflammation to **bleeding or ulceration**. Untreated, it can progress to PUD. H. pylori ↑ gastric cancer risk `[Engage]`.

#### What causes it?
- ==**H. pylori**== (common cause of chronic gastritis) and ==**NSAIDs**==. Alcohol and other irritants worsen the injury.
- Spicy/fried/acidic foods, food poisoning, infections, smoking `[Engage]`.
- **Acute physiological stress** `[Engage]`: massive burns, head injury with ↑ ICP, sepsis, severe trauma, multiorgan failure → **stress-induced gastritis**. Critically ill clients get **prophylactic PPIs/acid reducers**.
- **Autoimmune (AMAG)** `[Engage]`: seen with autoimmune thyroiditis or T1DM → **B12 deficiency**, ↑ gastric cancer risk.
- Not caused by acetaminophen or gluten `[Engage practice item]`.
- Adults **> 65**: thinner mucosa + frequent OTC NSAID use → ↑ bleeding risk `[Engage]`.

#### Findings and diagnostics?
- Epigastric pain, N/V, ↓ appetite, bloating, belching. **Black tarry stools or blood in emesis = bleeding** `[Engage]`.
- **Assess for GI bleeding.** CBC (↓ RBC/Hgb = anemia/bleeding; ↑ WBC = infection); liver, kidney, pancreatic labs; stool for blood `[Engage]`.
- **EGD + biopsy** (gold standard for H. pylori `[Engage]`) · H. pylori testing when indicated · CT `[Engage]`.

#### Care and teaching?
- **Identify and reduce contributing factors**: stop NSAIDs and alcohol; avoid irritants (caffeine, spicy food).
- **Medication adherence:** PPI / H2 blocker / antacid; antibiotics (e.g., **metronidazole**) for H. pylori `[Engage]`. Complete the whole regimen, then **test of cure** (stool antigen or urea breath test) `[Engage]`.
- Prevent H. pylori reinfection: **handwashing, no shared utensils**, disinfect food-prep surfaces `[Engage]`.
- Stay upright **2–3 hr** after meals; stress reduction (guided breathing, mindfulness) `[Engage]`.
- Monitor hydration and nutrition. Reassess at **4 wk** `[Engage]`. AMAG: screen B12 and iron; lifelong surveillance `[Engage]`.

> [!danger] Report immediately
> **Hematemesis, melena, severe pain, or worsening symptoms**. Gastritis can bleed.

## VI. Peptic ulcer disease (PUD)

> [!quote] Anchor
> Ulceration happens when mucosal defenses are overwhelmed. The danger is sudden bleeding, perforation, or obstruction.

#### What causes PUD?
- ==**H. pylori** and **NSAIDs** (including aspirin)== are the major causes. H. pylori inflames the mucosa and alters bicarbonate secretion `[Engage]`.
- **Acid + pepsin** injure the exposed tissue. **Smoking** and other irritants impair healing; regular alcohol use.
- Hypersecretory states: **Zollinger-Ellison syndrome** `[Engage]` (a gastrin-secreting tumor → massive acid → multiple or refractory ulcers `[ADDED]`), hyperparathyroidism, cystic fibrosis `[Engage]`.
- Family history; liver, kidney, or lung disease `[Engage]`.

> [!info] Key contrast
> Stress and spicy food **do not cause** PUD (a myth), but spicy food ↑ acid and **aggravates an existing ulcer** `[Engage]`. The extreme physiological stress of critical illness is different: it causes stress-induced gastritis (Section V).

#### Gastric vs. duodenal ulcer?
| | Gastric | Duodenal |
|---|---|---|
| Pain timing | **After** meals | **Before** meals, ==relieved by eating== |
| Typical age | **> 40** | **20–50** |

`[Engage]`

#### Clinical presentation?
- **Epigastric burning or gnawing pain**, often after meals or **at night** `[Engage]` · N/V, bloating, early satiety, weight loss.
- **Some clients have few or no symptoms.** Complications can appear suddenly.
- **Older adults:** often silent early; the first sign is ==unexplained weight loss or ↓ appetite== → late diagnosis → hemorrhage, perforation, obstruction `[Engage]`.

#### Red flags?
> [!danger] Report immediately: treat as urgent
> | Complication | Signs |
> |---|---|
> | **GI bleeding** | Hematemesis, **coffee-ground emesis** (blood partly digested by acid `[ADDED]`), **melena** (black tarry stool = upper GI blood `[ADDED]`); orthostatic hypotension, tachycardia, dizziness, weakness `[Engage]` |
> | **Perforation** | ==Sudden severe abdominal pain + rigid abdomen==; fever `[Engage]` |
> | **Obstruction** (gastric outlet) | Persistent vomiting, early satiety, can't tolerate intake |
>
> Notify the provider immediately for any sign of perforation, infection, or bleeding `[Engage]`.

#### PUD diagnostics?
- ==**EGD + biopsy**== is the gold standard: direct view, biopsy, benign vs. malignant, H. pylori detection `[Engage]`.
- **Test every PUD client for H. pylori** `[Engage]`: rapid urease test on a biopsy (color change); histology; culture; **stool antigen** (more accurate and cheaper than antibodies); **urea breath test** (H. pylori urease splits labeled urea → labeled CO₂ exhaled); serum antibodies.
- **CBC** (blood loss) and other labs: LFTs, amylase, lipase `[Engage]`.
- Suspected perforation: **chest x-ray shows free air**; upper GI contrast study shows contrast leaking `[Engage]`.
- Suspected Zollinger-Ellison: serum **gastrin** + secretin stimulation test `[Engage]`.
- ==**Follow-up testing after H. pylori treatment**== confirms eradication. PPIs (and recent antibiotics/bismuth) can cause false negatives, so PPIs are usually held about **2 wk** before a breath or stool test `[ADDED — verify vs course materials]`.

#### PUD treatment?
- **PPIs are central** to ulcer healing (faster, more effective). Calcium supplement is often added for fracture risk `[Engage]`.
- **NSAID ulcer:** stop or reduce the NSAID; **misoprostol** for prevention.
- **H. pylori:** ==combination therapy== of two antibiotics (clarithromycin + metronidazole or amoxicillin) + a PPI (or the PCAB vonoprazan) for **7–14 days** `[Engage]`. **Adherence is essential for eradication.** [NOTE: current practice: 2024 ACG guidance favors **14-day bismuth quadruple** therapy when antibiotic susceptibility is unknown and avoids empiric clarithromycin triple therapy. The course version above is what the exam tests. Verify.]

#### PUD meds?
| Drug | Action | Adverse effects / contraindications | Teaching |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Misoprostol** (Cytotec) | Prostaglandin analog: ↓ acid, ↑ protective mucus → prevents NSAID ulcers | Diarrhea, abdominal pain, N/V, headache; ==**contraindicated in pregnancy**: causes miscarriage== `[Engage]` | Childbearing age: it **will cause spontaneous abortion**; if pregnant, **stop and notify** the provider; don't share it |
| **Vonoprazan** (Voquezna) | PCAB: blocks active and inactive proton pumps | Diarrhea, abdominal pain, bloating, nausea, taste change | With or without food; AEs ↑ when combined with antibiotics `[Engage]` |
| **Clarithromycin** (Biaxin) | Macrolide; inhibits H. pylori protein synthesis | Headache, **torsades / QT**, liver toxicity, **SJS**, **C. diff diarrhea**. **Contraindicated:** macrolide allergy, prolonged QT, electrolyte imbalance, prior clarithromycin liver injury `[Engage]` | Report rash, fever, diarrhea, superinfection; full course; may cause dizziness/confusion; separate from zidovudine by **4 hr** `[Engage]` |

#### H. pylori teaching?
- ==**Complete the entire regimen**== · review common antibiotic adverse effects · report **diarrhea, rash**, or signs of a serious reaction
- **Follow-up testing** after therapy · report persistent or worsening symptoms
- Take NSAIDs only if necessary, **with food**, as prescribed · Mg-rich foods on a PPI · avoid caffeine, alcohol, spicy food (strict diets are no longer routine) · stop smoking `[Engage]`
- Seek care for **black stools, vomiting blood, severe abdominal pain** `[Engage]`

#### PUD clinical judgment?
| Step | PUD focus |
|---|---|
| Recognize cues | Pain (timing vs. meals), vomiting, **bleeding**, intake, **vital signs**; auscultate, then palpate for **rigidity**; NSAID/ASA, alcohol, tobacco history `[Engage]` |
| Analyze | Likely cause (H. pylori vs. NSAID) and complications |
| Prioritize | ==**Bleeding, perforation** → hydration → pain== |
| Take action | Treatment: pain relief, control vomiting, **frequent VS**, monitor Hgb, **I&O**, dietitian `[Engage]` |
| Evaluate | Symptom relief; **no complications**; eradication confirmed |

#### Surgical management?
- For selected refractory or complicated disease (also nonadherent or high-risk clients `[Engage]`).
- **Vagotomy:** cuts selected vagal branches → ↓ acid stimulation. **Partial gastrectomy:** removes the affected stomach tissue.
- Post-op: **bleeding, infection, nutrition, GI function**.

## Self-quiz

> [!question]- 1. A client with GERD reports chest discomfort. What does the nurse do first?
> Assess the pain (onset, location, radiation, intensity, duration, associated symptoms) and CV risk factors. Rule out cardiac causes before attributing it to reflux.

> [!question]- 2. Which test confirms GERD, and how long does a Bravo capsule record?
> Ambulatory esophageal pH/reflux monitoring. Catheter ~24 hr; Bravo 48–96 hr.

> [!question]- 3. Name four GERD lifestyle teaching points with numbers.
> Finish meals ≥ 3 hr before bed; HOB up 6–9 in; don't lie down after eating; avoid tight clothing and triggers (spicy/fried food, caffeine, chocolate, alcohol).

> [!question]- 4. PPI vs. H2 blocker vs. antacid: how does each act?
> PPI suppresses acid secretion; H2 blocker reduces production by blocking histamine receptors; antacid neutralizes acid already present.

> [!question]- 5. Four things to monitor with long-term PPI use?
> ↓ Mg, ↓ Ca, ↓ B12; fractures; C. diff; pneumonia.

> [!question]- 6. What makes Barrett's esophagus dangerous, and how is it followed?
> Precancerous intestinal-type epithelium from chronic GERD → ↑ esophageal adenocarcinoma. Followed with surveillance EGD + biopsy.

> [!question]- 7. A client uses a fluticasone inhaler and has white plaques that burn. Likely cause and prevention?
> Oral candidiasis from the inhaled corticosteroid. Rinse the mouth with water after each use.

> [!question]- 8. Which oral findings need provider follow-up for possible cancer?
> A sore that doesn't heal, a white or red patch, a lump or cervical lymphadenopathy, dysphagia/numbness, voice change, unexplained weight loss.

> [!question]- 9. Sliding vs. paraesophageal hiatal hernia: which is the emergency risk and why?
> Paraesophageal: the stomach stays in the chest and can strangulate, twist (volvulus), or lose blood supply.

> [!question]- 10. A large hiatal hernia client has fatigue and low Hgb without visible bleeding. Explanation?
> Cameron lesions → occult blood loss → iron-deficiency anemia.

> [!question]- 11. Classic gastroparesis clue and the gold-standard test?
> Vomiting undigested food several hours after eating; gastric-emptying scintigraphy.

> [!question]- 12. A client on metoclopramide reports lip-smacking and twitching. Action and teaching?
> Report it: extrapyramidal effects (TD risk). Teaching: empty stomach with a full glass of water, ≤ 12 weeks unless directed, no driving if drowsy.

> [!question]- 13. Why monitor K+ and Mg with ondansetron?
> It prolongs QT. Low K+/Mg add to torsades risk.

> [!question]- 14. Gastric vs. duodenal ulcer: pain timing?
> Gastric: after meals. Duodenal: before meals, relieved by eating.

> [!question]- 15. A PUD client suddenly develops severe abdominal pain and a rigid abdomen. What's happening, and what's the priority?
> Suspected perforation. Urgent: notify the provider immediately and monitor VS. Chest x-ray shows free air.

> [!question]- 16. Which PUD findings indicate bleeding?
> Hematemesis, coffee-ground emesis, melena, orthostatic hypotension, tachycardia, dizziness, falling Hgb.

> [!question]- 17. A 26-year-old client is prescribed misoprostol with her NSAID. Key teaching?
> It causes spontaneous abortion and is contraindicated in pregnancy. If pregnant, stop it and notify the provider.

> [!question]- 18. What confirms H. pylori was eradicated, and what adherence teaching matters most?
> Follow-up test of cure (stool antigen or urea breath test). Complete the entire antibiotic + acid-suppression regimen.

> [!question]- 19. An older adult's first sign of PUD is often what?
> Unexplained weight loss or decreased appetite, because early disease is often painless.
