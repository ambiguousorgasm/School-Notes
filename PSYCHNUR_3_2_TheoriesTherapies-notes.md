## I. Why theories matter

> [!quote] Anchor
> Why does a person behave the way they do — and why might maladaptive patterns develop? Theory is not a diagnosis; it is a lens for assessment, communication, and intervention.

- Failure to reach a developmental stage or unresolved internal conflict → maladaptive responses → mental illness can emerge `[Engage]`.

## II. Psychoanalytic and psychodynamic (Freud)

#### Id, ego, superego?

| Part | Function | Example `[Engage]` |
|---|---|---|
| **Id** | Immediate gratification; primitive drives (unconscious) | Takes someone's car because they want it |
| **Ego** | Rational mediator/decision maker between id and superego; conscious + unconscious | Sees a nice car, decides to buy one |
| **Superego** | Morality, rules, ideals (learned from culture) | Admires a car without touching it |

- Behavior reflects conflict among the three. Conscious and unconscious processes both drive behavior.

#### What are defense mechanisms, and how does the nurse respond?
- **Unconscious** strategies the ego uses to protect against anxiety or internal conflict.
- Lecture examples `[ADDED]` definitions:
  - **Denial** — refusing to acknowledge a painful reality ("I don't have a drinking problem").
  - **Projection** — attributing one's own unacceptable feelings to someone else ("The staff hate me" when the client is angry at staff).
  - **Displacement** — redirecting emotion to a safer target (angry at boss, yells at partner).
- Nursing: recognize without shaming; look for the **underlying anxiety**; use **therapeutic communication, not confrontation**.

#### Psychosexual stages?

| Stage | Age | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| Oral | **Birth–1 y** | Mouth; feeding/sucking |
| Anal | **1–3 y** | Control of elimination (toilet training) |
| Phallic | **3–6 y** | Genital focus; caregiver attachment |
| Latency | **6–12 y** | Peer relationships and skills; libido repressed |
| Genital | **13+ y** | Independence, mature relationships |

- Must progress in order to mature `[Engage]`. (Textbook lists genital as 13–18; lecture 13+.)

#### Psychodynamic theory adds what?
- Childhood experiences shape adult personality and ways of relating; behavior = **nature + nurture**.
- Nursing: understand the person behind the symptom and the life history driving current behavior.

## III. Interpersonal theories (Sullivan, Peplau)

#### Sullivan's three selves?
- **Good me** — approval, positive feedback.
- **Bad me** — disapproval, anxiety.
- **Not me** — threatening parts kept outside awareness (repressed).
- **Security operations** = self-system's defenses against anxiety that protect self-esteem.
- Nursing: relationships shape self-concept; plan interactions to fit developmental level.

> [!info] Sullivan's stages `[Engage]`
> Infancy (**0–18 mo**) gratification of needs · childhood (**18 mo–6 y**) delayed gratification · juvenile (**6–9**) peer groups · preadolescence (**9–12**) same-gender relationships · early adolescence (**12–14**) identity · late adolescence (**14–21**) lasting intimate relationships.

#### Peplau: four phases?

| Phase | What happens | Nurse's role |
|---|---|---|
| **Orientation** | Identify problems, establish trust, gather data | Information-gatherer, trust-builder, **boundary-setter** |
| **Identification** | Client connects with available help | Builds relationship |
| **Exploitation** | Client actively **uses** resources and support | Collaborative partner; supports progress toward goals |
| **Resolution** | Needs met; healthy separation → independence | Facilitator of healthy separation |

> [!tip] Instructor emphasis
> Peplau = "the nurse–client relationship **is itself** a therapeutic tool" — **therapeutic use of self**. "Exploitation" here is positive: the client makes full use of services.

## IV. Behavioral, cognitive, humanistic

#### Watson, Pavlov, Skinner — how do they differ?

| | Watson (behaviorism) | Pavlov (classical) | Skinner (operant) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core | Only observable behavior counts; environment shapes it | Neutral stimulus paired with another → learned response | Consequences change likelihood of behavior |
| Key word | Environment | **Association** | **Consequences** |
| Psych use | Learned behaviors can be modified | Learned fear, **specific phobias**; build adaptive responses | Structured reinforcement programs (e.g., OCD `[Engage]`, token/contingency systems) |

- Behavioral approaches change the maladaptive response **without needing to uncover its original cause**.
- Classical: the response can occur even when the original stimulus is absent.

> [!info] Terminology check `[ADDED]`
> Lecture wording: positive consequences strengthen behavior, negative consequences reduce it. In formal operant terms, **reinforcement** (positive = add something pleasant; negative = remove something unpleasant) **increases** behavior; **punishment decreases** it. "Negative reinforcement" is not punishment.

#### Cognitive theory chain?
- **Thoughts → emotions → behavior.** Maladaptive thinking → maladaptive behavior. Example: belief "I can't lose weight with diet/exercise" → purging `[Engage]`.
- **Social cognitive theory**: modeling behaviors observed in others (peer pressure) `[Engage]`.

#### Humanistic theory?
- Whole person; people inherently capable of growth (innately good); illness = movement away from healthy functioning. Theorists: **Maslow, Rogers, Bugental**.
- `[ADDED]` **Maslow** — hierarchy of needs: physiological → safety → love/belonging → esteem → self-actualization; basis for prioritizing physiologic and safety needs first.
- **Rogers** — people perceive the world through their experiences, striving toward self-actualization `[Engage]`; `[ADDED]` client-centered therapy built on **unconditional positive regard**, empathy, genuineness.
- **Bugental** — humans are a single entity that can't be broken into parts; conscious choice and responsibility `[Engage]`.

## V. Developmental theories

> [!warning] Priority
> Age does **not** automatically tell you the most important developmental task. Development continues across the lifespan; use theory to tailor communication and interventions.

#### Piaget's stages?

| Stage | Age | High-yield concept |
|---|---|---|
| Sensorimotor | **Birth–2** | **Object permanence**; learns through senses/motor |
| Preoperational | **2–6** | **Egocentrism**, symbolic thinking, animism |
| Concrete operational | **6–12** | Logic with **concrete** situations; classification |
| Formal operational | **12+** | **Abstract** thinking, hypothesis testing |

#### Erikson's stages?

| Age | Conflict | Success → / Failure → |
|---|---|---|
| **Birth–18 mo** | Trust vs. mistrust | Basic trust in caregiver / mistrust when needs unmet |
| **18 mo–3 y** | Autonomy vs. shame & doubt | Self-control, confidence / shame when independence restricted |
| **3–6 y** | Initiative vs. guilt | Purpose, self-directed activity / excessive guilt |
| **6–12 y** | Industry vs. inferiority | Competence via skills/recognition / inadequacy (harsh or unrealistic expectations) |
| **~12–20 y** | Identity vs. role confusion | Sense of self, direction / confusion about role and values |
| **~20–30 y** | Intimacy vs. isolation | Lasting emotional bonds / isolation |
| **~30–65 y** | Generativity vs. stagnation | Contribution, life goals / unproductive, self-absorbed |
| **~65–death** | Ego integrity vs. despair | Meaning, self-worth, no fear of death / regret, depression, anger |
| **~80+** | Transcendence (Joan Erikson) | Purpose, spirituality / hopelessness, despair |

> [!tip] Exam mindset
> Ask: **what developmental task is this client expected to work through at this age?** Unresolved conflict affects confidence, relationships, identity, coping.

#### Kohlberg's moral levels?

| Level | Typical age `[Engage]` | Stages |
|---|---|---|
| **Preconventional** | **4–10** | 1 Punishment & obedience · 2 Instrumental relativist (self-interest) |
| **Conventional** | **10–13** | 3 Interpersonal concordance (approval) · 4 Law & order |
| **Postconventional** | Adolescence+ | 5 Social contract/legalistic · 6 Universal ethical principles (justice, dignity) |

- Levels aren't age-locked. Nursing link: every client, regardless of diagnosis, has a right to treatment.

## VI. Biopsychosocial model and neurobiology

#### Three domains?
- **Biological**: brain anatomy, genetics, neurotransmitters, medications. **Psychological**: thoughts, emotions, coping, development. **Social**: relationships, environment, life experience. (Engel, **1977** `[Engage]`.)

#### Nervous system basics?
- CNS = brain + spinal cord. PNS = everything outside → somatic + autonomic. **Sympathetic** = fight-or-flight → relevant to anxiety and trauma disorders.
- Foundation for psychiatric meds and brain-stimulation therapies.

#### Neurotransmitter associations?

| Neurotransmitter | General role | Lecture association |
|---|---|---|
| **Dopamine** | Movement, reward/motivation | Schizophrenia (excess `[ADDED]`); substance use |
| **Serotonin** | Pain, sleep, digestion | **Low in depression** |
| **Norepinephrine** | ↑ BP/HR, mood regulation | Mood |
| **GABA** | Primary **inhibitory** signal | Anxiety/panic (low GABA activity `[ADDED]`) |
| **Glutamate** | **Excitatory**; learning/memory | Learning/memory |
| **Histamine** | Arousal/LOC; regulates other NTs | **Sedation** from some meds (antihistamine effect `[ADDED]`) |

## VII. Therapies

> [!quote] Anchor
> Treatment may combine medication, psychotherapy, supportive services, and other approaches. Some explore the past, some target thoughts or behaviors, some target relationships or the environment.

#### Psychoanalytic therapy aims to do what?
- Talk-based; bring **unconscious material into awareness**; gain insight; express repressed emotion. Hypnosis sometimes used `[Engage]`.

#### Transference vs. countertransference?

| | Transference | Countertransference |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | **Client → nurse/provider** | **Nurse/provider → client** |
| Source | Client's important past relationship | Provider's own past experiences |
| Example `[Engage]` | Client calls nurse "dear" and "honey" as with her grandmother | Nurse brings extra popsicles to a child who reminds her of her sibling |
| Risk | Current relationship stands in for an earlier one | Can be positive or negative; **impairs objectivity** if unrecognized |

#### Behavioral vs. cognitive vs. CBT?

| | Behavioral | Cognitive | CBT |
|---|---|---|---|
| Target | Maladaptive behaviors/responses | Present-day distorted thoughts | Thoughts + emotions + learned behaviors |
| Method | Learning/conditioning; no need to find cause | Identify and examine thoughts that amplify distress | Recognize distortions, test against reality, practice new behaviors, build coping/emotion regulation |
| Common use | **Phobias, SUD** | Anxiety | Broad |

#### Individual, group, family therapy?
- **Individual**: one client + therapist; highly individualized goals; trust is key.
- **Group**: clients learn from each other, share coping; realize they're not alone. Format varies by age: **play** (children), **peer relationships** (adolescents), **socialization/shared memories** (older adults) `[Engage]`.
- **Family**: treats the **family system**, not one person; **communication** is central. Uses: grief, conflict, substance use, divorce, behavior problems.

> [!info] Group phases `[Engage]`
> **Forming** (orientation: norms, confidentiality) → **storming** (members test roles, conflict) → **norming** (cohesion, alliances) → **working/performing** (growth, teamwork) → **adjourning** (termination, review outcomes).

#### What makes something milieu therapy?
- The **environment is structured as treatment** to reduce maladaptive behavior. Inpatient or outpatient.
- Components: therapeutic nurse–client relationship · structured daily routine · socialization · self-care support · safe, supportive environment.
- Example `[Engage]`: dayroom noise ↑ client's anxiety → nurse suggests exercising in another room = milieu therapy.

#### Biological therapies — vocabulary?
- **Psychopharmacology** (major category) + brain stimulation; often combined with other therapies. Details later in course. Brief definitions `[ADDED]`:
  - **ECT** — electrically induced seizure under anesthesia; severe/treatment-resistant depression.
  - **rTMS** — magnetic pulses to the scalp; no anesthesia, client awake.
  - **MST** — magnetically induced seizure under anesthesia.
  - **VNS** — implanted generator stimulates the vagus nerve.
  - **DBS** — surgically implanted electrodes in the brain.

#### Telehealth: pros and cons?

| Benefits | Concerns |
|---|---|
| ↑ Access; homebound clients; fewer delays; may ↓ stigma; off-hours contact may prevent ED visits `[Engage]` | Impersonal for some; privacy; technology access and fit |

- Example `[Engage]`: family finds weekly trips to therapy an obstacle → telehealth.

#### Complementary and alternative approaches?
- Yoga (mood), exercise (mood; ↓ med side effects like fatigue, **weight gain**), meditation (↓ anxiety), pet therapy, diet (omega-3s may help depression/schizophrenia) `[Engage]`. Not FDA-approved for mental illness `[Engage]`.
- Nurse supports **informed decision-making**; don't present CAM as an automatic replacement for evidence-based treatment. They may be used **alongside** medication.

## VIII. The nurse's role across therapies

#### What does the nurse do (vs. the therapist)?
- Providing formal psychotherapy requires a **master's-prepared PMH nurse** with credentialing `[Engage]`.
- Generalist nurse: know each therapy's purpose · assess and identify needs · **recognize when a client may benefit** · educate and reinforce goals/homework · document interventions and responses · collaborate · **advocate for right to treatment**. May **lead** psychoeducation groups `[Engage]`.

#### Therapy-specific nursing focus?

| Therapy | Nursing focus / teaching `[Engage]` |
|---|---|
| Behavioral | Identify maladaptive behaviors; teach **adaptive vs. maladaptive coping** |
| Cognitive | Encourage reality-based, positive thinking; safe place to talk about the past |
| CBT | Acknowledge feelings; explore alternative actions; **psychoeducation** |
| Group | Encourage all members to participate; teach topic (coping, stress) |
| Family | Recognize family dysfunction; explain expectations of therapy |
| Milieu | Therapeutic relationship, **safety**, ongoing assessment, maintain milieu at all times |
| Telehealth | Explain option, help set up, teach privacy |

> [!tip] Across every modality
> Trust, therapeutic communication, safety, recovery focus, client-centered goals stay central — even when the nurse isn't delivering the therapy. Support coping, create a safe space for expression, use **trauma-sensitive** approaches, promote engagement and autonomy.

## IX. Mental health promotion and prevention

#### How to assess suicide risk?
- Ask **directly**, in a **private one-on-one** setting.
- **Differentiate suicide from nonsuicidal self-harm** — clients may see them as different topics.
- Warning signs: talking about wanting to die · social withdrawal · **giving away possessions** · frequent talk of death · significant emotional or physical pain.

> [!danger] Inpatient suicide precautions (milieu)
> 1:1 observation when indicated · document location at defined intervals (**every 15 min** `[Engage]`) · search belongings for self-harm objects per policy · ensure prescribed meds are actually taken · maintain a safe, therapeutic environment.

#### Resilience and PCEs?
- **Resilience** = positive adaptation to adversity: determination, hope, ability to bounce back.
- **Positive childhood experiences**: talking about feelings, reliable support in hard times, sense of belonging. More PCEs → better mental health `[Engage]`. Nurse reinforces safety and caring relationships.

#### Three levels of prevention?

| Level | When | Goal | Example `[Engage]` |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Before illness | Reduce risk | Teaching a junior-high class healthy coping |
| **Secondary** | Early illness | Detect and intervene | Suicide risk scale for a client showing depression |
| **Tertiary** | Established illness | Reduce disability, support quality of life | Teaching a client with bipolar disorder to recognize early relapse signs (e.g., not sleeping) |

- **Promotion** strengthens wellness; **prevention** reduces onset or impact of illness.

#### Back at the bedside?
- Before intervening, ask: what's shaping behavior now — development, thoughts, relationships, environment, biology, or a mix?
- Match communication to developmental level; use the relationship intentionally; think beyond the diagnosis.

## Self-quiz

> [!question]- 1. A client thinks the staff hate him when he is the one who is angry. Which defense mechanism, and how should the nurse respond?
> Projection. Recognize it without shaming, look for the underlying anxiety, use therapeutic communication rather than confrontation.

> [!question]- 2. Name Peplau's phases in order. In which phase does the client actively use services?
> Orientation → identification → exploitation → resolution; exploitation.

> [!question]- 3. Nurse sets boundaries and gathers data at the first meeting. Which Peplau phase?
> Orientation.

> [!question]- 4. A client with a dog phobia was bitten as a child. Which theorist best explains the fear?
> Pavlov — classical conditioning (learned association).

> [!question]- 5. A unit gives privileges for attending groups. Which theory?
> Skinner — operant conditioning (consequences shape behavior).

> [!question]- 6. A 4-year-old believes everyone sees what she sees. Piaget stage and concept?
> Preoperational; egocentrism.

> [!question]- 7. A 45-year-old feels he has contributed nothing and is disengaged. Erikson stage?
> Generativity vs. stagnation.

> [!question]- 8. A 70-year-old reviews her life with regret and wants "a second try." Which outcome of which stage?
> Despair — ego integrity vs. despair.

> [!question]- 9. Client calls the nurse "honey," just as she did her grandmother. Transference or countertransference?
> Transference (client → nurse).

> [!question]- 10. A nurse feels protective of a teen who resembles her brother and gives extra privileges. What is this, and why does it matter?
> Countertransference; it can impair objectivity and judgment if unrecognized.

> [!question]- 11. Client teaching on behavioral therapy: which statement shows understanding — "I'll change negative thoughts," "My peers will give feedback," "My brother will help work out our dysfunction," or "I'll learn adaptive vs. maladaptive coping"?
> "I'll learn adaptive vs. maladaptive coping." (Others = cognitive, group, family.)

> [!question]- 12. Structured routines, socialization, and a safe environment are core to which therapy?
> Milieu therapy.

> [!question]- 13. Which neurotransmitter is low in depression? Which is the primary inhibitory one linked to anxiety?
> Serotonin; GABA.

> [!question]- 14. A client asks if she can do yoga and meditation while on her antidepressant. Best answer?
> Yes — complementary approaches can be used alongside medication; support informed decisions and don't stop the prescribed treatment.

> [!question]- 15. Can a generalist staff RN conduct CBT sessions? What is her role?
> No — formal therapy requires a master's-prepared, credentialed PMH nurse. She assesses, recognizes need, reinforces goals/homework, educates, documents, advocates.

> [!question]- 16. Name three suicide warning signs and the correct interview setting.
> Talking about wanting to die, withdrawal, giving away possessions (also frequent death talk, severe pain); ask directly in a private one-on-one setting.

> [!question]- 17. Screening a depressed client with a suicide scale is which level of prevention?
> Secondary.

> [!question]- 18. Which Kohlberg level centers on law and order and approval from others?
> Conventional.

> [!question]- 19. Which therapy explores the unconscious to bring repressed material into awareness?
> Psychoanalytic therapy.

> [!question]- 20. The biopsychosocial model's three domains and one example each?
> Biological (neurotransmitters, genetics) · psychological (thoughts, coping) · social (relationships, environment).
