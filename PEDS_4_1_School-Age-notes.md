## I. Biological development

> [!quote] Anchor
> Steady growth, refined motor skills, and school becomes the child's world: peers and accomplishment drive development.

### Physical growth

#### How fast do school-age children grow?
- Steady pace driven by genetics, nutrition, activity: **2–2.5 in** and **4–7 lb** per year. Monitor **height, weight, BMI on CDC growth charts**.
- Height **~115 cm (45 in)** at 6 → **150–152 cm (59–60 in)** at 12; females slightly taller at 12.
- Weight **~20 kg (44 lb)** at 6 → **~40 kg (88 lb)** at 12.

#### What dental and pubertal changes occur?
- Deciduous teeth lost, permanent teeth erupt from **~6 yr**, earlier in females.
- Puberty begins **~10** in females, **~12** in males `[Engage]`. Both sexes: underarm and pubic hair. Males: chest hair, testicular and penile growth. Females: breast budding → ==menarche about **2–3 yr** later==.

#### Child reports vague stomach aches or limb pain?
- ↑ body awareness can bring physical complaints. Advise parents to **investigate to rule out a medical cause**; this also reassures the child that parents care about their health.

### Motor skills

#### Gross vs. fine motor milestones?
| | Gross motor | Fine motor |
|---|---|---|
| Pattern | Smooth, strong; coordination, endurance, balance | Writing, dressing, household chores (cleaning, making bed) `[Engage]` |
| Examples | **From 6:** running, jumping rope; team sports | **6:** ties shoelaces · **9:** most dress and groom self |
| Goal | **≥ 1 hr** activity daily | Keep refining writing, drawing, painting |

> [!warning] Priority
> Milestones are guidelines and variation is normal, but a child who misses one **should be observed and evaluated**. ==Earlier developmental services = better outcomes.== Parents, teachers, or caregivers with concerns → discuss with the provider. `[Engage case: 6-yr-old with fine motor delay → Early Intervention referral]`

### Psychosocial, cognitive, language, play

#### What drives psychosocial development?
- **School involvement.** Friends and peer groups grow in importance; children **mirror others' behavior**; ==peer acceptance is crucial==.
- Friend groups mostly **same-sex**, more accepting of the opposite sex near adolescence.
- Exposure to violence, sexuality, illicit drugs via social media and peers → give chances to **openly discuss concerns and feelings** and correct misconceptions.

#### Cognitive milestones?
- Early school years: fundamentals and routines to adjust to the classroom. **By grade 3:** complex concepts (fractions, the cosmos).
- Attention: on task **15 min at 6**, **1 hr by 9**.

#### Language milestones?
- Start of period: simple complete sentences of **5–7 words** `[Engage]`. **By 10:** follows **5 commands** in a row `[Engage]`.
- Reading begins **~5**; grade 3 shifts from decoding words to **comprehension**; **10–12:** reads well independently.
- Language delay → frustration, "acting out." Ask about trouble understanding or expressing → anticipate **speech-language pathologist** referral `[Engage]`.

#### Play?
- Highly active. **~6:** fantasy play alone or with same-sex friends → later **structured, competitive group play with rules**; team sports teach rules and friendship.
- Mix of **unstructured** (running, swimming, biking, playgrounds) and **structured** (organized sports) supports development.

> Neither source names a developmental theorist. Erikson (**industry vs. inferiority**) and Piaget (**concrete operations**) for this age are NCLEX staples. Check the assigned textbook.

## II. Bullying

#### What counts as bullying, and when does it peak?
- Physical or verbal attacks, **social exclusion, gossip, property damage**; includes **sexual bullying** (harassment) and **cyberbullying**. Harm or distress to the targeted child.
- ~**1 in 3** children globally report being bullied; ==highest in middle school==.
- Higher risk `[Engage]`: LGB students, female students; cyberbullying victims usually also bullied in person.

#### Findings that should prompt a bullying assessment?
- Physical injury, **depression, anxiety, sleep disturbance**, poor grades, ==**poor school attendance**==, ↓ belonging, ↑ **self-harm**.
- **Psychosomatic** complaints (physical symptoms driven by psychological distress `[ADDED]`): tiredness, poor appetite, stomach pain, difficulty sleeping, headache, back pain, dizziness.

#### Prevention and reporting?
- Effective programs: school + community; **education, advocacy, positive peer relations**; empathy and conflict-resolution teaching; address **sibling bullying**.
- Anyone can recognize, intervene, and report. Children/parents report to **school** officials and seek **primary care and mental health** follow-up.

> [!danger] Report immediately
> Notify **law enforcement** if bullying involves ==cyber or sexual bullying, a weapon, serious bodily harm, or hate-motivated threats== `[Engage]`.

> [!example] Worked example `[Engage case]`
> 11-yr-old with **14 missed school days**, insomnia, withdrawn, no eye contact, quit online gaming with friends. Nurse interviews him **privately** → cyberbullying (memes, exclusion). Further assess: psychosomatic symptoms, **depression screening**, school anti-bullying programs. Success = ↑ attendance, ↑ grades, class engagement, fewer peer concerns.

## III. Psychosocial behaviors

### Self-esteem and body image

#### What builds self-esteem at this age?
- Self-esteem = subjective sense of one's own worth `[Engage]`. Linked to **academic achievement** and **parental support/trust**.
- High self-esteem → better adaptation, relationships, grades, mental and physical health; ↑ learning engagement.
- Children must learn to handle change, frustration, and failure **without losing self-esteem**. Nurse gives child and parent room to express challenges; interventions target **school performance** and **parent relationships**.

#### Body image: how common is dissatisfaction?
- Awareness from **~6 yr**; ages **6–8** have many questions about changing bodies.
- ==**40–50%** of school-age children report body dissatisfaction==; most want to be **smaller**.
- Shaped by comments from peers, family, adults; physical change, culture, media, social norms.

#### Nursing actions for body image?
- **Assess the child's own self-perception.** Warm, supportive environment; model positive body image.
- Communicate expected growth changes; address beauty norms; **combat size-based stereotypes**; ↓ negative social media exposure.
- Promote activity for **health, not appearance** → lifelong habits.

### Sexuality

#### How does the nurse assess gender identity and orientation?
- Gender identity can be recognized as early as **18 months**; sexual orientation forms **10–17 yr**. Ideals change → **periodically reassess** and update the record with consent.
- Concealing identity is linked to **negative health outcomes** → assess, acknowledge, give a safe space for questions.

> [!tip] NCLEX tip
> Gender identity: "Some kids feel like a girl inside, some a boy, both, or neither; there's no right answer. ==**How do you feel on the inside?**==" Orientation: use words like "**crush**." Give information, then time to process, then invite questions to normalize it `[Engage]`.

- Many children get misinformation from peers, media, internet `[Engage]`. Respect personal, cultural, religious values; integrate into routine health promotion (respect, consent, relationships).

### Discipline

#### Why not corporal punishment?
- Spanking/hitting does **not** help children manage feelings or behave better; → more **aggression and anger**, a cycle of misbehavior and punishment.
- Linked to ↓ **gray matter** and **lower IQ** in young adults; behavioral disorders, ↓ self-esteem, depression, ==substance use, self-harm, suicide attempts==; WHO: poor school performance, dropout, cancer, CVD, obesity.

#### Effective discipline techniques?
| Technique | How |
|---|---|
| Positive reinforcement | Praise, encouragement, small rewards |
| Clear expectations + consistency | Age-appropriate rules, enforced the same way every time |
| Logical consequences | Tied to the misbehavior (won't clean up → loses related play privilege) |
| Time-out / time-in | Time-out **1 min per year of age**, for specific behaviors (aggression, defiance); time-in = sit with child to co-regulate |
| Redirecting, modeling, communication | Redirect to better behavior; adults **stay calm**; active listening |
| Problem-solving + empathy | Child helps find solutions; discuss effects on others |

- School-wide: **PBIS** (Positive Behavioral Interventions and Supports), an evidence-based framework for behavior and well-being `[Engage]`. Refer to individual/family therapy when needed.

### Stress and fears

#### How does stress show up in a school-age child?
- Children often **don't recognize stress** → it appears physically or behaviorally. Sources: self, parents, teachers, peers, society; positive or negative changes (new activity, growth, academics, home change).
- **Physical:** rapid breathing, ↑ HR, muscle tension, GI upset, headache, eating changes, **new or recurrent enuresis** `[Engage]`, sleep disturbance, nightmares, vague complaints.
- **Behavioral:** anxiety, new fears, clinginess, anger, emotional instability, ==**regression**==, avoiding activities.

#### Fear vs. phobia?
| | Common fear | Phobia |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | Being alone, the dark, large animals | Fear of animals → refuses to go outdoors |
| Course | Mild, transient; resolves with reassurance | Persistent, irrational; ==disrupts daily activities== |
| Action | Reassure and support | Refer to **behavioral therapist** |

#### How do nurses build healthy stress responses?
- Assess and **acknowledge the stressor**, give time to share feelings; help the child understand it while **finding their own solution**.
- Protective: past success coping, high self-esteem, family/friend support. Strategies: **routine**, limit fear-inducing media, physical activity, **let the child make choices**.
- Unresolved stress → provider, counselor, or therapist.

## IV. School health and injury prevention

#### What do school health programs and nurses do?
- Children spend **~6 hr/day** in school (slide says 6–8). Chronic conditions and disparities in nutrition, exercise, and care → missed school, poorer grades.
- **CDC Healthy Schools + WSCC** (Whole School, Whole Community, Whole Child): nutrition, activity, chronic-condition support, health education/literacy, social-emotional learning, school/community services. ==Childhood habits beat adult behavior change.==
- **School nurse:** access to insurance, care coordination, services during the day, ==**immunization adherence**==, chronic condition management; expanded role post-COVID.
- SDOH domains `[Engage]`: economic stability, education access, health care access, built environment, social/community context.

#### Bicycle safety teaching?
- School-age children = ==**highest risk** for bicycle injury==. **Always wear a helmet**; helmet laws ↓ injuries and deaths.
- Day: **fluorescent** clothing. Night: **retro-reflective** clothing/materials + active lights (**white front, red rear**).

#### Bus safety teaching?
- Wait **≥ 10 ft from the curb**; board only on the **driver's signal**; stay seated until signaled to exit.
- Driver can't see **directly in front or behind** the bus → ==never walk behind it== or stop to tie shoes/pick up items near it; **make eye contact** with the driver before crossing in front.

#### Social media and internet?
- 2023 Surgeon General advisory: harms **sleep**, ↑ depression and anxiety, unrealistic expectations; ↓ social relationships and activity. Risks: false information, illegal activity (trafficking `[Engage]`).
- Teach: guard **name, school, photos, passwords**; tell reliable from unreliable sources; don't click suspicious links or download unknown files; cyberbullying and digital footprint.
- Parents **tell the child they will monitor** (builds trust); focus on **quality** use over time limits.

#### Firearm safety?
- Firearms = ==the leading cause of injury-related death under 18== `[Engage]`; accidental shooting death **4×** more likely in homes with guns `[Engage]`.
- **Safest: no firearm in the home.** Otherwise: **unloaded, locked, ammunition stored separately**, child can't access keys/combination.
- **> 1/3** of unintentional child shootings occur at friends', neighbors', or relatives' homes `[Engage]` → ==ask about guns and storage== before visits.
- Teach that media/video-game gun violence is unrealistic; guns kill in real life.

> [!warning] Priority
> Wrong answer on exams: "make sure the child knows where the gun is stored." Right: guns and ammunition stored **separately**, locked, inaccessible `[Engage practice items]`.

## V. Health promotion

### Nutrition

#### Why focus on diet now?
- Habits formed in school age are ==easier to build and more lasting== than changing adult behavior; unhealthy habits carry into adulthood.

#### Daily intake targets?
| Item | Target |
|---|---|
| Calories | **1,200–2,600/day** by age, sex, activity. `[Engage]`: 5–8 yr **1,200–2,000**; 9–13 F **1,400–2,200**, M **1,600–2,600** |
| Vegetables / fruit | **1–3.5 cups** / **1–2 cups** |
| Grains / dairy / protein | **3–9 oz** / **2–3 cups** / **2–6.5 oz** |
| Added sugar · saturated fat | **< 10%** of daily calories each `[Engage for sat fat]` |
| Sodium | **< 1,500 mg** (4–8 yr) · **< 1,800 mg** (9–13 yr) `[Engage]` |

- Offer fruits, vegetables, whole grains, lean meat, fish, poultry, lentils, beans; **low-fat or fat-free dairy**; ==water instead of sugary drinks==; healthy breakfast and lunch at home or school; review school menus together.
- Fried-food habit → swap preparation (**baked** chicken, baked vegetable fries) `[Engage practice item]`.

### Physical activity and sleep

#### Activity recommendation?
- **≥ 60 min/day**: **aerobic** (running, biking), **muscle-strengthening** (push-ups, climbing), **bone-strengthening** (jumping, running).
- Benefits: ↓ BP, obesity, diabetes; ↓ depression; ↑ grades, memory, attention. Family walks/bike rides, parks, school sports.

#### Sleep recommendation and hygiene?
- ==**9–12 hr/night**== for ages 6–12. Too little → obesity, diabetes, attention/behavior problems, poor mental health, injuries.
- Same bedtime and wake time daily · daytime activity · **no heavy meals or caffeine before bed** · ==**electronics out of the bedroom**== · dark, quiet room.

### Substance use

#### When does use start, and who is at risk?
- Reported as early as **9–10 yr** `[Engage]` (deck: 10): alcohol, tobacco, marijuana, illicit and prescription drugs. Early alcohol/tobacco → later misuse and illicit drug use.
- **Risk:** early aggressive behavior, mental health problems `[Engage]`, **parental substance use**, parental disengagement, feeling unsafe.
- **Protective:** self-regulation skills, reliable caregivers, safe environment, positive peers.

#### Nursing role?
- Teach: no smoking, alcohol, or ==medications not prescribed to you==; correct misconceptions; discuss substance-related ads and media.
- **Screen for substance use at routine well visits** → intervene and refer. Non-using parents and friends model prevention.
- Community: minimum-age purchase laws, education campaigns, ad limits. Families with SUD need **family-centered treatment**.

> [!tip] NCLEX tip
> School-age children rarely use substances, but ==prevention education starts now==, before use begins.

## VI. Health concerns

### Learning disabilities (LD)

#### What is an LD?
- Disorders affecting how a child **acquires, organizes, retains, comprehends, or applies** information. ==IQ is average or above==. Not caused by emotional disorders, culture, or disadvantage; **not corrected** by glasses or hearing aids.
- ~**5%** globally, **10%** in U.S.; chronic conditions → **2×** as likely. Usually emerges **6–12 yr**. Cause: hereditary + environmental.
- **Risk factors:** family history, poverty, prematurity, **prenatal alcohol**, **TBI**, other neurodevelopmental disorders.
- **Comorbid:** ODD (persistent angry/irritable, argumentative, defiant behavior toward authority `[ADDED]`), ADHD, anxiety, OCD.

#### Types?
| Disorder | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| ==**Dyslexia**== (most common, **≥ 80%** of LDs) | Reading |
| Dyscalculia | Numbers/math |
| Dysgraphia | Writing |
| ADHD | Attention, hyperactivity |
| DCD | Motor coordination |
| DLD | Language development |

#### Nursing role?
- **Developmental screening** → early identification; interprofessional care (speech therapist, psychologist, educators).
- Inclusive school: **IEP** (individualized education plan: a written, legally required plan of special-education services and accommodations `[ADDED]`), trained staff, resources.
- Screening example: an **8-yr-old who can't print their name** warrants assessment `[Engage practice item]`.

### ADHD

#### Manifestations and diagnosis?
- **Hyperactivity, impulsivity, difficulty sustaining attention**; also low self-esteem, troubled relationships, poor grades.
- Onset ==**before 12**==, as early as **3**; mild to severe.
- Mimics stress, sleep disorders, anxiety, depression, illness → thorough evaluation. Symptoms must **persist over time, impair daily function, and occur in ≥ 2 settings** (school **and** home).

#### Treatment and nursing role?
- **Medication** (usually stimulants; non-stimulants possible), **behavior therapy**, counseling, educational services. ==Treatment manages, does not cure.==
- **Stimulants** `[ADDED]`: methylphenidate, amphetamines; ↓ appetite/weight loss, insomnia, ↑ HR/BP → give **in the morning**, monitor **height, weight, VS**.
- Help families recognize signs and seek evaluation; address **parental frustration, anger, guilt**; promote school–family–provider communication.

> [!tip] NCLEX tip
> Parent blames self for not being strict: respond with ==the cause is unknown and many parents feel this way== (validate + correct), not discipline advice `[Engage practice item]`.

### Obesity

#### How is pediatric weight classified?
| Class | BMI percentile (age/sex) `[Engage]` |
|---|---|
| Overweight | **85th to < 95th** |
| Obese class I | **≥ 95th** |
| Obese class II | **≥ 120% of 95th** or **≥ 35 kg/m²** (whichever lower) |
| Obese class III | **≥ 140% of 95th** or **≥ 40 kg/m²** |

- BMI = kg / m². **Well-child check ≥ yearly** for height, weight, BMI; ==a big jump in BMI percentile over 1 yr signals risk==.
- Risks: diabetes, cardiac disease, adult obesity, morbidity, premature death. Boys more often obese at **6–11**, girls in adolescence (**12–19**).

#### Prevention teaching?
- Use ==neutral words ("weight," "BMI")==, not "fat" or "obese"; language affects the family's comfort.
- Family activity and healthy eating together; healthy snacks (air-popped popcorn, low-fat dairy, whole-grain cereal); **offer new foods many times**; reward with **non-food** items; adequate **sleep** (sleep loss → hormonal ↑ appetite).

### Food insecurity and dental care

#### Food insecurity?
- Limited or uncertain access to enough nutritious food `[ADDED]`. **10.3%** of 6–11 yr olds; ↑ in Black and Hispanic households and children with disabilities `[Engage]`.
- → poorer health, acute and chronic illness. Teach healthy eating **within the budget**; connect to **nutrition assistance**, school meal programs, food banks.

> [!example] Worked example `[Engage case]`
> 7-yr-old in the nurse's office weekly with morning stomachache and headache, ↓ schoolwork and attention; ate **nothing** today and usually doesn't; father's work hours cut → **food insecurity**. Nurse gives a snack and water, meets with parent. Evaluate: food diary, height, weight, growth chart, resolution of pain.

#### Dental care teaching?
- Cavities affect **> 40%** of ages 2–19; ==**sugar** is a major risk factor==.
- Brush **2×/day with fluoride toothpaste** from first tooth; **supervise until 10**. **Fluoridated water** (most bottled water lacks fluoride); fluoride varnish/applications at well visits.
- **Sealants** prevent cavities but are underused (< half of 6–11 yr olds); low-income children **2×** as likely to have untreated cavities → refer to **school/mobile dental programs**.

### Nocturnal enuresis

#### Primary vs. secondary?
| | Primary | Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | **Never** had nighttime bladder control | Dry **≥ 6 mo** `[Engage]`, then wets again |
| Causes | Delayed bladder/kidney/brain development (more urine at night, brain doesn't wake) | ==UTI==, neurologic problems, **stress**, other illness |
| Action | Usually outgrown | ==**Evaluate by provider**== in older children |

- Prevalence: up to **10% at 7 yr** (deck); **15–20% at 5 yr**, males **2–3×** `[Engage]`.
- Risk factors (both types): genetics, stress, **sleep apnea**, **constipation**, bladder/kidney disease, **spinal cord**/neurologic disorders; rarely diabetes or ADHD.
- Not serious, but distressing. Teach: limit fluids and food before bed, void regularly day and night, wake to void if needed.

#### Enuresis meds?
| Drug | Class / action | Adverse effects | Teaching `[Engage]` |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Imipramine** | **TCA**; blocks NE/serotonin reuptake | Sedation, dizziness, confusion, seizures; anticholinergic (dry mouth, constipation, urinary retention, blurred vision); ==**arrhythmia**==, orthostatic hypotension; rare marrow suppression. Antidepressant **boxed warning: suicidality** in youth `[ADDED — verify]` | **Cardiac evaluation before starting**; PO **60 min before bed**; monthly follow-up; dose adjusted after **7 days**; ==hold **2 wk every 3 mo**== to ↓ tolerance. Avoid with **MAOIs**, linezolid, IV methylene blue |
| **Desmopressin** | Synthetic vasopressin (ADH); ↓ urine volume, ↑ urine osmolality | ==**Hyponatremia**==, HTN, headache, nausea, abdominal pain, nasal symptoms | PO **60 min before bed**; restrict fluids (Engage: hold **60 min before** dose; standard: **1 hr before until 8 hr after** `[ADDED — verify vs course materials]`); **taper** to stop. Contraindicated in **renal impairment** |

> [!danger] Report immediately
> On desmopressin: headache, nausea/vomiting, confusion, or seizure = possible **water intoxication / hyponatremia** → hold and notify `[ADDED]`.

- Bedwetting often **returns when meds stop** unless the child has outgrown it.

### Encopresis

#### What is encopresis and why does it happen?
- Repeated, usually involuntary passing of stool into clothing after toilet training (develops **after age 4**) `[Engage]`.
- Usually ==**chronic constipation**==: impacted stool → **liquid stool leaks around it** → stained underwear; colon distends, control lost. Less often emotional (school start, death, divorce).
- Risk `[Engage]`: constipating meds (cough suppressants), ADHD, autism, anxiety, depression, withholding, **low fiber, excess dairy, low water**. More common in boys.

#### Findings and management?
- Leakage ==mistaken for diarrhea==; hard/large stools that clog the toilet; avoiding BMs, ↓ appetite, abdominal pain, urinary incontinence.
- **Prevent constipation:** ==high fiber, plenty of water, regular toileting routine==; early treatment to rule out physical cause.
- Assess toileting at every well visit; **patience + positive reinforcement**, no shaming; refer for social/emotional impact.

### PTSD and other behavioral concerns

#### When is it PTSD?
- After severe or unresolved stress/trauma, symptoms ==**> 1 month**== that interfere with relationships and activities.
- Reliving the event, **nightmares**, sleep disturbance, emotional numbing/lack of positive emotion, **avoidance** of reminders; irritability, withdrawal, startle, outbursts, denial the event happened `[Engage]`.
- ==Can be mistaken for **ADHD**== → thorough assessment.
- Refer to trauma-skilled mental health professionals; safe, supportive environment; psychotherapy, behavior therapy, ± meds. Prevent trauma, maltreatment, violence.
- Bereavement example: concern = symptoms **> 1 month**, withdrawal, reluctance to join activities; **not** crying relieved by comfort or talking about the deceased parent `[Engage practice item]`.

#### Other behavioral concerns?
- Anxiety, depression, conduct problems; hard to spot during rapid development. ==Parents know their child best==: invite their concerns, refer early. Early intervention improves outcomes.

## VII. Clinical judgment (nursing process)

#### What does each step look like for a school-age child?
| Step | Key actions |
|---|---|
| Recognize cues | Height, weight, **BMI**; motor, psychosocial, cognitive, language skills; self-esteem, body image, sexuality, discipline, stress, **bullying**; nutrition, sleep, exposures, **immunizations**; environment safety; LD, ADHD, obesity, food insecurity, dental, bowel/bladder, PTSD |
| Analyze cues | Compare to **growth chart and milestones**; give child and parent room to fully express concerns |
| Prioritize hypotheses | Consult provider + team (**speech, PT, OT, nutrition**); behavioral concerns need space, time, information, cultural/family context |
| Generate solutions | Collaborative plan with child, parents, provider; **destigmatize mental health**; well-checks and vaccines per **AAP**; prevent injury from **firearms, vehicles, falls** |
| Take action | Prevention + **early intervention**; safety education at every visit |
| Evaluate | Growth charts, milestone tools; **AAP periodicity schedule** (recommended screenings/visits by age `[ADDED]`); injury counts |

- School-age vaccines named but not listed. Typical U.S. schedule `[ADDED — verify vs course materials]`: **4–6 yr** DTaP, IPV, MMR, varicella boosters; **11–12 yr** Tdap, HPV, MenACWY; flu yearly.
- Health-promotion assessment order in the Engage case: sleep, tobacco exposure, activity, diet (ranking not given).

## Self-quiz

> [!question]- 1. Which statement about school-age physical development is correct: height doubles, adult weight by 12, puberty complete by 12, or teeth lost from age 6?
> Deciduous teeth are lost and permanent teeth erupt beginning at ~6.

> [!question]- 2. A parent asks what to expect at 10. Which is accurate?
> The child can follow a series of (about 5) commands. Peer acceptance matters a lot; dating interest is not expected yet.

> [!question]- 3. A 6-yr-old can't use scissors or hold a pencil well at a well visit. Why prioritize referral?
> Variations in milestones should be evaluated for early intervention; earlier services = better outcomes.

> [!question]- 4. A 7-yr-old is being bullied. Which findings need further evaluation?
> Poor school attendance, poor appetite, difficulty sleeping (psychosomatic/depressive signs).

> [!question]- 5. When must bullying be reported to law enforcement?
> Cyber or sexual bullying, a weapon, serious bodily harm, or hate-motivated threats.

> [!question]- 6. Best question to assess gender identity in a school-age child?
> "Can you tell me how you feel on the inside?"

> [!question]- 7. Which parent statement shows understanding of discipline teaching?
> "Children who receive corporal punishment are at increased risk of substance use disorders."

> [!question]- 8. How long should a time-out last for a 7-yr-old?
> About 7 minutes (1 minute per year of age).

> [!question]- 9. An 11-yr-old has slept on the parents' floor for 3 months from fear of the dark. Action?
> Refer to a behavioral therapist: a persistent fear disrupting function suggests a phobia.

> [!question]- 10. Correct bus-safety teaching?
> Wait ≥ 10 ft from the curb, board on the driver's signal, never walk behind the bus, make eye contact before crossing in front.

> [!question]- 11. Which firearm statement is correct?
> Guns and ammunition should be stored separately (unloaded, locked, no child access to keys).

> [!question]- 12. Activity and sleep recommendations for ages 6–12?
> ≥ 60 min activity daily (aerobic, muscle, bone); 9–12 hr sleep.

> [!question]- 13. A 6-yr-old wakes twice weekly. Which further assessments matter?
> Food intake before bed, bedroom noise, electronics before bed, daytime activity.

> [!question]- 14. Which child needs LD assessment: 6-yr-old reading picture books, 8-yr-old who can't print their name, 10-yr-old who dislikes math, 12-yr-old who likes sports?
> The 8-yr-old who struggles to print their name.

> [!question]- 15. Three criteria for diagnosing ADHD symptoms?
> Persist over a long period, interfere with daily function, present in multiple settings (school and home); onset before 12.

> [!question]- 16. Which BMI finding suggests obesity? What words should the nurse use?
> ≥ 95th percentile. Use neutral terms like "weight" or "BMI."

> [!question]- 17. Primary vs. secondary enuresis, and which needs evaluation?
> Primary = never dry at night. Secondary = dry ≥ 6 months then relapse; needs provider evaluation (UTI, neuro, stress).

> [!question]- 18. Child on desmopressin has a headache, vomiting, and confusion. Concern?
> Hyponatremia (water intoxication): hold and notify.

> [!question]- 19. What must happen before starting imipramine, and when is it given?
> Cardiac evaluation (arrhythmia risk); PO 60 min before bed; held 2 weeks every 3 months.

> [!question]- 20. Parent reports "diarrhea" on a toilet-trained 6-yr-old's underwear and hard stools that clog the toilet. Likely problem and teaching?
> Encopresis from chronic constipation (overflow leakage). High fiber, plenty of water, toileting routine, positive reinforcement.

> [!question]- 21. Which manifestations after a parent's death concern the nurse?
> Symptoms lasting > 1 month, withdrawal from relationships, reluctance to participate in activities.

> [!question]- 22. Correct dental teaching for a 6-yr-old?
> Brush 2×/day with fluoride toothpaste, supervised until 10; fluoridated water; sealants when appropriate.
