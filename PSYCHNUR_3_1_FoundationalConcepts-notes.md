# Foundational Concepts — PSYCHNUR 3.1

## Cram sheet

**Dates to anchor the shift to community care**

| Year | Event | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **1946** | National Mental Health Act (Truman) → NIMH | Post-WWII need put MH in the spotlight |
| **1950s** | **Chlorpromazine (Thorazine)** | First effective drug for psychosis → made deinstitutionalization possible |
| **1963** | Community Mental Health Centers Act (Kennedy) | Federal support for CMHCs; **custody → community care** |
| **1996** | HIPAA | Psychotherapy notes get extra protection |
| **2008** | Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act | MH/SUD coverage can't be more restrictive than medical |

**Definitions:** mental health = functioning + adaptation, not "no diagnosis" · mental illness = changes in emotion/thinking/behavior **+ distress and/or impaired functioning** · disorder = cluster meeting **DSM-5-TR** criteria · goal of disorder care = **recovery**, not symptom elimination

**Models:** continuum = moving spectrum (adaptive ↔ maladaptive) · wellness–illness = **well-being / neutrality / disability** · diathesis–stress = **vulnerability + stress → risk**

**Stigma:** public (society) · self (internalized shame) · institutional (policies/systems)

**Bias:** implicit = outside awareness · explicit = conscious, intentional

**Ethics:** autonomy (self-govern) · beneficence (do good) · nonmaleficence (do no harm) · justice (fair/equitable)

**Priority rules**
- Safety vs. autonomy → **least restrictive** intervention that keeps the client safe; document
- Involuntary **hold ≠ consent to treatment** — forced meds need a separate court order
- Credible threat to a **specific, identifiable person** → duty to warn/protect
- Suspected abuse of child/older adult/disabled → **mandatory report** (legal threshold, not personal certainty)
- No release of info to family/caller **without signed client consent**

**DSM-5-TR:** diagnoses (criteria + duration + severity) · **not a treatment manual** · aligned with ICD

**Cultural care trio:** patient-centered + cultural competence + cultural humility → "**Ask rather than assume**"

## I. From asylums to community care; mental health today

> [!quote] Anchor
> The major shift: long-term custody → community-based treatment. Mental health nursing now extends beyond the psychiatric unit.

#### What drove deinstitutionalization?
- Post-WWII psychiatric need → national attention (**1946** National Mental Health Act created NIMH `[Engage]`).
- **Chlorpromazine (Thorazine)**, **1950s** — first-generation (typical) antipsychotic `[ADDED]`; controlled psychosis/aggression enough for clients to live outside institutions.
- **1963** Community Mental Health Centers Act → federal money for community centers.
- Historically illness was explained as spiritual, moral, or supernatural; practices now recognized as inhumane.

#### Why a population-health lens?
- **Healthy People 2030**: promotion, screening, treatment, safety; goal = quality of life. Focus areas include more **primary-care screening** and fewer **adolescent safety-related outcomes** (suicide/self-harm) `[Engage]`.
- Many psychiatric symptoms **first appear in primary care** → every nurse screens.
- Access barriers `[Engage]`: **~1 in 5** adults have mental illness, **< half** treated; **55%** of U.S. counties have no psychiatrist; lack of insurance and transportation.

## II. Mental health, mental illness, disorders

#### Health vs. illness vs. disorder?

| Term | Definition | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| **Mental health** | Foundation for emotion, thinking, communication, learning, resilience, self-esteem (APA) | **Function**: relationships, work/school, self-care, ADLs |
| **Mental illness** | Changes in emotion, thinking, or behavior (or combination) | Associated with **distress and/or impaired functioning** |
| **Mental health disorder** | Recognizable cluster of manifestations meeting defined criteria | **DSM-5-TR** criteria (incl. duration) |

- Stress, grief, and intense emotion can occur **without** mental illness. Unusual thoughts alone ≠ disorder.
- Think adaptation and functioning, not perfection.
- Don't assume who "looks like" they have a psychiatric diagnosis.
- ==Recovery — not necessarily complete symptom elimination — is the larger goal.== Not all disorders are preventable.

#### Major DSM categories (lecture list)?
- Mood · anxiety · psychotic · trauma-related · substance-related · neurocognitive · personality. (Textbook lists ~20 DSM-5-TR classes; all covered in later units.)

## III. Continuum, models, contributors

#### Where is the client on the continuum?
- Spectrum: **adaptive** (wellness, healthy coping) ↔ **maladaptive** (illness, unhealthy coping or inability to cope). Not two fixed boxes; position moves over time.
- Placement influenced by **behaviors, emotions, cognition, physical adaptation** (response to stress).
- Nursing question: **where is the client now, and which direction are they moving?**

> [!info] Five-level continuum `[Engage]`
> In crisis → struggling → surviving → thriving → excelling. Example mapping `[ADDED — verify]`: hopelessness = in crisis · absenteeism = struggling · socially withdrawn = surviving · fostering social relationships = thriving · motivated toward goals = excelling.

#### Wellness–illness model: three zones?

| Zone | What you see |
|---|---|
| **Well-being** | Adaptive coping, functioning, stressors managed, capacity for growth |
| **Neutrality** | No clear illness or wellness manifestations; awareness/learning may be reduced, but still does ADLs and interacts |
| **Disability** | Clear manifestations of illness + meaningful difficulty functioning |

- Nursing goal: identify the zone, support movement toward wellness. Medical paradigm aims poor health → **neutral**; wellness paradigm aims → **optimal** health `[Engage]`.

#### Diathesis–stress: what are the two parts?
- **Diathesis** = biological/genetic vulnerability → found in **family history** (e.g., parent with MDD).
- **Stress** = psychological or physiological trigger → found in **recent life changes**.
  - Physiological: infection → hallucinations. Psychological: job change → worry + tachycardia `[Engage]`.
- ==Vulnerability + stress → increased risk.== Assess both sides. Some disorders onset from stress alone `[Engage]`.

#### Contributors to mental illness?
- Trauma/abuse · medical conditions (often chronic: thyroid, cancer, diabetes `[Engage]`) · social environment/relationships · biological/genetic · substance use. **Usually multifactorial.**

## IV. Social determinants of mental health (SDOMH)

> [!quote] Anchor
> Many drivers of mental health cannot be fixed with medication alone. Ask: what is happening in this client's life?

#### Which conditions raise risk?
- Lack of social support · housing inaccessibility · ACEs/trauma · un/underemployment · food insecurity · poor access to care · educational and income inequity · unsafe neighborhood `[Engage]`.
- Discrimination, violence, chronic insecurity ↑ distress. Social support and family relationships are **protective**.

#### WHO levels — examples at each?

| Level | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Life course** | Timing matters: adversity during rapid neuro development hits hardest; ACEs |
| **Household** | Income, food/water, parental health, support; unemployment → ↑ depression, anxiety, suicide attempts |
| **Community** | Neighborhood safety, crime/violence (↑ depression, PTSD), connection |
| **Local services** | Schools, early education, health/social services, youth programs |
| **Country/policy** | Housing, health care, education, poverty reduction, inequality |

#### What do ACEs do, and what protects?
- **ACEs** = traumatic events in childhood (abuse, neglect, household dysfunction).
- Long-term: depression, anxiety, behavioral problems, substance use, **suicide risk**; poorer school/work/relationships.
- Protective: support systems, screening, access to MH resources. **Assess both risk and resilience.**

## V. Stigma and bias

#### Three types of stigma — whose barrier is it?

| Type | Source of barrier | Example `[Engage]` |
|---|---|---|
| **Public** | Society's negative attitudes/discrimination | People with schizophrenia seen as dangerous |
| **Self** | Client internalizes shame; sees illness as character flaw | Depressed client too embarrassed to tell family |
| **Institutional** | Policies/organizations limit opportunity (intentional or not) | Insurance covers medical but not psychiatric admissions |

- Stigma delays help-seeking (avg **11-year** delay from symptom onset; only **~37%** of males treated annually `[Engage]`) and changes how clients are treated. Media reinforces stereotypes.
- Nursing response: challenge assumptions, protect dignity.

#### Implicit vs. explicit bias?

| | Implicit | Explicit |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Outside conscious awareness | Consciously held, intentionally expressed |
| Example `[Engage]` | Nurse avoids entering room of client with schizophrenia | Telling a colleague a nurse is incompetent because of her depression |

- Bias forms: **stereotype** ("all depressed people want to hurt themselves"), **prejudice** (dismissing concerns as attention-seeking), **discrimination** (lower coverage for psych stays) `[Engage]`.

> [!danger] Diagnostic overshadowing
> A psychiatric diagnosis **does not make physical symptoms less real**. Stereotypes cause both overreaction and **dangerous dismissal** of legitimate complaints. Self-awareness is a patient-safety skill.

#### Professional values to know?
- Grounded in **ANA Scope and Standards** + **Code of Ethics**.
- ANA core values: respect, service, relationships, bearing witness, self-determination, pursuit of health.
- PMH values: **empowerment, inclusivity, integrity, collegiality, innovation, transparency, stewardship**.
- Trust matters: clients must feel safe enough to be honest.

## VI. DSM-5-TR and nursing assessment

#### What does the DSM-5-TR do — and not do?
- Principal U.S. diagnostic reference; defines criteria (required manifestations **and duration**), severity, specific features.
- Harmonized with WHO **ICD** coding → shared diagnostic language.
- ==**Not a treatment manual**== — no medications, no care plans.

#### Who does what in diagnosis?
- Nurse gathers: **psychosocial history** (context, functioning), **medical history** (physiologic contributors, comorbidities), **family history** (patterns, vulnerability).
- **Provider** maps findings to diagnostic criteria. **Nursing** care targets responses, needs, safety, goals.

> [!tip] PN scope `[Engage]`
> Report any **change in manifestations** and any data on the client's **cultural view of mental illness** to the supervising RN.

## VII. Legal and ethical issues

#### Four ethical principles?

| Principle | Meaning | Psych example |
|---|---|---|
| **Autonomy** | Right to self-govern decisions | Client refuses a medication |
| **Beneficence** | Promote welfare / do good | Limiting choices to keep client safe |
| **Nonmaleficence** | Avoid or minimize harm; weigh risk vs. benefit | Choosing least restrictive option |
| **Justice** | Fair, equitable care | Low staffing with high acuity; uninsured client needing resources |

#### Autonomy vs. safety — how to resolve?
- Safety concerns pressure the nurse to restrict choices (beneficence/nonmaleficence vs. autonomy).
- Use the **least restrictive intervention** that reasonably maintains safety; clinical judgment + careful documentation.
- Advocacy = protect the client while preserving as much autonomy as safely possible. **State law** governs involuntary treatment.

#### Involuntary treatment and refusal?
- Clients retain rights to **humane treatment, confidentiality, informed consent, refusal**.
- Restraint, seclusion, and treatment against wishes need strict safeguards; violating rights → state/federal discipline `[Engage]`.
- Refusal: assess **reasoning, capacity, risks, legal authority**; nurse **clarifies and documents the client's rationale** `[Engage]`.

> [!warning] Priority: hold ≠ treatment consent
> Involuntary **admission** does not authorize involuntary **medication**. Treating over refusal needs a **specific court order** (provider petitions the court; emergent vs. nonemergent) `[Engage]`.

#### When does confidentiality yield?

| Trigger | Duty | Nurse action |
|---|---|---|
| Credible threat toward a **specific, identifiable** person | **Duty to warn/protect** | Report per law/policy so the person can be warned; document |
| Suspected abuse/neglect of **child, older adult, disabled** person (physical, sexual, emotional, financial, neglect) | **Mandatory reporting** | Follow the required pathway; failure to report → criminal charges, malpractice liability `[Engage]` |

- Requirements vary by jurisdiction — know your state and facility.

#### HIPAA essentials?
- National privacy standard for PHI; no disclosure without client consent.
- **Psychotherapy notes** get additional protection (generally only the treating MH team).
- Share only with those directly involved in care; family/friends only with **signed consent**. Clear explanations about privacy strengthen trust.

#### What is parity?
- MHPAEA (**2008**), ACA (**2010**), Health Care and Education Reconciliation Act (**2010**): MH/SUD coverage can't be more restrictive — no different **deductibles, treatment limits, or co-pays** than medical `[Engage]`.
- Parity = access + equity; reduces **institutional stigma**. Policy decides whether treatment is realistically available.

## VIII. Culturally sensitive, holistic, recovery-oriented care

#### Competence vs. humility?

| | Cultural competence | Cultural humility |
|---|---|---|
| What | Building **knowledge and skills** to work across differences | Ongoing **self-reflection** on assumptions, bias, and **power imbalance** |
| Nurse action | Learn about the client's culture | Examine own culture; limit power imbalance; seek supervisor support if bias/barrier found `[Engage]` |

- Patient-centered care = respect for the client's needs, preferences, values (not the nurse's). Combine all three → empowers client, promotes autonomy.
- **Ask rather than assume.** Incorporate culturally meaningful practices when safe.

#### Holistic assessment covers what?
- Five dimensions: **physical, emotional, social, spiritual, intellectual**.
- Ask about health beliefs, traditional practices/remedies (acupuncture, massage, herbs `[Engage]`), diet, meaningful routines.
- Identify barriers you can't solve alone → use resources.

#### What does recovery-oriented care ask?
- Traditional: symptom control, crisis containment. Recovery: what helps the person build a **meaningful, self-directed life**.
- Recovery can occur **even when some symptoms remain**.
- Principles: **person-centered, strengths-based, hopeful, collaborative**. Opening question: ==**"What matters to you?"**==

#### Therapeutic vs. non-therapeutic responses
Example phrasing built from lecture principles; row 1 is the textbook's answer `[ADDED]`.

| Situation | Therapeutic | Non-therapeutic | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Caregiver calls for an update on an adult client | "I'm unable to give information about a client's condition without their consent." `[Engage]` | "She's doing well, want to talk to her?" / "That client isn't here." | First discloses PHI; second is dishonest. No consent = no info |
| Client fears employer will find out | "Your information is shared only with the people directly involved in your care." | "Don't worry about that." | Accurate privacy info builds trust; false reassurance doesn't |
| Involuntary client refuses a med | "Help me understand your concerns about this medication." (then document rationale) | "You're on a hold, so you have to take it." | Hold ≠ consent to treatment; clarify and document refusal |
| "I should be able to snap out of this depression." | "Depression is a health condition, not a weakness. What's made it hard to talk about?" | "Just try to stay positive." | Addresses self-stigma instead of reinforcing it |
| Client mentions a traditional remedy | "Tell me how you use it and what it means to you." | "That won't help; stick to your meds." | Ask, don't assume; incorporate safely |

## Self-quiz

> [!question]- 1. Mental illness vs. a strong emotional reaction: what two features make it clinically significant?
> Distress and/or impaired functioning (social, work, family).

> [!question]- 2. A client's mother had major depressive disorder; the client just lost a job and now has symptoms. Name the model and label each part.
> Diathesis–stress: family history = diathesis (vulnerability); job loss = stress (precipitant).

> [!question]- 3. Client does ADLs and interacts but shows little growth or awareness; no illness manifestations. Which zone?
> Neutrality (wellness–illness model).

> [!question]- 4. A client with depression doesn't tell family because she feels she "should be able to control it." Which stigma?
> Self-stigma.

> [!question]- 5. An insurer covers medical but not psychiatric hospitalizations. Which stigma, and which law addresses it?
> Institutional stigma; Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act (2008).

> [!question]- 6. A nurse unconsciously avoids the room of a client with schizophrenia. Implicit or explicit bias?
> Implicit — outside conscious awareness.

> [!question]- 7. A client with a psychiatric diagnosis reports new chest pain. What bias risk and what action?
> Dismissal of a legitimate concern (diagnostic overshadowing); assess and treat the physical complaint — psychiatric diagnosis doesn't make it less real.

> [!question]- 8. Which two things does the DSM-5-TR provide, and what does it NOT provide?
> Diagnostic criteria (incl. duration/severity) and a standardized language; it does not describe treatment.

> [!question]- 9. Nurse restricts an at-risk client's access to belongings. Which principles are being served, and which is limited?
> Beneficence and nonmaleficence served; autonomy limited → use least restrictive option and document.

> [!question]- 10. An involuntarily admitted client refuses an antipsychotic. Can the nurse give it anyway?
> Not on the hold alone — treatment over refusal needs a separate court order (emergent or nonemergent, per state law). Clarify and document the reason for refusal.

> [!question]- 11. A client says he'll kill his neighbor "Tom" and confirms intent. What duty applies?
> Duty to warn/protect — credible threat to a specific, identifiable person; report per law/policy and document.

> [!question]- 12. A caller says she is the client's caregiver and asks how the client is doing. Best response?
> "I'm unable to give information on a client's condition without their consent."

> [!question]- 13. Which HIPAA records get extra protection?
> Psychotherapy notes.

> [!question]- 14. Staffing is short while unit acuity is high. Which principle is strained?
> Justice.

> [!question]- 15. Cultural competence vs. cultural humility: one-line difference?
> Competence = knowledge/skills about other cultures; humility = ongoing self-reflection on own assumptions and the nurse–client power imbalance.

> [!question]- 16. What is the goal of care for mental health disorders, and can it occur with ongoing symptoms?
> Recovery — a meaningful, self-directed life; yes, it can occur even when some symptoms remain.

> [!question]- 17. Which drug started the move toward community care, and in what decade?
> Chlorpromazine (Thorazine), 1950s.

> [!question]- 18. An LPN learns the client believes her illness is a spiritual punishment. What does the LPN do?
> Report the client's cultural view of illness to the supervising RN; it shapes the plan of care.
