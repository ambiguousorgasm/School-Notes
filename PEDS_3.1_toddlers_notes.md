## I. Biological development

> [!quote] Anchor
> Growth slows after infancy, but cognitive, language, social, and emotional development accelerate. Build expected findings into every encounter (well-child, urgent care, ED, inpatient).

### Physical development and assessment

#### How fast do toddlers grow?
- Gain **4–6 lb (1.8–2.7 kg)** and **2–3 in (5–7 cm)** per year (deck also summarizes this as "up to **5 lb** and up to **3 in**").
- By the end of toddlerhood the child is ~**half** of anticipated adult height. Body proportions shift from infant-like to child-like.
- Plot height and weight at **every** encounter and compare with prior plots.
- Report an unexpected trend: a jump or drop in percentiles, or a **plateau** since the last visit. It may not be pathology, but it needs follow-up.

#### How do you get an accurate toddler assessment?
- Toddlers fear new situations and still have **separation anxiety**, so use family-centered care. **Comfort hold:** the parent holds the child, holds their hand, or comforts them during the exam.
- Let the child handle safe equipment (stethoscope) and explain each step at their level. A crying, moving child gives inaccurate vitals. `[Engage: Vital Signs]`

#### Expected toddler vital signs?
| Vital sign | Expected range |
|---|---|
| BP | Males **85–91/37–46** mm Hg<br>Females **86–89/40–49** mm Hg |
| Heart rate | **80–140/min** |
| Respiratory rate | **25–30/min** |
| Temperature (temporal) | **36–38 °C (96.8–100.4 °F)** |
| SpO2 | **95–100%** |

Ranges vary by source, age, and activity. `[Engage: Vital Signs]`

#### BP technique?
- Cuff width covers ~**80%** of the extremity; a wrong-size cuff gives a false reading.
- Usual site is the right upper arm `[Engage]`; a lower extremity is acceptable if needed.
- Routine BP starts at **age 3** and continues annually, earlier if indicated.

#### Which temperature route, and when not rectal?
| Route | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Axillary** | Most common and safe. Center of the axilla, arm down against the body. **Not a core temp.** |
| **Tympanic** | Head turned away; pull pinna **down and back**; insert at a slightly anterior angle, rotate to seal `[Engage]` |
| **Temporal** | Swipe across forehead per manufacturer |
| **Rectal** | **Most dangerous:** mucosal injury. Contraindicated with diarrhea, coagulation disorders; also hemorrhoids, immunocompromise, rectal surgery `[Engage]` |

> [!warning] Priority
> If an axillary reading is high or low and a core temperature is needed, recheck by **tympanic or temporal** route. (The module contradicts itself on this point; see Source notes.)

#### When to treat a fever?
- Fever = **≥ 100.4 °F (38.0 °C)**. Causes: infection, recent immunization.
- Case-by-case. First rest and ↑ oral fluids. Give an antipyretic if the child is uncomfortable, the temperature doesn't fall with rest and fluids, or it is **> 102.0 °F (38.8 °C)**. `[Engage]`
- Get a current **weight** first, since facility dosing is weight-based. At home, dose by weight or age per label.
- External cooling: tepid water **85–90 °F (29–32 °C)**. ==Never cold water==: shivering raises temperature.

#### Signs of respiratory distress?
- By toddlerhood the breathing pattern is regular and rhythmic.
- Distress: abnormal pattern, ↑ work of breathing, tachypnea, retractions, stridor, nasal flaring, accessory muscle use. **Cyanosis is late.** Small airways occlude easily, so take distress seriously and get immediate evaluation. `[Engage]`
- Retractions: **intercostal** (between ribs), **subcostal** (below rib cage), **suprasternal** (above sternum), **substernal** (below sternum), **supraclavicular** (above clavicle).

#### ABC assessment in distress?
| | Look for / do |
|---|---|
| **Airway** | Appearance; agitation → lethargy → respiratory fatigue. Clear, maintainable, or not? Let the child take a position of comfort (**tripod**, sniffing). Suction if possible. |
| **Breathing** | Stridor, accessory muscles, sniffing position; quality/quantity of respirations; SpO2 |
| **Circulation** | Pallor, cyanosis; pulses, perfusion; HR and rhythm |

> [!danger] Report immediately
> **Inspiratory stridor** = upper airway obstruction and takes priority over tachypnea (e.g., RR **45**), diminished bases, or a dry cough. The ABC check should take **< 1 min**; history, prior treatments, full vitals, and a focused respiratory exam come once the child is stable. `[Engage]`

### Developmental milestones

#### What counts as a missed milestone?
- Milestones are **guidelines**. Missing one doesn't prove a delay, but **document it and notify the provider**.
- Risk factors for developmental concerns: **prematurity**, **low birth weight**, **lead exposure**.
- **Corrected (adjusted) age** for preterm infants: subtract the weeks early from current age (born **6 wk** early → subtract **6 wk**). Usually caught up and no longer needed by **age 2**.
- Developmental and behavioral screening for all U.S. children at **9, 18, and 30 months**; ASD screening at **18 and 24 months**.

#### Toddler milestones by age?
| Age | Gross / fine motor | Cognitive / language / social |
|---|---|---|
| **15 mo** | Takes a few independent steps; stacks **2** small objects; claps; finger-feeds | |
| **18 mo** | Walks alone; climbs on/off furniture; scribbles; open cup (spills), tries utensils | Says **5+** words (deck) / **3+** words (module); copies others |
| **24 mo** | Runs; kicks ball; stairs ± help; holds object in one hand while using the other; gestures (wave, blow kiss, nod, point) | **2-word** sentences ("Want milk"); names **≥ 2** body parts; reads facial cues |
| **30 mo** | Jumps with **both feet**; turns **1** page at a time; twists knobs/lids; takes some clothes off | Vocabulary ~**50** words `[Engage]` |
| **36 mo** | Rides tricycle; puts some clothes on; strings **2+** items | **3-word** sentences ("Me want truck"); understood by others; plays with other children |

- Motor milestones are skills **> 75%** of children reach by that age `[Engage]`. Gross motor delay → PT, OT, or early intervention. Fine motor delay → OT or early intervention.

#### Cognitive and language milestones?
- Up to **80%** of the brain is developed by **age 3**. Follows **two-step** directions, knows body parts, avoids hot objects, imagination emerging.
- Language: two-syllable words by **18 mo** ("mama," "bye-bye"); ==words per sentence ≈ age in years==; several hundred words by **3** `[Engage]`.

### Psychosocial development

#### Erikson stage and parenting strategy?
- **Autonomy vs. shame and doubt.** Intense urge for independence → "terrible twos," with **egocentrism** and **tantrums** as defining traits. It is also a stage of inquiry: processing emotions, learning from mistakes, self-exploration, imagination.
- Parents need praise and support. ==Offer choices that can't be answered "no"==: "Red shirt or orange shirt?" rather than "Do you want to wear this shirt?"

#### Types of toddler play?
| Type | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Solitary** | Plays alone, no interaction with adults or children |
| **Parallel** | Plays beside peers with similar toys; no direct interaction |
| **Peer** (social/cooperative) | Begins **2–3 yr**; includes others; first rudimentary friendships |

- Play reduces stress and builds social-emotional, language, cognitive, and self-regulation skills `[Engage]`.
- Play is also a window: repeated violent or sexual play may signal abuse, and play that doesn't evolve warrants a developmental assessment.
- Hospitalized: involve a **child life specialist** (medical play). `[Engage]`

### Developmental delays

#### Nurse's role when a delay is found?
- Delay = expected milestone not reached. ==Early intervention is key== to outcome: PT, speech, OT, specialist referral, early intervention programs.
- Communicate with empathy and compassion. Expect an emotional response, give the parent time to process and ask questions, and provide follow-up contact plus reputable resources (e.g., AAP). Keep the relationship positive.

## II. Toddler behavioral challenges

> [!quote] Anchor
> Autonomy drives most toddler "misbehavior." Give **anticipatory guidance** before the behaviors start, so parents can decide how they'll respond.

#### Negativism vs. temper tantrum?
- **Negativism:** refusing options or doing the opposite of what's asked. Expected; mostly resolves by preschool.
- **Temper tantrum:** a form of negativism. Brief behavior **out of proportion** to the trigger: crying, screaming, hitting, throwing, throwing self down, short **breath-holding** or **head-banging**. Causes: desire for autonomy, or inability to express emotions. They decrease as the child nears **3**.
- Toddlers can't self-regulate yet (brain connections not formed). Tantrums are **normal and unavoidable**, not deliberate misbehavior. `[Engage]`

#### What to teach about handling tantrums?
- ==The adult stays calm==, whatever method the family picks. Options: walk away if the child is safe, remove from an overstimulating setting, speak calmly at eye level. `[Engage]`
- Time-out **≤ 1 min per year of age** (2-year-old → **≤ 2 min**). `[Engage]`
- The nurse models calm behavior and gives the child control (e.g., choosing where to sit). `[Engage: Case Study]`

> [!danger] Report immediately
> - Excessive tantrums → provider: extreme aggression, **> 5/day**, or lasting **> 15 min** `[Engage]`. Possible developmental delay or medical/psychological condition.
> - A caregiver hitting, slapping, or pulling hair in response to behavior → report immediately to **state authorities**. All U.S. health care providers are **mandated reporters**; **25%** of child abuse victims are **1–3 yr**. `[Engage]`

#### What is regression, and when to worry?
- Returning to an earlier developmental stage after mastering it: baby talk, pacifier or bottle after weaning, repeated incontinence after toilet training, thumb sucking, refusing own bed, sleep disruption `[Engage]`.
- Causes: a new milestone (walking, potty training), hospitalization or illness, routine change, move, new baby, new daycare, death, divorce, **abuse or neglect**.
- Notify the provider if it lasts **> 2–3 weeks** or comes with other manifestations.

#### How do toddlers show stress?
- They take cues from how the parent reacts.
- Signs: regression, ↑ attention-seeking, ↑ crying and tantrums, irritability, appetite and sleep changes.
- Help: consistent daily routine, appropriate choices, calm and safe environment, ↓ stimuli, quality time. Chronic or overwhelming stress → provider; possible counseling. `[Engage]`

#### Sibling rivalry: why, and what helps?
- Egocentric toddlers can't empathize, so conflict is expected. Signs: more tantrums, frustration, attention-seeking, regression. Regression after a new baby is expected and temporary (**< 2–3 wk**). `[Engage]`
- **Never leave the toddler alone with the baby**; supervise play near the baby.
- Make big changes (new room, toilet training, weaning) a few months **before or after** the baby arrives. Involve the toddler in preparing. `[Engage]`
- Praise often, give one-on-one time to each child, keep rules fair and consistent, don't compare siblings.

#### Signs of toilet-training readiness?
- Usually ready **2–4 yr**. Don't attempt **< 18 months–2 years** `[Engage]`.

> [!info] Why
> By **age 2** the spinal cord is fully myelinated, so the child can sense a full bladder or bowel and coordinate sphincter relaxation. `[Engage]`

- Readiness: pulls pants up and down; senses and **communicates** the urge; interest in the toilet; stays dry several waking hours or wakes dry from a nap.

#### Toilet-training problems and tips?
- ==Resistance at any point = not ready.== Stop and retry in a few weeks to months (tips list: **2–3 months**). Avoid starting during stress (new baby, new parent, daycare change). `[Engage]`
- Regression is common (illness, stress). Respond with compassion, not anger. Refusal affects up to **20%** and can lead to UTI, pyelonephritis, and chronic constipation. `[Engage]`
- Tips: be consistent; child helps choose a potty chair with **feet touching the floor**; keep it convenient; books/videos; scheduled attempts; **praise every attempt and never discipline accidents**; easy clothing (no zippers, buttons); all children start **seated** (boys learn standing later). `[Engage]`

## III. Health promotion and disease prevention

### Nutrition

#### Toddler dietary needs?
- **1,000–1,400 kcal/day** in **4–6** smaller meals. Weaning means relying on solids while also wanting control → "picky eater." Stay patient and keep offering healthy foods.
- Variety: protein, fruit, vegetables, grains, dairy. Providers often recommend a daily **children's multivitamin**.
- Supports continued breastfeeding nonjudgmentally; dietitian referral if needed. `[Engage]`

#### Toddler nutrition: do vs. avoid?
| Do | Avoid |
|---|---|
| **Whole** milk, especially **< 2 yr** | Low-fat milk **< 2 yr** |
| Water over juice; milk **≤ 16–24 oz/day** | Sweets and sweetened drinks (juice, soda, sports drinks) |
| Nutrient-dense variety; re-offer new foods many times | Caffeine; added salt |
| Make meals fun; child helps prep when safe | Pressuring the child to eat |
| Grapes **quartered**; hot dogs in finger-length strips, then quartered lengthwise | Popcorn, corn kernels, chewy fruit snacks, tough meat, raw carrots |

`[Engage: Toddler Tips for Nutrition]`

#### How is malnutrition picked up?
- Includes **over**nutrition, **under**nutrition, and food insecurity. Plot and analyze height and weight.
- Under: **< 15th percentile** or a **drop across ≥ 2 major percentile curves**. Over: **> 85th percentile** or a **rise across ≥ 2** `[Engage]`.
- Not every child outside those lines is malnourished; consider genetics. Physical signs: hair loss, dry skin, fatigue. `[Engage]`

#### How is food insecurity screened?
- **> 20%** of U.S. children are food insecure. AAP supports screening **all** families with the two-item **Hunger Vital Sign** (past **12 months**):
  1. We worried our food would run out before we got money to buy more.
  2. The food we bought didn't last and we didn't have money for more.
- ==Positive = "often true" or "sometimes true" to either item== `[Engage]` → refer to **SNAP**, **WIC**, **TANF**, and local food banks or pantries. Stay nonjudgmental. Use MyPlate.gov for education.

### Sleep

#### Sleep needs and routine?
- **11–14 hr per 24 hr**, counting naps. Sleep affects immunity, cognition, learning, behavior, and mental health.
- Common problems: daytime sleepiness, trouble falling asleep, night wakings, snoring or breathing problems, nightmares or night terrors. Parents may not bring these up, so screen routinely (**BEARS**). Concern → provider (possible sleep study, referral). `[Engage]`
- Routine: portable (works at home or on vacation), **dim** while winding down, room **dark** and **cool** (overheating → restless sleep), bedtime book, a safe comfort item.
- Electronics: experts advise none **< 2 yr**; if used, off **≥ 1 hr** before bed. `[Engage]`

> [!example] Mnemonic — BEARS
> **B**edtime issues · **E**xcessive daytime sleepiness · **A**wakenings at night · **R**egularity and duration of sleep · **S**leep-disordered breathing / snoring `[Engage]`

### Dental and activity

#### Dental care teaching?
- Most primary (deciduous) teeth erupt during toddlerhood.
- The **parent brushes** twice daily with toothpaste until the child can do it alone (deck specifies *fluoride-free*; see Source notes). `[Engage]`
- First dental exam at first tooth or by **12 months**, then **biannually** `[Engage]`.
- Juice **≤ 4 oz/day**. Avoid bottles and sippy cups, which pool fluid on the teeth. Replace toothbrush every **3–4 months** or after illness. `[Engage]`
- Thumb sucking and prolonged pacifier use can misalign teeth; most children stop by **2–4 yr**. `[Engage]`

#### Activity recommendation?
- **≥ 180 min/day**, as **active play** (running, chasing, tricycle) rather than formal exercise.
- Benefits: lung, heart, and bone health; memory; sleep; ↓ HTN, diabetes, obesity, anxiety, depression.
- Assess the home and neighborhood for safety; if outdoors is unsafe, suggest rec centers, parks, trails. `[Engage]`

## IV. Safety promotion and injury prevention

> [!quote] Anchor
> Toddlers explore but can't recognize danger. Accidental injury accounts for up to **one-third** of pediatric deaths each year `[Engage]`.

#### Car seat rules for toddlers?
- Motor vehicle crashes are a leading cause of death. Safety seats ↓ fatal injury **54%** `[Engage]`.
- **Middle of the back seat.** Five-point harness snug **at or below the shoulders** (rear-facing); chest clip centered at **armpit** level; no pinchable slack.
- Rear-facing until **≥ 2 yr**, and ideally until the manufacturer's height/weight limit. Long legs are fine (children are flexible). State laws may differ.
- Install with LATCH **or** seat belt, not both unless both manufacturers allow it `[Engage]`. Free installation checks: fire departments, Safe Kids, inspection stations.
- Seats expire ~**6 yr**; replace after **any** crash, even minor `[Engage]`.
- No bulky coats under the harness; put a coat or blanket over the buckled child `[Engage]`.

#### Child-proofing for falls?
- Falls are among the most common toddler injuries (walking, running, stairs). Usually nonfatal but dangerous.
- Baby gates at the **top and bottom** of stairs and at outside doors; doorknob covers on **all** doors; doors and windows locked; **no infant walkers**; carpet is safer than tile or hardwood `[Engage]`.

#### How to prevent drowning?
- Leading cause of injury death at **1–4 yr**; highest risk **12–36 months** `[Engage]`. Bathtubs, pools, buckets, toilets.
- **Layered (multimodal)** protection: constant adult supervision, barriers, life jacket, swim lessons (benefit shown **> 1 yr**).
- Pool: alarm, **self-closing, self-latching** gate, toys out of the pool area; exits locked, doorknob covers, gates; pet doors can't be reachable unnoticed `[Engage]`.
- Bathroom (next most dangerous): doorknob covers, drain plug removed, toilet-lid latches, never unsupervised `[Engage]`.

#### Burn prevention?
- Most burns happen at home (kitchen, bathroom). Water heater **≤ 120 °F**. Keep the child **3 ft** from stoves, heaters, fireplaces, hair appliances; supervise cooking `[Engage]`.
- Test smoke detectors; replace batteries every **6 months** (module: per manufacturer). Family fire-escape plan, reviewed regularly `[Engage]`.

#### Sunburn prevention and treatment?
- All sunburn is preventable. Avoid sun **10 a.m.–4 p.m.**; protect on cloudy days too. SPF **≥ 15**, applied **30 min** before, reapplied per label (~**q2h**). SPF clothing, wide-brim hat `[Engage]`.
- Signs: painful red or darkened, warm skin; severe → fever, chills, blisters, headache. Appears **6–12 hr** after exposure `[Engage]`.
- Treat: cool compresses, aloe, ↑ oral fluids, acetaminophen or ibuprofen.

#### OTC analgesics: tested facts?
| Drug | Class / use | Key adverse effects | Teaching / hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acetaminophen** | Antipyretic, nonopioid analgesic | Hepatotoxicity; SJS/TEN; rash | Max **5 days** unless directed; stop if rash; dose by **weight** with the supplied device. Contraindicated: severe hepatic impairment, active liver disease |
| **Ibuprofen** | NSAID, antipyretic, analgesic | GI upset, prolonged bleeding, renal failure, hyperkalemia | Max **3 days** for fever / **10 days** for pain. **Not if dehydrated** (vomiting, diarrhea, poor intake) → renal injury. Contraindicated: bleeding disorders, kidney disease, HF |

`[Engage: Sunburns]`

#### Poisoning prevention and response?
- ==Never call medicine "candy"== (or "yummy," "tastes like candy"). Lock up meds and cleaners (a high shelf in an **unlocked** cabinet doesn't count). Keep products in original packaging.
- Ingestion: take the item away, have the child spit out or remove what's in the mouth, call **poison control**. **Do not induce vomiting** unless poison control says to. Post the poison control number visibly `[Engage]`.

## V. Health concerns during toddlerhood

#### Lead poisoning: risk factors?
- **No safe level.** Affects growth, development, learning, behavior, and neuro function. About **500,000** U.S. children have levels diagnostic of lead poisoning.
- Sources: lead paint in pre-**1978** housing (chips, dust), soil, water, older plumbing, imported goods (ceramics, toys), parental occupation or hobby (construction, plumbing, shooting ranges) `[Engage]`.
- Toddler risk: hand-to-mouth behavior, **incomplete blood-brain barrier**, **iron deficiency anemia** (↑ GI absorption of lead), older neighborhoods, immigrant or refugee families with imported goods.
- Family support: possible hospitalization, frequent labs, cost, housing change, child fear of blood draws, **parental guilt**. Give emotional support, financial resources, and a referral for lead testing and removal. Detail is in the Cognitive System module.

#### FBA: where does it lodge, and how to prevent it?
- Inhaled object lodges in the airway. Most common sites: **right and left main bronchi** and **trachea**. Common items: peanuts, popcorn, grapes and other round foods, coins, small toy parts `[Engage]`.
- ~**5,000** U.S. child deaths in 2020; ~**80%** of pediatric FBAs occur **< 3 yr** `[Engage]`.
- Prevention: active supervision during meals and play; child **seated** while eating, no distractions; teaching starts at **6 months** `[Engage]`. Quarter grapes, **smash blueberries**, quarter hot-dog strips; avoid popcorn, peanuts, hard candy, gum.
- Parents should take **CPR**. Unresponsive child → alternating back blows and abdominal thrusts or Heimlich, by age. ==Never a blind finger sweep== (pushes the object deeper) `[Engage]`.

> [!danger] Report immediately
> Any suspected FBA (e.g., pointing at nose and blowing hard after eating, even with nothing visible) → **assessed by a provider immediately**. Don't reassure, and don't diagnose over the phone. Treatment ranges from removal to bronchoscopy to surgery. `[Engage]`

#### Iron deficiency anemia: why toddlers?
- Main toddler causes: too few iron-rich foods, or **> 16–24 oz** cow's milk daily (low in iron and it displaces food). Affects up to **15%** of U.S. toddlers.
- Risks: poverty; GI malabsorption (celiac, giardiasis, short bowel, small-bowel resection) `[Engage]`; overweight or obesity `[Engage]`; **lead poisoning** (lead binds Hgb, disrupts heme synthesis, destroys RBCs).
- Screening by risk assessment or blood sample: routinely ~**12 months**, any time **9–24 months** `[Engage]`.
- Prevention: milk **≤ 16–24 oz/day**; **≥ 3** servings of iron-rich foods or **≥ 7 mg/day** (meats, tofu, legumes, fortified cereals, dark leafy greens). Detail is in the Hematological module.

#### ASD: manifestations and screening?
- Lifelong neurodevelopmental disorder (DSM-5-TR) with a spectrum of severity. Possible genetic component. ==Vaccines do not cause ASD==; give evidence-based resources `[Engage]`.
- Usually evident by **18 months** `[Engage]`. Behavioral signs: little eye contact, no response to name, low interest in others, repetitive behaviors, odd hand movements, **lining up objects**, hyperfixation on certain toys, sensory-processing problems, **speech regression**. Emotional signs: violent tantrums, flat response to family, infrequent smiling, difficulty forming attachments.
- Prevalence: **1 in 31** U.S. children, males **4×** `[Engage]` (deck: **1 in 59**, more common in males; older figure).
- Screen ASD at **18 and 24 mo**; general developmental at **18 and 30 mo**. Screening tools **don't diagnose**. A positive screen gets a full provider assessment plus education; a diagnosis gets developmental services and early intervention referrals.

#### Which ASD screening tool when?
| Tool | Age | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **ITC** (Infant-Toddler Checklist) | **6–24 mo** | Parent questionnaire on communication delay; doesn't diagnose ASD |
| **M-CHAT-R/F** | **16–30 mo** | Parent questionnaire (part 1) + provider follow-up (part 2) |
| **POSI** | **16–35 mo** | Parent questionnaire scored by provider |
| **STAT** | **24–36 mo** | Play-based; separates ASD from other disorders in children already flagged |

## VI. Nursing process: the Javier case

> [!example] Worked example — 15-month well-child visit
> **Javier, 15 mo:** wt **10 kg** (**17th**), ht **75 cm** (**10th**), HC **44 cm** (**10th**); T **36.8 °C**, HR **110**, RR **26**, SpO2 **99%**. Crawls, but can't pull to stand or walk; no pincer grasp; no words; doesn't respond to his name.
> - **Recognize:** vitals expected. Motor, fine motor, language, and name-response findings are unexpected for 15 mo (should be taking steps, stacking 2 objects).
> - **Analyze:** consider infection, lead, nutrition, prematurity, normal variation. History: born at **34 wk** (**6 wk** early), **3 wk** NICU for RDS, newly built home (lead unlikely).
> - **Prioritize:** most probable cause = **prematurity** → corrected age.
> - **Act:** teach adjusted age and expected progression; refer to **early intervention**; coach the parent on encouraging development. Don't tell parents the deficits are irreversible. `[Engage: Applying the Nursing Process]`

#### What do the later Javier cases test?
| Scenario | Key call |
|---|---|
| **24 mo**, inpatient for bilateral pneumonia; new thumb sucking, toilet-training setback, won't talk | **Regression** from illness and hospital stress, expected to resolve. Diarrhea/nausea = possible antibiotic effect. Teach-back at eye level, written summary, no jargon. |

## Self-quiz

> [!question]- 1. An 18-month-old's vitals: HR 145, RR 28, BP 90/44 (LLE), axillary 37.7 °C. Which do you report?
> **HR 145.** Above the **80–140** range; the others are within range.

> [!question]- 2. A 2-year-old has inspiratory stridor, RR 45, diminished bases, and a dry cough. Which finding is the priority?
> **Inspiratory stridor**, because it signals upper airway obstruction.

> [!question]- 3. An axillary reading comes back elevated. How do you confirm it?
> Recheck with a core route: **tympanic or temporal**. Axillary isn't core.

> [!question]- 4. When should a toddler with a fever get an antipyretic?
> When the child is uncomfortable, the temperature doesn't respond to rest and fluids, or it is **> 102.0 °F (38.8 °C)**. Get a current weight first.

> [!question]- 5. A child was born 6 weeks early. How do you use corrected age, and until when?
> Subtract **6 weeks** from chronological age to judge milestones. Usually no longer needed by **age 2**.

> [!question]- 6. At what ages are developmental screening and ASD screening done?
> Developmental at **9, 18, and 30 mo**. ASD at **18 and 24 mo**.

> [!question]- 7. A parent asks how to get a toddler dressed without a fight. What do you suggest?
> Offer a choice without "no" as an option: "Red shirt or orange shirt?" This supports **autonomy** (Erikson: autonomy vs. shame and doubt).

> [!question]- 8. Two 2-year-olds sit side by side playing with blocks without interacting. What type of play is this?
> **Parallel play.** Peer (cooperative) play begins around **2–3 yr**.

> [!question]- 9. Which tantrum findings should be reported to the provider?
> Extreme aggression, more than **5/day**, or lasting more than **15 min**.

> [!question]- 10. What is the maximum time-out for a 2-year-old?
> **2 minutes** (1 min per year of age).

> [!question]- 11. A toddler uses a bottle again after a new baby arrives. Expected or not, and when does it become a concern?
> Expected and temporary. It's a concern if it lasts **> 2–3 weeks** or comes with other manifestations.

> [!question]- 12. A child resists halfway through toilet training. What do you advise?
> Stop; the child isn't ready. Retry in a few weeks to months (**2–3 months**). Never punish accidents.

> [!question]- 13. A toddler drinks 32 oz of cow's milk a day. What is the risk, and what's the limit?
> **Iron deficiency anemia.** Limit milk to **16–24 oz/day** and give **≥ 3** servings of iron-rich foods (**≥ 7 mg/day**).

> [!question]- 14. A family answers "sometimes true" to one Hunger Vital Sign item. What does that mean, and what's next?
> **Positive screen.** Refer to SNAP, WIC, TANF, and food banks.

> [!question]- 15. A parent proposes these sleep habits: warm room, tablet until lights out, 9 hours a night with no nap. Which need correcting?
> All three. The room should be **cool** and dark, screens off **≥ 1 hr** before bed, and total sleep **11–14 hr/24 hr**, naps included.

> [!question]- 16. A 2-year-old's parent wants to turn the car seat forward. What do you teach?
> Keep it **rear-facing** until the manufacturer's height/weight limit, even past age 2. Middle of the back seat, chest clip at armpit level, no bulky coat.

> [!question]- 17. Which child-proofing choice is correct: a gate only at the bottom of the stairs, a walker, doorknob covers on all doors, or hardwood on the stairs?
> **Doorknob covers on all doors.** Gates go top *and* bottom; walkers ↑ fall risk; carpet is safer than hard floors.

> [!question]- 18. A toddler swallowed a cleaning product. What are the steps?
> Remove the product, have the child spit out or remove what's in the mouth, and call **poison control**. **Do not induce vomiting** unless poison control directs it.

> [!question]- 19. A caller says her 2-year-old is pointing at his nose and blowing hard after lunch, and she sees nothing. What do you advise?
> Have the child **assessed by a provider immediately** for possible FBA.

> [!question]- 20. Which ASD screening tool fits a 34-month-old?
> **POSI** (**16–35 mo**). STAT (**24–36 mo**) is for children already flagged with a developmental disorder. M-CHAT-R/F stops at **30 mo**.

> [!question]- 21. Name two toddler-specific reasons for increased lead-poisoning risk.
> Two of: hand-to-mouth behavior, an incomplete blood-brain barrier, or iron deficiency anemia (↑ lead absorption).

> [!question]- 22. When is ibuprofen contraindicated for a toddler with fever and diarrhea?
> When the child may be **dehydrated**: it raises the risk of renal injury. The fever course limit is **3 days**.
