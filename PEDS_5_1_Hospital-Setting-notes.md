## I. Emotional and social responses to hospitalization

> [!quote] Anchor
> Hospitalization disrupts routine and separates children from people and places they know. Stress shows up differently at each age and can slow recovery.

#### How does hospital stress affect children?
- May **delay developmental milestones** and harm mental health.
- Stress → **anxiety**, physical complaints without illness (e.g., unexplained stomach pain), **aggression**; ==stress worsens recovery and health outcomes==.
- Chronic illness with frequent admissions → vivid memories of past bad experiences → more fear each stay; repeated stays strain coping.

#### Response to hospitalization by age?
| Age | Behaviors / understanding | Main stressors | Nurse approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Infant** | Can't verbalize or understand why → distress | Separation (late infancy), unfamiliar caregivers | ==**Consistent caregivers**==, familiar objects, caregiver presence |
| **Toddler** | Notices disrupted routine; cries, irritable, ==clings==, **regresses** | ==Separation anxiety== peaks; fear of pain; too much/too little stimulation | Consistent caregivers and routine; **promote play**; **offer choices** |
| **Preschool** | Magical thinking: ==pain/illness = punishment==; fears procedures cause lasting harm | Pain, bodily harm | Clear explanations; ==reassure it's not their fault==; **let them ask questions**; **pretend play** |
| **School-age** | Understands why; upset, sad, afraid; may regress, act out, withdraw, resist | Separation from friends, school, activities; fear of unknown/pain; ==loss of routine== | **Routine activities and tasks**; age-appropriate info; talk about feelings |
| **Adolescent** | Understands cause/effect; anxiety, **acts out**, becomes introverted | ==Body image==, peers, independence; confusing communication, poor sleep, ==lack of privacy== | ==Involve in plan of care==; offer choices; **respect privacy** |

#### What is regression, and how is it handled?
- Return to behaviors of an earlier stage under stress, e.g., loss of toilet training, bedwetting, bottle or baby talk `[ADDED]`.
- Toilet training: use **hospital training pants** rather than diapers or expecting underwear; ==reassure parents regression is temporary== and resolves at home. Don't punish or shame `[ADDED]`.

#### Separation anxiety: when and what phases?
- Early childhood: separation from parents/caregivers; typically **late infancy to early toddlerhood**. School-age and adolescents: separation from home, friends, school, activities.
- Three phases `[ADDED]`: **protest** (screams, cries for parent, rejects strangers) → **despair** (withdrawn, sad, ↓ interest in play and food) → **detachment** (seems adjusted, ignores parent on return; long separation). ==Detachment is not adjustment==; maximize parental presence `[ADDED]`.

#### Loss of control: what causes it?
- Limited understanding of procedures and no say in decisions → helplessness. Counter by improving communication and ==involving the child in care as much as possible==.

## II. Support systems

#### How does a child's hospitalization affect the family?
- Disrupted routine, **anxiety, depression, lack of sleep**; physical, emotional, and financial burdens.
- ==The family's response affects the child's coping.== Support the whole family.

#### Bowen family systems theory?
- The family is ==one interconnected emotional unit==: one member's stress affects all. Eight concepts `[slide 12 graphic text]`; definitions `[ADDED]`:

| Concept | Meaning `[ADDED]` |
|---|---|
| **Triangles** | Smallest stable relationship unit; tension between two pulls in a third (e.g., parents' conflict pulls in a child) |
| **Differentiation of self** | Ability to keep one's own thinking amid family emotion; low = more reactive under stress |
| **Nuclear family emotional process** | Patterns of handling tension: conflict, distance, dysfunction in one spouse, impairment of a child |
| **Family projection process** | Parents pass anxieties onto a child |
| **Multigenerational transmission** | Patterns passed across generations |
| **Emotional cutoff** | Managing conflict by distancing from family |
| **Sibling position** | Birth order shapes roles and behavior |
| **Societal emotional process** | Society's stress mirrors family dynamics |

- Nursing implication `[ADDED]`: assess the whole family's stress and coping, not just the child's.

#### Siblings: feelings and nursing actions?
- **Resentment, anxiety, jealousy, sadness, loneliness, guilt**; the sick child gets attention and gifts.
- Actions: explain the sick child's condition ==in age-appropriate language== (nurse can do this with visiting siblings); encourage family communication and sibling questions with honest answers; **phone/video calls** home; ==keep a steady routine at home==.

## III. Meeting emotional and social needs

### Communication

#### AIDET?
> [!example] Mnemonic — AIDET
> **A**cknowledge: welcome client and family
> **I**ntroduce: your name and role
> **D**uration: time expectations and changes for interventions, procedures, treatments
> **E**xplanation: the plan of care, interventions, procedures, treatments
> **T**hank you: for their time and cooperation; invite feedback

#### What makes communication therapeutic with kids?
- Respectful, honest, clear, ==matched to developmental stage==. Child-friendly or quiet spaces.
- Nonverbal: approachable, eye contact, smile, **being physically present**. ==Silence== gives time to process.

### Reducing stress and loss of control

#### Interventions to minimize stress?
- ==**Family-centered care**==: family included in the plan and in decisions.
- Clear explanation of information and **changes in the plan of care**; active listening; time for questions.
- **Consistent nurses** (especially frequent admissions).
- ==Offer choices when possible==; let the child **touch and hold (medically safe) equipment**.

#### Parents who can't stay?
- Tell the child **and staff** where they'll be, when they'll return, how to reach them; recorded messages for the child.
- Nurse keeps parents updated by phone and ==encourages them to call==.

#### What to teach parents?
- Family-centered teaching; how to support the child and siblings. Infants/toddlers: sing familiar songs.
- Bring ==favorite items from home== (pillow, blanket, stuffed animal).
- ==Expect behavior changes==: normal response to hospital stress.

### Play and support

#### Why play, and what fits each age?
- Play helps children ==cope and ↓ anxiety==, builds **resilience**, supports growth and development.

| Age | Play options |
|---|---|
| **Infant** | Lullabies/music, rattles, stuffed animals, visually stimulating space |
| **Toddler** | Music, stacking blocks, painting/coloring, balls, **reading** (language), Play-Doh, hammering toys (channel energy) |
| **Preschool** | Toddler activities + ==**pretend play**==, dress-up, arts and crafts |
| **School-age** | ==Peer interaction==, crafts, board/card/video games, reading, **journaling**, library services |
| **Adolescent** | ==Peers via digital devices==, games, journaling, scrapbooking, personalize space with music/movies |

- Playroom on the unit; if the child can't leave, bring toys to the room.

#### Who supports the child and family?
- **Child life specialists**: psychosocial support mainly through ==therapeutic play==, helping the child adjust to the hospital.
- **Art, music, animal-assisted therapy**: self-expression, ↓ stress.
- Family, caregivers, friends: guide parents to outside help for **transportation, meals, sibling care**.

### Discharge and PICU

#### When does discharge teaching start?
- ==As soon as possible during the stay==, not discharge day; **confirm** on the day of discharge.
- Include parents and the child when appropriate; consider **health literacy**.
- Confirm understanding with ==**teach-back**== (they explain it) and **show-me / skill demonstration** (they do it).

> [!tip] NCLEX tip
> Best evidence that teaching worked: the parent ==states it back or demonstrates the skill==, not "Do you have any questions?" or nodding.

#### PICU: what support does the family need?
- Critical illness, close monitoring; stays of days to months. Very stressful for the whole family.
- Support: **child life**, **social work** (financial, emotional), **temporary housing** (e.g., Ronald McDonald House), **school support**; PT and OT.
- Prepare family for IV lines, invasive equipment, constant monitoring, possible ventilator. Parents encouraged to stay; space may be limited.

## IV. Admission assessment

> [!info] Why this structure
> The admission slides follow **Gordon's functional health patterns** `[ADDED]`: a head-to-toe of how the child lives, not just body systems.

#### Health perception / management?
- On admission: ==complete physical assessment, health history, current medications==.
- Ability to manage care at home; **potential barriers to recovery**; confidentiality; discharge teaching.

#### Nutrition / metabolism?
- **Accurate intake**, diet restrictions, age-specific needs. Calories:

| Age | Need |
|---|---|
| Infant | **100 cal/kg/day** |
| 1–3 yr | **80 kcal/kg/day** |
| 4–5 yr | **70 kcal/kg/day** |
| 6–8 yr | **65 kcal/kg/day** |
| 9+ yr | **35–45 kcal/kg/day** |

#### Elimination: how is output measured?
- **Toilet hat** (collection device under the toilet seat `[ADDED]`), urinal, ==**weigh diapers**==: wet weight − dry weight; **1 g = 1 mL** `[ADDED]`.
- Adequate pediatric UO ≈ **1 mL/kg/hr** `[ADDED — verify vs course materials]`.
- Toilet-training regression: training pants; reassure (see I).

#### Sleep / rest?
- **Dim lighting**, consistent schedule when possible. Hours/day: newborn **14–17**, infant **12–16**, toddler **11–14**, preschool **10–13** (incl. naps), school-age **9–12**, adolescent **8–10**.

#### Activity / exercise?
- **3–5 yr**: active play throughout the day.
- **6–17 yr**: ==**≥ 60 min/day**== moderate-to-vigorous, can be split; mostly heart-rate-raising (walking, running); muscle-strengthening (climbing, push-ups) and bone-strengthening (jumping) ==**≥ 3 days/wk**==.

#### Cognitive/perceptual, self-concept, role/relationship?
- **Language needs**: ==professional medical interpreter== (not family) `[ADDED for "not family"]`; translated discharge instructions.
- **In-hospital school programs**: schedule treatments around school sessions.
- **Self-esteem**: comfortable space, express feelings, involve child in care and goal-setting, let them choose task order (initiative, independence).
- **Trust**: fidelity, honesty, competence, ==follow through== on promises, open communication, reassurance.

#### Sexuality and coping/values?
- **Adolescents** may be going through puberty in hospital: listen, respect, ==privacy and confidentiality==, treat as competent.
- Coping/values: consider **culture, background, faith**; build trust.

## V. Nursing interventions and safety

#### What happens at admission?
- **Orient** child and family: room, unit, family sleeping arrangements, lounge, restrooms.
- ==Invasive or painful procedures in the treatment/procedure room==, keeping the bed (and playroom) a safe space `[ADDED for rationale]`.

#### IV access: what to watch?
- Some hospitals have IV teams; infants may have **scalp** IVs; central lines for long-term (including home) IV therapy.
- ==Check IV sites regularly== for infiltration and phlebitis:

| | Infiltration `[ADDED]` | Phlebitis `[ADDED]` |
|---|---|---|
| What | IV fluid leaking into tissue | Inflamed vein |
| Signs | Swelling, **cool**, pale, taut skin; slowed infusion; pain | **Warm**, red, tender along vein; may feel a cord |
| Action | Stop infusion, remove IV, elevate, restart elsewhere | Stop, remove IV, warm compress, restart elsewhere |

#### Safety measures?
- Fall/injury prevention: declutter, harmful items out of reach, **non-slip socks**.
- ==Assess **latex allergy** on admission==. Higher risk: spina bifida, many surgeries; cross-reacting foods (banana, avocado, kiwi, chestnut) `[ADDED]`.
- ==Age-appropriate bed==: **crib with protective cover/top** for young children; regular hospital bed for older children.
- Protect patient information; standardized handoff: **SBAR** (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation) or **I-PASS** (Illness severity, Patient summary, Action list, Situation awareness/contingency, Synthesis by receiver) `[ADDED for expansions]`.

### Adherence to care standards

#### Hygiene, diet, fever?
- Daily rounds; ==**family-centered rounds**== preferred. Follow hospital policy to prevent errors.
- Hygiene: hand hygiene, baths, teeth, clean hospital pajamas.
- Know clear vs. full liquids:

| Clear liquid `[ADDED]` | Full liquid `[ADDED]` |
|---|---|
| Transparent at room temp: water, broth, apple juice, gelatin, popsicles, clear soda | Clear + opaque: milk, pudding, ice cream, strained cream soups, smooth yogurt |

- Fever = **100.4°F**: infection, teething, overdressing, after vaccines. Watch for ==rash or dehydration== → seek medical attention. [NOTE: current practice: teething may slightly raise temperature but doesn't cause true fever ≥ 100.4°F; look for another cause.]
- Infection control: hand hygiene; contact, droplet, airborne precautions (see PEDS 5.2).

#### Restraint rules?
- Types: soft wrist, side rails, padded arm wraps / elbow immobilizers (e.g., protect an IV).
- ==Provider order required==; ==discontinue ASAP==; monitor frequently per protocol: **ROM, turning, skin care, hydration, toileting**.
- Circulation/skin checks and release for ROM at least **q2h** `[ADDED — verify vs course materials]`; tie to bed frame, not side rail, with a quick-release knot; two fingers fit under `[ADDED]`.

> [!warning] Priority
> Restrained limb that's cool, pale, numb, or with weak pulses → loosen the restraint and reassess circulation first `[ADDED]`.

### Consent

#### Who signs, and when isn't consent needed?
| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Minor (< 18)** | Parent or legal guardian; ==confirm who the custodial parent/legal guardian is first== |
| **Emancipated minor** | Signs own consent. Examples: legally separated from parents, **military**, **married** |
| **Implied consent** | ==Emergency==: life-saving care when consent can't be obtained or would delay it; provider's judgment |

#### What must informed consent include?
- Legal process: ==the **provider** discloses== (nurse witnesses `[ADDED]`): diagnosis; procedure name; what it is and why; **risks and benefits**; alternatives and their risks/benefits; risks/benefits of **not** doing it.

## VI. Procedures, medications, and tube/stoma care

#### Diagnostic procedures?
- **Blood**: vein, heel/finger stick, peripheral IV, central line. Infant heel stick on the ==lateral (outer) heel== `[ADDED]`.
- **Urine**: sterile cup, intermittent catheterization. **Swab**: nasopharynx or pharynx.
- **Surgery** ↑ anxiety → tour of surgical area and unit; ==explain what to expect==.

#### Medication safety in peds?
- ==Dosing by weight (mg/kg) is typical==; know safe dosage ranges. Check: convert lb → kg (**÷ 2.2**), calculate the safe range, compare to the order; ==outside the range → hold and clarify with the provider== `[ADDED]`.
- Routes: PO, SL, PR, IV, IM, subcut, ophthalmic, otic, inhalation, topical/transdermal. Infant IM site = **vastus lateralis** `[ADDED]`. Ear drops: pull pinna **down and back < 3 yr**, **up and back > 3 yr** `[ADDED]`.
- **Medication reconciliation** at ==admission, transfer, and discharge==: all meds, supplements, vitamins; teach family any changes.

#### Liquid meds by age?
| Age | How |
|---|---|
| **< 4 mo** | Graduated dropper/syringe, ==slowly, into the side of the mouth==; pacifier or bottle (breast milk/formula) between and after |
| **4 mo–1 yr** | Syringe, dropper, or cup; then **15 mL** diluted juice or baby food |
| **12–36 mo** | Syringe, dropper, or cup; food/drink between and after (juice, yogurt, frozen fruit pops) |
| **> 3 yr** | Syringe, dropper, or cup; then water, juice, yogurt, or other food |

- Infants: swaddle and position upright. Don't mix meds into a full bottle of formula (incomplete dose, may refuse feeds) `[ADDED]`.

#### Gavage feeding?
- **G-tube** (gastrostomy: through abdominal wall) or **NG tube**.
- NG `[ADDED]`: measure **nose → earlobe → xiphoid**; confirm placement before use (X-ray initially; aspirate pH per policy); HOB up during and after feeding; flush per order.

#### Stoma care?
- Stoma/ostomy = surgical opening; **colostomy** (colon) or **ileostomy** (ileum); temporary or permanent.
- Assess stoma and surrounding skin; treat irritation/breakdown; empty stool from the appliance; change the appliance. Work with the **wound-ostomy team**.
- Healthy stoma = ==moist, pink-red==; pale, dusky, or black → report `[ADDED]`.

## VII. Clinical judgment

#### Six steps applied to a hospitalized child?
| Step | In this lecture |
|---|---|
| 1 **Recognize cues** | Assess child and family needs; which need follow-up |
| 2 **Analyze cues** | Connect needs to the situation and plan |
| 3 **Prioritize hypotheses** | ==Most important needs first== |
| 4 **Generate solutions** | Plan interventions: physiological, psychosocial, growth and development |
| 5 **Take actions** | Implement the plan |
| 6 **Evaluate outcomes** | Effective? Not met → ==replan and reprioritize== |

## Self-quiz

> [!question]- 1. A 4-yr-old says the IV is because she was bad. What's going on, and the response?
> Preschool magical thinking (illness as punishment). Explain simply and reassure her it's not her fault.

> [!question]- 2. A toddler screams and clings when the parent leaves, then a week later ignores the parent at visits. Interpretation?
> Separation anxiety progressing from protest to detachment. Detachment isn't adjustment; maximize parent presence and consistent caregivers.

> [!question]- 3. Top hospitalization stressors for an adolescent?
> Body image, separation from peers, lack of privacy, confusing communication, poor sleep.

> [!question]- 4. Best nursing approach for a hospitalized school-age child?
> Keep routine activities and tasks, give age-appropriate information, encourage talking about feelings, peer contact.

> [!question]- 5. Parent of a 3-yr-old: "He was potty trained, now he's wetting the bed." Response?
> Expected regression from hospital stress; use training pants, don't punish; it usually resolves at home.

> [!question]- 6. Which actions help the sibling of a hospitalized child?
> Age-appropriate explanation, encouraging questions, phone/video calls, keeping the home routine steady.

> [!question]- 7. What does the "D" in AIDET mean?
> Duration: updating client and family on how long things will take and changes to timing.

> [!question]- 8. Where should a venipuncture be done for a 5-yr-old in a room?
> In the treatment/procedure room, not the child's bed.

> [!question]- 9. When does discharge teaching start, and how is it verified?
> As early as possible during the stay; confirmed on discharge day with teach-back and skill demonstration.

> [!question]- 10. How much sleep does a toddler need? An adolescent?
> Toddler 11–14 hr (including naps); adolescent 8–10 hr.

> [!question]- 11. Calorie need for a 10-kg infant? A 2-yr-old weighing 12 kg?
> 100 cal/kg/day × 10 = 1,000 cal/day; 80 kcal/kg/day × 12 = 960 kcal/day.

> [!question]- 12. Activity recommendation for a 10-yr-old?
> At least 60 min/day moderate–vigorous (can be split); muscle- and bone-strengthening at least 3 days/wk.

> [!question]- 13. Dry diaper 20 g, wet diaper 65 g. Output?
> 45 mL (1 g = 1 mL).

> [!question]- 14. Admission safety priorities for a toddler?
> Latex allergy, crib with protective top, declutter/remove hazards, non-slip socks.

> [!question]- 15. IV site is swollen, cool, and pale. Action?
> Infiltration: stop the infusion, remove the IV, elevate, restart at another site.

> [!question]- 16. A 17-yr-old married client needs surgery. Who signs consent?
> She does: married minors are emancipated.

> [!question]- 17. Child in hemorrhagic shock, parents unreachable. Consent?
> Implied consent: life-saving treatment proceeds on provider judgment.

> [!question]- 18. Requirements for wrist restraints on a child pulling at an IV?
> Provider order; remove ASAP; frequent monitoring with ROM, turning, skin care, hydration, toileting.

> [!question]- 19. How is liquid medication given to a 2-month-old?
> Slowly, by graduated dropper/syringe into the side of the mouth; pacifier or bottle between and after.

> [!question]- 20. When is medication reconciliation done?
> Admission, transfer, and discharge.

> [!question]- 21. Clear or full liquid: pudding, apple juice, broth, milk?
> Clear: apple juice, broth. Full: pudding, milk.
